# One adventure: how the physics chapters are told

*The Moving World* reads as one long story: two and a half thousand years of people asking why things move, what light is,
and what holds the sky up. The chapters keep their logical order (section numbers are reserved for a future lab app), but
each chapter is an episode in the same journey, and Kym walks through it beside the people who were confused, then wrong,
then right.

## The shape of every section

1. **A scene or a puzzle she can feel.** `:::scene Delft, 1586` and a real person facing a real problem, or a moment from
   her own day: the local train braking, the matka on the kitchen counter, the causeway to Haji Ali going under the sea.
2. **Her likely wrong idea, said out loud and taken seriously.** Most wrong ideas in physics were once the best ideas
   anyone had. Aristotle believed motion needs a mover; so does almost every twelve-year-old; that is good company.
3. **Predict first** (`:::predict`). She commits before she is told. Aim each prediction where about half of
   twelve-year-olds would get it wrong (Crouch & Mazur: discussion helps most when 35–70% answer right first).
4. **The decisive evidence.** The experiment that settled it, historically, and a version she can do (`:::home`) or watch
   (`:::live`). A demonstration alone rarely changes a mind; it works when it confronts a prediction she has just made.
5. **The better model, explained the simplest true way** (BOOKPLAN §2). Phenomenon first, then the name. Mechanism, never a
   label. Several angles: a picture, a number, a story, her hands.
6. **`:::careful`** with her wrong idea in her own voice, and why it fails.
7. **`:::check`**, one per section, answer at the back.
8. **`:::school`** only where IB/IGCSE words it differently (one kind sentence; use research/school-wording.md).

Not every section needs every box. Story and explanation come first; boxes earn their place.

## Threads that run through the book
- **Bridges.** "Eighty years after Galileo's ramps…", "Remember the luggage scales in Chapter 3?". Each chapter opening
  glances back at where the story has reached.
- **Wrong ideas come back** (`:::recall N`). Corrections fade within months (Winer 2002). From Chapter 2 on, each chapter
  revisits one or two misconceptions from earlier chapters in a new setting: impetus returns in orbits, "used up" returns
  in circuits, "something leaves the eye" returns in colour.
- **Mumbai is the laboratory.** The local train (frames, inertia, momentum), the Sea Link (projectiles, cables), the monsoon
  (raindrops, sea breeze, humidity), the matka, Marine Drive at sunset, Haji Ali's tides, Colaba's old observatory, TIFR on
  Peddar Road, the Nehru Planetarium. One or two per chapter, where they fit naturally.
- **India in the story, truthfully.** Aryabhata, J. C. Bose, Raman and Krishnan, Saha, S. N. Bose, Chandrasekhar, Bhabha,
  Bibha Chowdhuri, Anna Mani, Sarabhai, Swarup, Kalpana Chawla, ISRO. Never overclaim (Brahmagupta's "attraction" was an idea,
  not a law of gravity; say so).
- **The chemistry book is next door.** Link to https://saxster.github.io/kym-chem/ where atoms, energy or electrons meet
  (particles and heat in Ch 8, the nucleus in Ch 15, batteries in Ch 12).

## Home experiments (`:::home`)
Kitchen-safe and Mumbai-real: water, salt, sugar, ice, balloons, coins, a steel thali, a matka, a torch, a phone (camera
slow motion, the free phyphox app), luggage scales, AA cells, LEDs, a fridge magnet. No flames, no mains electricity, nothing
hotter than warm tap water, nothing tasted that isn't ordinary food, "ask an adult" for anything sharp, electrical or outside.
Every one is checked at the safety and will-it-work gate. Lessons already learned: Indian coins are steel, not copper;
many homes have no hot tap; humidity kills static demos in the monsoon; a matka barely works in July.

## Voice
Warm, plain, vivid, precise. Speak to her as "you". Short sentences, varied rhythm. No exclamation-mark gush (at most one
genuine surprise per chapter), no "let's dive in", no rhetorical filler. Never "the ball wants", "electricity wants",
"nature hates". Forces, energy, fields, motion, counting.

## History rules
True history only. Every scene, date and quote traces to research/history.md or a gate-checked source. LEGEND items are told
as legends with what really happened beside them (Pisa → Stevin at Delft; Archimedes' bath → Galileo's *Bilancetta*;
Pythagoras' hammers → Vincenzo Galilei's strings). HEDGE items get "the story goes". Never invent a quote.
