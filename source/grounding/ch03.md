## 3.1 Adding forces

**Concept sequence**
1. A force is a push or a pull, with size and direction. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-1-development-of-force-concept
2. Several forces on one object combine into the net force: forces in the same direction add, opposite ones cancel. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force
3. That forces add as vectors is an *experimental* result, not a definition. Apply two forces separately, then together: the accelerations add as vectors, so the forces do. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
4. A free-body diagram shows the object as a dot, with only the *external* forces on it as arrows whose lengths are proportional to their size. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force
5. Tug of war: a rope pulled at both ends. Inside the rope, each bit pulls its neighbour; the size of that pull is the tension. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/7-1-pushing-pulling-and-tension/
6. A standard force can be defined by a stretched spring (restoring force), which is how a spring balance works. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-1-development-of-force-concept

**Definitions**
- *Net force:* the total of all forces acting on an object. *External force:* one acting on the system from outside. *Internal force:* one between two parts of the system. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force
- *Free-body diagram:* represents the object as a point, with only the external forces drawn as arrows. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force
- *IGCSE 0625:* "resultant force". The bridge (source/research/school-wording.md): "Resultant force, net force and unbalanced force all mean the same thing: what's left over after the pushes cancel out."

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- F_T = F₁ + F₂, a vector sum. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
- SI unit of force: the newton, N. 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Post pulled by two ropes* (Example 7.1):
  - F₁ = (70, 20) N, F₂ = (−30, 40) N, gravity (0, −10) N. The ground's force is (−40, −50) N, magnitude 64 N, at 51.3° to the horizontal.
  - `python3 -I -c 'import math;print(math.hypot(40,50), math.degrees(math.atan(50/40)))'` → 64.03 51.34.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
  - Too advanced in 2D for Kym; use the 1D version below.
- *1D tug of war* (**ASSUMED** forces): 4 children pull left with 120, 100, 90, 80 N; 3 pull right with 150, 130, 110 N.
  - `python3 -I -c 'print(150+130+110-(120+100+90+80))'` → 0 N net, so the rope doesn't speed up.
  - The idea matches PhET's tug-of-war tab (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-2-newtons-first-law-of-motion-inertia).

**Demonstrations**
- *PhET "Forces and Motion: Basics", Net Force tab* (tug of war): https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-2-newtons-first-law-of-motion-inertia ; sim https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/forces-and-motion-basics. Link out, with attribution beside it (source/research/sources.md §4).

**Concept questions**
- Do two bodies have to touch to exert a force on each other? (No; gravity acts at a distance.) https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force (CYU 2)
- Is force a scalar or a vector? https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force (CYU 3)
- Which forces go on a free-body diagram, internal or external? https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force (CYU 4)

**Warnings the sources give**
- Draw only the forces acting *on* the body, not the forces the body exerts. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force
- One choice of positive direction must be used for every vector in a problem. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **OS Physics 4.1 opens "Force is the cause of motion"** (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-1-force). That is false and is exactly Chapter 2's misconception. Write "a net force changes motion".
- **"Balanced forces mean it's not moving" is false.** Balanced forces mean constant velocity, which may be non-zero (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-2-newtons-first-law-of-motion-inertia).
- **"The team that pulls harder on the rope wins"** needs care. Each team pulls the rope with the same tension at its end, if the rope's mass is negligible (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/7-1-pushing-pulling-and-tension/). The winner is the team whose feet get the bigger friction force from the ground. That is our inference from the friction notes (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf). Gate it before printing.

## 3.2 Why heavy things are hard to start

**Concept sequence**
1. If a net force acts, the body accelerates in proportion to it; the constant of proportionality is the mass, F = ma. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-1-newtons-first-and-second-laws/
2. a ∝ F_net (double the force, double the acceleration) and a ∝ 1/m (double the mass, half the acceleration). https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
3. The same push on a basketball and on a stalled car gives identical free-body diagrams but very different accelerations. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion (Fig 4.5)
4. Mass can be *measured* this way: apply the same force to a standard body and to an unknown one; the mass ratio is the inverse ratio of the accelerations. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
5. The more general form is F = dp/dt; F = ma is the constant-mass special case. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-1-newtons-first-and-second-laws/
6. F = ma is more than a definition because separate *force laws* (gravity, springs, friction) tell us F independently. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
7. Weight: if gravity is the only force, F_net = W and a = g, so W = mg. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion

**Definitions**
- *Newton:* the force that accelerates a 1 kg system at 1 m/s². https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- *Weight:* the gravitational force on a mass, W = mg. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- *Inertial mass:* the mass found by comparing accelerations under the same force. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
- *IGCSE 0625:* "weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass"; g = W/m in N/kg, "equivalent to the acceleration of free fall". The bridge: in a physics answer, write mass in kg and weight in N (source/research/school-wording.md).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- F_net = ma; a = F_net/m. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- g = 9.80 m/s² on Earth (textbook value); 1.67 m/s² on the Moon. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- Weight of 1.0 kg: 9.8 N on Earth, ~1.7 N on the Moon. `python3 -I -c 'print(1.0*9.8, 1.0*1.67)'` → 9.8 1.67. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Lawn mower:* 51 N net on 240 kg → 0.21 m/s². `python3 -I -c 'print(51/240)'` → 0.2125. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- *Rocket sled:* four rockets, 2100 kg, a = 49 m/s², friction 650 N → T = 2.6 × 10⁴ N each. `python3 -I -c 'print((2100*49+650)/4)'` → 25887.5. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- *Professor pushing a cart:* the floor's 150 N forward minus 24.0 N of resistance, on 84 kg → 1.5 m/s². `python3 -I -c 'print((150-24)/84)'` → 1.5. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion-symmetry-in-forces
- *Scooter vs truck* (BOOKPLAN; masses **ASSUMED**: scooter + rider 200 kg, loaded truck 10,000 kg; same 2000 N net force):
  - `python3 -I -c 'print(2000/200, 2000/10000)'` → 10.0 vs 0.2 m/s², a 50× difference for the same force.
  - Real masses and forces need a source before gating.
- *Cricket ball:* 0.16 kg (**ASSUMED**, BOOKPLAN), weight 1.57 N. `python3 -I -c 'print(0.16*9.8)'` → 1.568.

**Demonstrations**
- *OS Physics "Mass and Weight" activity:*
  - A bathroom scale *detects weight* (its springs compress), then divides by 9.80 to *display* mass.
  - While standing on the scale, push down on a table: the reading changes.
  - https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
  - **Mumbai-flat feasible:** yes, with a bathroom scale and a sturdy table.
- *Same push, two loads:* push an empty trolley and a loaded one with the same luggage-scale reading. Source principle: https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion. Feasible with a trolley bag; it is qualitative, because friction differs.

**Concept questions**
- True or false: to halve an object's acceleration you must halve the net force. (True, for a fixed mass.) https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- Which is correct: net force causes motion, or net force causes change in motion? https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-conceptual-questions (section 4.3)
- If an object's acceleration is zero, are no external forces acting on it? https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-conceptual-questions (section 4.3)
- Describe a case where the net external force is not zero but the speed stays constant. (Turning.) https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-conceptual-questions (section 4.3)
- Standing on a bathroom scale, you push down on a table. Does the reading go up or down? https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion

**Warnings the sources give**
- Only the *net* external force sets the acceleration. Using one force alone gives the wrong answer. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- Students may confuse *equal* with *proportional*, and *weight* (a force) with *g* (an acceleration). https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion (Teacher Support)
- Objects falling on Earth are never truly in free fall: there is always some air resistance and air buoyancy. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion
- F = ma fails for systems whose mass changes, such as rockets; use F = dp/dt. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **"A constant push gives a constant speed" is false.** A constant *net* force gives constant *acceleration* (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion). Held by 66% of n=478 and 44% of US middle/high schoolers (source/research/misconceptions.md).
- **OS Physics says "When people talk about gravity, they don't always realize that it is an acceleration"** (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion). Gravity is a *force*; g is the acceleration it produces in free fall. Do not copy the sentence.
- **"Heavy things are hard to start because they're heavy"** mixes up weight and mass. In space, a truck is weightless but just as hard to start: same mass, same inertia (https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-2-newtons-first-law-of-motion-inertia, mass the same on Earth, in orbit or on the Moon).
- **"F = ma means force is in the object"** is false. F is the net *external* force from other objects (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-1-newtons-first-and-second-laws/).

## 3.3 Can a table push?

**Concept sequence**
1. Rest your hand on a table. Press harder and the atoms are pressed closer together; the electrons in the atoms repel, and the table pushes your hand back. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf
2. Even rigid things deform under load. A card table visibly sags under a bag of dog food, and its elastic restoring force grows with the sag until it matches the load. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces
3. It acts like a deformed spring, trampoline or diving board: the more deformation, the more push. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces
4. Whatever supports a load, living or not, must supply an upward force equal to the weight. If that force is perpendicular to the contact surface, it is the normal force. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces
5. The normal force adjusts: it equals mg only in special cases. It can be less (on a slope) or more (when you push off to jump). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces
6. What we *feel* as the "weight" of a block in the hand is really the normal force from the hand. With your other hand helping, it feels lighter; in a jump, there is no normal force and you feel "weightless", even though gravity is still on. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf

**Definitions**
- *Normal force:* the perpendicular part of the contact force; "normal" means perpendicular. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces
- OS Physics puts it as the outward force a surface applies perpendicular to itself, which stops the object passing through. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
- *Constraint force:* a force not given by a force law in advance; it takes whatever value the motion requires. Normal force and static friction are examples. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf
- *IGCSE 0625:* our research file has no specific normal-force sentence. Use "contact force" / "normal contact force"; the exact wording is UNVERIFIED (source/research/school-wording.md does not list it).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- N = mg for an object at rest on a horizontal surface with no other vertical forces. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf
- On a 25° slope: N = mg cos 25°.
  - The source's skier gives 532 N. Recomputed: `python3 -I -c 'import math;print(60*9.8*math.cos(math.radians(25)))'` → 532.9 N, so 533 N to 3 s.f.; the source truncated.
  - Either way it is less than mg = 588 N.
  - https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-5-normal-tension-and-other-examples-of-forces

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Block in the hand at rest:* N − mg = 0, so N = mg. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf
  - Recast: a 0.16 kg cricket ball (**ASSUMED**) resting on her palm: N = 1.57 N.

## 3.4 Every push has a partner

**Concept sequence**
1. Forces always come in equal and opposite pairs: the force of 1 on 2 equals minus the force of 2 on 1. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/
2. Newton's own example, paraphrased: press a stone with your finger and the stone presses your finger. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
3. Extreme test: a marble hits a train. Which feels the bigger force? Intuition says the marble, because it is smashed. Wrong: the forces are equal. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/
4. Equal forces do not mean equal effects. The accelerations differ by the mass ratio: |a_marble|/|a_train| = m_train/m_marble. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/
5. The two forces of a pair act on *different* objects, so they never cancel each other. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/ ; https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
6. Person standing still:
   - gravity (from Earth) and the normal force (from the ground) both act on the person and balance, but they are not a pair.
   - Gravity's partner is the person pulling Earth up; the normal force's partner is the person pushing down on the ground.
   - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/
7. Getting around:
   - a swimmer pushes the wall back and the wall pushes her forward;
   - walking: you push the floor back and the floor pushes you forward;
   - cars: wheels push the road back;
   - helicopters push air down; an octopus pushes water back.
   - https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
8. A rope is a chain of third-law pairs; the tension is the size of each internal pair. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/7-1-pushing-pulling-and-tension/

**Definitions**
- *Newton's third law:* "Newton's third law of motion states that whenever a first object exerts a force on a second object, the first object experiences a force equal in magnitude but opposite in direction to the force that it exerts." https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
- *Interaction pair:* the two forces between two interacting bodies. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
- *Tension:* a pull along a flexible connector (string, rope, chain, cable), which can only pull along its length. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
- *IGCSE 0625:* our research file's extract does not quote a third-law sentence. Check the 1.5 wording in the syllabus PDF at the gate (source/research/school-wording.md).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- F₁,₂ = −F₂,₁. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
- A 5.00 kg mass hanging at rest: T = mg = 49.0 N. `python3 -I -c 'print(5*9.8)'` → 49.0. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion

## 3.5 Momentum and rockets

**Concept sequence**
1. Momentum p = mv: a vector, in the direction of the velocity, with SI unit kg·m/s. It depends on the reference frame. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-5-momentum-and-impulse/15-1-momentum-and-impulse/ ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter10.pdf
2. Newton called it the "quantity of motion" and stated his second law in terms of it. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter7.pdf
3. F = dp/dt is the most general form of the second law; it works when mass flows. Impulse = force × time = change in momentum. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-5-momentum-and-impulse/15-1-momentum-and-impulse/
4. If the net external impulse on a system is zero, its total momentum is constant. Choose the system so that the collision forces are internal. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-5-momentum-and-impulse/16-1-conservation-of-momentum/
5. Momentum is conserved if you take a big enough system. A player who bounces off a goalpost makes Earth recoil, by an amount too small to notice. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-2-conservation-of-momentum
6. The third law guarantees it: the force on the surroundings is minus the force on the system, so their momentum changes cancel. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter10.pdf
7. Rocket: it pushes exhaust backwards; by the third law the exhaust pushes the rocket forwards (thrust). Its mass decreases as fuel leaves. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter12.pdf ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-6-continuous-mass-transfer/19-1-rocket-problem-1-set-up-the-problem/
8. The same principle covers rockets, jet engines, deflating balloons, squid and octopuses, and a gun's recoil. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/8-7-introduction-to-rocket-propulsion

**Definitions**
- *Linear momentum:* "Linear momentum is the product of a system's mass and its velocity." https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse
- *Impulse:* average net external force × the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse
- *Isolated system:* one with zero net external force; its total momentum is conserved. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-2-conservation-of-momentum ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter10.pdf
- *Thrust:* the reaction force of the expelled gas on the rocket. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion
- *IGCSE 0625:* topic 1.6 "Momentum" exists. Its exact wording is not in our research file; check the syllabus PDF (source/research/school-wording.md lists the topic heading only).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- p = mv; FΔt = Δp. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse
- Isolated system: p_total before = p_total after. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter10.pdf
- Rocket acceleration (approximate): a = (v_e/m)(Δm/Δt) − g. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/8-7-introduction-to-rocket-propulsion

**Worked numbers we can reuse**

- **"Momentum is always conserved" is false without the condition.** It holds for an isolated system, or for system plus surroundings (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter10.pdf). A single ball's momentum changes when it's hit.

## Case: The mosquito and the windscreen

**Concept sequence**
1. This is 8.01SC's marble-and-train question with the ratio made even more extreme. At the instant of impact the forces are equal and opposite; the accelerations differ by the mass ratio. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/
2. The mosquito is "smashed" because a huge acceleration acts on a fragile body, not because a bigger force acts on it. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-2-newtons-laws/4-2-newtons-third-law/

**Worked numbers we can reuse** (all inputs **ASSUMED**: mosquito 2.5 mg, closing speed 25 m/s (90 km/h), stopped in 1 ms, car 1200 kg)
- Average force F = mΔv/Δt (impulse, https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/8-1-linear-momentum-force-and-impulse):
  - `python3 -I -c 'm=2.5e-6;F=m*25/1e-3;print(F, F/1200, 25/1e-3, 1200/m)'` → 0.0625 N on each.
  - Car: 5.2 × 10⁻⁵ m/s². Mosquito: 2.5 × 10⁴ m/s², about 2550 g. Ratio 4.8 × 10⁸.
- The mosquito mass needs a source before printing (OS Physics Table 1.3 gives a mosquito as "10⁻⁵ kg" to order of magnitude: https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/1-3-the-language-of-physics-physical-quantities-and-units).
  - With 10⁻⁵ kg instead: `python3 -I -c 'print(1e-5*25/1e-3)'` → 0.25 N. The conclusion is unchanged.

**Fidelity traps**
- **"The windscreen feels nothing" is false.** It feels exactly the same force; the car's acceleration is just immeasurably small.
- **"The car hits harder because it's moving faster"** is false, and frame-dependent: in the car's frame it is the mosquito that is moving.

**Gaps**
- Mosquito mass, impact time and car mass are in no source (ASSUMED); only the order-of-magnitude mosquito mass is sourced.
