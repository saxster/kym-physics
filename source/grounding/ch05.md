## 5.1 The apple and the Moon

**Concept sequence**
1. Newton's first law says straight-line motion is the natural state. Planets and the Moon move on curves, so some force must bend their paths; Newton proposed it was gravity. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
2. Newton's insight: Earth's gravity might reach as far as the Moon and curve its path away from a straight line. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
3. If gravity weakens as 1/distance², then at the Moon, 60 Earth radii away, the pull per kilogram should be 1/3600 of an apple's. That predicts about 0.00272 m/s², which matches the Moon's actual acceleration. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
4. The Moon does not fall *to* Earth with this acceleration; it falls *around* Earth. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
5. Newton made the same comparison himself and found the two accelerations agreed "pretty nearly"; OS College's version differs by under 1%, the leftover due to the slightly elliptical orbit and Earth also moving. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
6. The 8.01SC notes run it the other way: from the law of gravitation and the Moon's distance, predict the orbital period; it comes out 27.2 days, the sidereal month. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter9.pdf
7. Earth and Moon pull on each other equally (third law); both circle their common centre of mass, about 1700 km below Earth's surface. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation

**Definitions**
- *Newton's universal law of gravitation:* every particle attracts every other along the line joining them, with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
- *Sidereal month:* time for the Moon to return to the same position against the stars; *synodic month:* time between full moons (29.53 days on average). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter9.pdf
- *IGCSE 0625:* "the force that keeps an object in orbit around the Sun is the gravitational attraction of the Sun"; orbital speed v = 2πr/T. Bridge (school-wording.md §6): "think of a planet that keeps falling towards the Sun and keeps missing it." source/research/school-wording.md

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- F = GMm/r². G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (OS College); 6.67384(80)×10⁻¹¹ (8.01SC constants). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_constants.pdf
- Moon's orbit radius 3.84×10⁸ m and period 27.3 d (OS College); 3.82×10⁸ m (8.01SC notes Ch 9). Sidereal month 2.3605847×10⁶ s, synodic 2.5514429×10⁶ s (8.01SC data): `python3 -I -c 'print(round(2.3605847e6/86400,2), round(2.5514429e6/86400,2))'` → 27.32 d, 29.53 d. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter9.pdf ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Inverse-square prediction (OS Astronomy):* `python3 -I -c 'print(round(9.8/60**2,5))'` → 0.00272 m/s². https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
- *Newton's check (OS College worked example):* `python3 -I -c 'import math; r=3.84e8; T=27.3*86400; a=4*math.pi**2*r/T**2; print(a, round(9.80*(6.38e6/r)**2,6))'` → centripetal 0.002725 m/s²; gravity predicts 0.002705 m/s² (under 1% apart). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
- **How far the Moon "falls" each second (BOOKPLAN's number).** With r = 3.84×10⁸ m and the 8.01SC sidereal period: `python3 -I -c 'import math; r=3.84e8; T=2.3605847e6; a=4*math.pi**2*r/T**2; print(round(a,6), round(0.5*a*1**2*1000,3),"mm in 1 s", round(0.5*a*60**2,2),"m in 60 s", round(0.5*9.8*1,2),"m apple 1 s")'` → a = 0.002721 m/s²; the Moon drops **1.36 mm** below its straight-line path in one second, and **4.9 m in one minute**, the same as an apple drops in its first second. (The 60² = 3600 match is Newton's 1/60² in disguise.) Inputs: https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf
- Moon's orbital speed: `python3 -I -c 'import math; r=3.84e8; T=2.3605847e6; print(round(2*math.pi*r/T,1),"m/s")'` → 1022 m/s, about 1 km/s. (Same inputs.)
- *Period from gravity (8.01SC notes eq. 9.2.5–9.2.6):* `python3 -I -c 'import math; print(round(2*math.pi*(3.82e8**3/(6.67e-11*5.98e24))**0.5,-4), round(2*math.pi*(3.82e8**3/(6.67e-11*5.98e24))**0.5/86400,1))'` → 2.35×10⁶ s = 27.2 days. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter9.pdf

## 5.2 Newton's cannon

**Concept sequence**
1. Imagine a gun on a high mountain firing horizontally, with no air. The only force after firing is gravity. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
2. A slow shot curves down and lands nearby; a faster one lands farther away. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
3. Fast enough, and Earth's surface curves away as fast as the bullet falls: it falls all the way round, a circular orbit. That "circular satellite velocity" near Earth is about 8 km/s. OS Astronomy's figure adapts Newton's own diagram (*De Mundi Systemate*, 1731 edition). https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
4. Faster still, about 11 km/s, and it escapes Earth. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
5. Astronauts feel "weightless" for the same reason as people in a lift whose cable has snapped: they and the craft are falling together. The difference is that they are falling around Earth, not into it. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
6. There is no "zero gravity" in orbit; "weightlessness" means free fall at the local acceleration due to gravity. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
7. Once in orbit above the atmosphere's drag, a satellite needs no engine to stay up; the hard part is the energy to get it there. Very low orbits slowly decay because of atmospheric drag. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
8. For a circular orbit, gravity supplies the centripetal force; the satellite's mass cancels, so every satellite at the same radius has the same speed and period. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-6-satellites-and-keplers-laws-an-argument-for-simplicity ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-3-circular-motion/ps-3-1-worked-example-orbital-circular-motion/

**Definitions**
- *Orbit:* the path of an object moving under gravity. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
- *Circular satellite velocity:* the launch speed at which the ground curves away as fast as the object falls (~8 km/s at Earth). *Escape speed:* the speed to leave Earth for good (~11 km/s). https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft
- *Microgravity:* an environment where the apparent net acceleration of a body is small compared with that at Earth's surface. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
- *IGCSE 0625:* weight is "a gravitational force on an object that has mass" (so an astronaut in orbit still has weight in the exam sense). source/research/school-wording.md §1

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- v = √(GM/r) for a circular orbit; T = 2πr/v. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-6-satellites-and-keplers-laws-an-argument-for-simplicity
- ISS height: "an average of 400 km above Earth's surface" (OS Astronomy Ch 3 Thought Questions). https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-thought-questions
- NASA ISS facts: speed "five miles per second"; orbits "about every 90 minutes"; 16 orbits in 24 hours; mass 419,725 kg. https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/ — conversion: `python3 -I -c 'print(round(5*1.609344,2), round(24*60/16,1))'` → 8.05 km/s, 90.0 min (these are NASA's round numbers).

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- **g at the ISS (BOOKPLAN's 89%):** with the 8.01SC Earth radius: `python3 -I -c 'R=6.371009e6; print(round((R/(R+400e3))**2,4))'` → **0.8853**, i.e. about 89% of surface gravity. Absolute value: `python3 -I -c 'G=6.67384e-11; M=5.97219e24; R=6.371009e6; g0=G*M/R**2; print(round(g0,3), round(G*M/(R+400e3)**2,3))'` → 9.82 m/s² at the surface, 8.69 m/s² at 400 km. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-thought-questions
- ISS speed and period at 400 km: `python3 -I -c 'import math; G=6.67384e-11; M=5.97219e24; r=6.371009e6+400e3; v=(G*M/r)**0.5; print(round(v/1000,2),"km/s", round(2*math.pi*r/v/60,1),"min", round(86400/(2*math.pi*r/v),1),"orbits/day")'` → 7.67 km/s, 92.4 min, 15.6 orbits/day. NASA's rounded "5 miles per second / 90 minutes / 16 orbits" are consistent to a few per cent. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf ; https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/

## 5.3 Ellipses

**Concept sequence**
1. Tycho Brahe recorded the positions of the Sun, Moon and planets at Hven for almost 20 years without a telescope; his data disagreed with the published tables. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
2. Kepler, who got the records after Tycho died in 1601, first assumed circles, but no circle fitted the data for Mars. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
3. Mars's orbit turned out to be an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. Its eccentricity is only about 0.1, so drawn to scale it looks almost like a circle, but the difference was decisive. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
4. Mars was lucky: among the planets other than Mercury it is unusually eccentric; otherwise Tycho's naked-eye data would not have revealed the ellipse. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system
5. Second law: a planet speeds up near the Sun and slows down far away, so the Sun–planet line sweeps equal areas in equal times. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
6. First two laws published 1609 (*The New Astronomy*); third law 1619. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf
7. Kepler's laws describe, they do not explain; Newton's gravity later showed why the orbits must be ellipses. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion ; https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation

**Definitions**
- *Ellipse:* a closed curve where the sum of the distances from two fixed points (the foci, a word Kepler invented) to any point on the curve is constant. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
- *Semimajor axis:* half the longest diameter; for a planet, its average distance from the Sun. *Eccentricity:* distance between the foci divided by the major axis (0 = circle). https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
- *Perihelion / aphelion:* closest / farthest point from the Sun; *perigee / apogee* for Earth orbits. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system
- *Kepler's laws as stated by OS Astronomy:* (1) each planet's orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus; (2) the line joining planet and Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times; (3) the square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semimajor axis. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
- *As stated by the 8.01SC notes:* (I) ellipse with the Sun at one focus; (II) the radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal time; (III) T² = k a³ with k the same for all planets. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf
- *IGCSE 0625:* mentions "elliptical orbits" and that gravity of the Sun keeps objects in orbit; the three laws by name were not captured in source/research/school-wording.md.

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- P² = a³ with P in years and a in AU; 1 AU ≈ 1.5×10⁸ km. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-1-the-laws-of-planetary-motion
- Mars: a = 228 million km, P = 1.88 yr, e ≈ 0.1 (OS Astronomy); e = 0.093 (8.282J PS3). ## 5.4 Weighing the Earth

**Concept sequence**
1. G has to be measured; the law alone does not give it. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
2. Cavendish, in 1798, more than 100 years after Newton's law, measured the tiny attraction between ordinary lab masses (tens of kilograms at most) with a torsion balance: suspended spheres twisted a fibre as they were drawn toward fixed spheres. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/7-2-newtons-law-of-universal-gravitation-and-einsteins-theory-of-general-relativity
3. Gravity between everyday objects is extraordinarily weak: two 1 kg masses 1 m apart pull with 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
4. With G known, measure g and Earth's radius, and g = GM/r² gives Earth's mass: "weighing the Earth". https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
5. Cavendish's result differs by under 1% from the modern value. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
6. Extension: Eötvös found that gravity does not depend on what the mass is made of (to 5 parts per billion). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation

**Definitions**
- *Gravitational constant G:* the proportionality factor in F = GmM/r², thought to be the same everywhere; not to be confused with g, which varies from place to place. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/7-2-newtons-law-of-universal-gravitation-and-einsteins-theory-of-general-relativity
- *Mass vs weight:* mass is the amount of matter and does not change; weight mg changes with g. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/7-2-newtons-law-of-universal-gravitation-and-einsteins-theory-of-general-relativity
- *IGCSE 0625:* mass is "a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer"; weight is "a gravitational force on an object that has mass". source/research/school-wording.md §1

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (OS); 6.67384(80)×10⁻¹¹ (8.01SC constants). OS College: G is by far the least well determined fundamental constant. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_constants.pdf
- Earth mass 5.97219×10²⁴ kg; mean radius 6.371009×10⁶ m (8.01SC data); OS uses 5.97–5.98×10²⁴ kg and 6.38×10⁶ m. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf ; https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/7-2-newtons-law-of-universal-gravitation-and-einsteins-theory-of-general-relativity

## 5.5 Predicting the sky

**Concept sequence**
1. Newton's law plus his laws of motion allow only the orbits Kepler described (conic sections: circles, ellipses, parabolas, hyperbolas). https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-5-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
2. Newton's version of Kepler's third law includes the masses: a³ = (M₁ + M₂)P² in AU, years and solar masses. Kepler missed the mass term because planets are tiny compared with the Sun. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
3. So orbits can weigh things: the Sun from the planets, Jupiter from its moons. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-3-newtons-universal-law-of-gravitation
4. Once an orbit is known, future positions can be calculated. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system
5. Comets have big, eccentric orbits (e ≥ 0.8) and spend most of their time far out, moving slowly; they whip through perihelion fast. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system
6. Real orbits are nudged by other bodies (perturbations). These nudges have been used to predict the positions of unseen planets and moons. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-6-satellites-and-keplers-laws-an-argument-for-simplicity
7. More than two bodies cannot be solved exactly; the Moon–Earth–Sun problem drove 18th-century mathematics. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf

**Definitions**
- *Perturbation:* a change in an orbit caused by bodies other than the main attractor (OS College's wording: "perturbations of the orbits due to other bodies"). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/6-6-satellites-and-keplers-laws-an-argument-for-simplicity

## 5.6 The slow road to the Moon

**Concept sequence**
1. Launch puts a spacecraft into an elongated Earth orbit. https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html
2. Each "Earth-bound perigee firing" near the closest point raises the far point (apogee) of the orbit, step by step. https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html
3. A final burn (trans-lunar injection) sends it on a path reaching out to the Moon. https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html
4. To be captured, a spacecraft must brake with its rocket when it is near its destination, so it drops into an elliptical orbit there. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft ; https://www.isro.gov.in/Ch3_Lunar_Orbit_Insertion_Press_Release.html
5. More burns shrink and round the lunar orbit; then the lander separates, brakes further and descends. https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html
6. Physics link: after each burn the craft just coasts on a Kepler orbit; the engines only change which orbit it is on. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-5-motions-of-satellites-and-spacecraft ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf

**Definitions**
- *Perigee/apogee:* closest/farthest point of an Earth orbit; for the Moon ISRO uses "perilune". https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-4-orbits-in-the-solar-system ; https://www.isro.gov.in/Ch3_Lunar_Orbit_Insertion_Press_Release.html
- *Transfer orbit:* an orbit used to move between two others (8.01SC notes Example 25.4). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter25new.pdf

**What ISRO documents (record only this)**
All from the mission updates list, https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html, unless noted:
- 6 Jul 2023: launch scheduled 14 July 2023, 14:35 IST, Second Launch Pad, SDSC-SHAR, Sriharikota.
- 14 Jul 2023: LVM3 M4 launched Chandrayaan-3 into orbit. The planned Elliptic Parking Orbit was "~170 x 36500 km" (https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html).
- 15 Jul 2023: first orbit-raising manoeuvre (Earthbound firing-1) from ISTRAC, Bengaluru → 41762 km × 173 km.
- 17 Jul 2023: second orbit-raising manoeuvre → 41603 km × 226 km.
- 22 Jul 2023: fourth orbit-raising manoeuvre (Earth-bound perigee firing) → 71351 km × 233 km.
- 25 Jul 2023: another orbit-raising manoeuvre; next firing (TLI) planned for 1 August.
- 1 Aug 2023: inserted into translunar orbit, 288 km × 369328 km; LOI planned 5 August.
- 5 Aug 2023: Lunar Orbit Insertion by retro-burning at perilune for 1835 s, starting 19:12 IST; orbit 164 km × 18074 km, "as intended" (https://www.isro.gov.in/Ch3_Lunar_Orbit_Insertion_Press_Release.html).
- 6 Aug: LBN#2 → 170 km × 4313 km. 9 Aug: 174 km × 1437 km. 14 Aug: 151 km × 179 km (circularisation phase). 16 Aug: 153 km × 163 km.
- 17 Aug: Lander Module separated from the Propulsion Module. 19 Aug: lander in 113 km × 157 km. 20 Aug: lander in 25 km × 134 km; powered descent expected 23 Aug around 17:45 IST.
- 23 Aug 2023: "successfully soft-landed on the moon". 24 Aug: rover ramped down. 4 Sep: lander and rover in sleep mode. 22 Sep: efforts to re-establish contact.
- Prime landing site 69.367621 S, 32.348126 E; mission life one lunar day (~14 Earth days); planned 100 km circular polar orbit for the propulsion module. https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html
- Masses: Propulsion Module 2148 kg, Lander 1752 kg incl. 26 kg rover, total 3900 kg (https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html). The launch brochure gives 2145.01 kg, 1749.86 kg and "Payload Mass: 3895 kg" (https://www.isro.gov.in/media_isro/pdf/Missions/LVM3/LVM3M4_Chandrayaan3_brochure.pdf). `python3 -I -c 'print(2148+1752, round(2145.01+1749.86,2))'` → 3900, 3894.87. Use "about 3.9 tonnes".

**Worked numbers we can reuse** (ISRO's figures treated as heights above the surface, **ASSUMED**; ISRO does not say)
- After Earth-bound firing-1 (173 × 41762 km): `python3 -I -c 'import math; GM=6.67384e-11*5.97219e24; a=((173+41762)/2+6371.009)*1e3; print(round(a/1e3,1),"km", round(2*math.pi*(a**3/GM)**0.5/3600,1),"h")'` → a = 27,338 km, period **12.5 h**. Inputs: https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_data.pdf
- After the fourth firing (233 × 71351 km): `python3 -I -c 'import math; GM=6.67384e-11*5.97219e24; a=((233+71351)/2+6371.009)*1e3; print(round(2*math.pi*(a**3/GM)**0.5/3600,1),"h")'` → **23.9 h**. Each burn lengthens the "year" of the spacecraft's orbit (Kepler's third law).
- First lunar orbit (164 × 18074 km), with the 8.01SC Moon mass and radius: `python3 -I -c 'import math; GM=6.67384e-11*7.3477e22; a=((164+18074)/2+1737.10)*1e3; print(round(a/1e3,1),"km", round(2*math.pi*(a**3/GM)**0.5/3600,1),"h")'` → **28.2 h** round the Moon.
- Near-circular lunar orbit (153 × 163 km): `python3 -I -c 'import math; GM=6.67384e-11*7.3477e22; a=((153+163)/2+1737.10)*1e3; print(round(2*math.pi*(a**3/GM)**0.5/60,1),"min")'` → about **2 h** (123 min).
- Do not compare the translunar apogee (369328 km) with "the" Moon distance: ISRO does not say whether it is measured from Earth's surface or centre, and the Moon's distance varies. `python3 -I -c 'print(369328+6371-384000)'` → −8301 km: an unexplained ~2% gap, so leave the comparison out.

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- "Chandrayaan went slowly because India's rocket was weak / to save fuel by going slowly." ISRO's pages fetched here state the sequence, not the reason.
