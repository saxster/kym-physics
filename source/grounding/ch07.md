## 7.1 What is energy?

**Concept sequence**
1. Start with many processes: falling water spins turbines; a refrigerator moves energy out; food keeps a body going; petrol moves a car; a spring stores and releases; the Sun fuses hydrogen. Each is energy changing form. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
2. List the forms: kinetic, gravitational, thermal, elastic, electrical, chemical, electromagnetic, nuclear and more. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
3. The bookkeeping rule: for any process, add up the change in every form of energy; the sum is zero. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
4. If your sum is *not* zero, then either your formulas for the energies are wrong or there is a kind of energy change you haven't found yet. That is how conservation becomes a tool for discovery. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
5. Draw a boundary round a "system"; whatever energy the system gains, the surroundings lose: ΔE_system + ΔE_surroundings = 0. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter14.pdf
6. Statement of the law: total energy is constant in any process; it may change form or move between systems. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy

**Definitions**
- *Conservation of energy (8.01SC notes):* the changes in all forms of energy sum to zero, ΣΔE_j = 0. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
- *Law of conservation of energy (OS College):* "Total energy is constant in any process." https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
- *Closed system (8.01SC notes):* one that only energy (not matter) can enter or leave. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter14.pdf
- *IGCSE 0625:* energy "may be stored as kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic (strain), nuclear, electrostatic and internal (thermal)"; it is "transferred between stores… by forces (mechanical work done), electrical currents (electrical work done), heating, and by electromagnetic, sound and other waves". Bridge: "School calls the place where energy sits a store and how it moves a transfer." source/research/school-wording.md §1, §6

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- ΔE₁ + ΔE₂ + … + ΔE_N = 0 (8.01SC notes eq. 13.1.2). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
- OS College's bookkeeping form: KE_i + PE_i + W_nc + OE_i = KE_f + PE_f + OE_f ("OE" = other energy). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- None needed; the bookkeeping idea is carried by 7.2–7.4 numbers.

**Demonstrations**

**Concept questions**

**Warnings the sources give**
- 8.01SC notes start by ignoring friction and random molecular motion, and say so; that simplification is temporary. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- "Energy is the ability to do work." OS Physics 9.1 says energy "can be defined as the ability to do work". Do not use this as *the* definition: thermal energy spread through the surroundings is still energy but can't be turned back into work, so the definition misleads in 7.4. Use the bookkeeping version (8.01SC notes). https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- "Energy is a substance / a fuel inside things." FALSE. It is a number we can calculate for each store, and the total stays fixed. Movability test (BOOKPLAN §2): "energy makes it go" explains nothing. source/research/misconceptions.md §6
- "Closed system = no energy is lost." OS Physics 9.2 glosses "closed system" this way; the 8.01SC notes use "closed" for a system that energy can still cross. Say "if nothing gets in or out" instead of "closed". https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-2-mechanical-energy-and-conservation-of-energy ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter14.pdf
- "Light is a very pure form of energy" (OS College 7.6). Vague; don't repeat. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy

## 7.2 mv or mv²?

**Concept sequence**
1. Do net work on an object and it speeds up. Using Newton's second law and the constant-acceleration equations, the net work turns out to equal the change in ½mv². https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
2. That quantity, ½mv², is the (translational) kinetic energy. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
3. Surprise: it goes with speed *squared*. A car at 100 km/h has four times the kinetic energy it has at 50 km/h, which helps explain why high-speed crashes are so much worse. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
4. Momentum, mv, by contrast only doubles. That was the 18th-century argument: is a moving body's "force" mv or mv²? (story: source/research/history.md, 's Gravesande and du Châtelet). Both quantities are real and both are conserved in their own way; the dispute was about which one measures what. https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/3-2-newtons-great-synthesis (momentum = mass × velocity)
5. Link to falling: a ball dropped from height h hits at v = √(2gh). Four times the height gives twice the speed and four times the kinetic energy. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy

**Definitions**
- *Kinetic energy:* the energy of motion, KE = ½mv², in joules. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- *Work–energy theorem:* the net work on a system equals its change in kinetic energy. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/
- *Joule:* 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s². https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- *IGCSE 0625:* kinetic is one of the listed energy stores. source/research/school-wording.md §1

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- KE = ½mv²; W_net = ΔKE. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- **Doubling the speed (vis viva):** `python3 -I -c 'm=1.0; v=1.0; print((0.5*m*(2*v)**2)/(0.5*m*v**2), (m*2*v)/(m*v))'` → KE × 4.0, momentum × 2.0. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- Car at 100 vs 50 km/h: same ratio, 4×. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- *Package on a conveyor (OS College worked example):* 30.0 kg at 0.500 m/s: `python3 -I -c 'print(0.5*30.0*0.500**2)'` → 3.75 J (small; that's why people can shove such packages without tiring). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- *Marble into flour, Mumbai version (heights ASSUMED, 25 cm and 1 m):* `python3 -I -c 'print(round((2*9.8*0.25)**0.5,2), round((2*9.8*1.0)**0.5,2), round((2*9.8*1.0)**0.5/(2*9.8*0.25)**0.5,2))'` → 2.21 m/s and 4.43 m/s: 4× the height gives exactly 2× the speed, and so 4× the KE. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy

## 7.3 Stores and transfers

**Concept sequence**
1. Work, in physics, is a force moving something along the force's direction: W = Fd (more generally Fd cos θ). Holding a heavy bag still does no work on it; homework is not work in this sense. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
2. Doing work on something changes its energy. Lifting a mass m through height h at steady speed takes force mg over distance h, so work mgh goes into gravitational potential energy. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy ; https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem
3. That energy belongs to the object–Earth system (how far apart two attracting things are), and it can come back as kinetic energy when the object falls. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy
4. The change mgh depends only on the change in height, not on the path (straight up, a ramp, the stairs). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/
5. With only gravity doing work, KE + PE stays the same: a roller coaster trades height for speed and back. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-2-mechanical-energy-and-conservation-of-energy ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-8-potential-energy-and-energy-conservation/
6. Only *changes* in potential energy matter; you choose where to call it zero. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter14.pdf ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy

**Definitions**
- *Work:* W = Fd cos θ, θ the angle between force and displacement. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
- *Gravitational potential energy:* ΔPE_g = mgh near Earth's surface, energy of the object–Earth system. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy
- *Mechanical energy:* KE + PE. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-2-mechanical-energy-and-conservation-of-energy
- *Conservative force:* one whose work depends only on start and end points (gravity); *non-conservative:* one whose work depends on the path (friction). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/
- *IGCSE 0625:* stores and transfers as in 7.1; transfer "by forces (mechanical work done)". Conservation is taught "through flow diagrams in Core and Sankey diagrams in Supplement". source/research/school-wording.md §1

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- ΔPE_g = mgh (valid while h is small compared with Earth's radius). https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy
- KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ when only conservative forces act. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-2-mechanical-energy-and-conservation-of-energy

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Speed at the bottom of a 1 m drop, from energy:* ½mv² = mgh, so v = √(2gh): `python3 -I -c 'print(round((2*9.8*1.0)**0.5,2))'` → 4.43 m/s, independent of mass. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy
- *Climbing a floor, Mumbai version:* Kym 40 kg (**ASSUMED**) climbing 3.0 m (**ASSUMED**, one storey): `python3 -I -c 'print(round(40*9.80*3.0))'` → 1176 J gained by the Kym–Earth system per floor. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-3-gravitational-potential-energy
- *Woman running up stairs (OS College worked example):* `python3 -I -c 'print(round(60.0*9.80*3.00), round(0.5*60.0*2.00**2), round((60.0*9.80*3.00+0.5*60.0*2.00**2)/3.50), round((60.0*9.80*3.00+0.5*60.0*2.00**2)/3.50/746,2))'` → 1764 J into PE, 120 J into KE, 538 W, 0.72 hp. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-7-power

## 7.4 Where does it go?

**Concept sequence**
1. Friction is a non-conservative force: the work it does depends on the path, and it turns mechanical energy into thermal energy. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/ ; https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
2. Example: push a lawn mower at constant speed, and the energy you put in is removed continuously by friction and leaves as heating of the surroundings; carry a briefcase upstairs, and it is stored as PE. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy-and-the-work-energy-theorem
3. In any real conversion, the *useful* output is less than the input, even though total energy is conserved. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
4. Efficiency measures the useful fraction. A coal power plant turns about 40% of the coal's chemical energy into electrical energy; the rest goes mostly to thermal energy released through flue gases and cooling towers. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
5. The thermal energy is still there, just spread out among the random motion of many molecules (8.01SC: "random motion of molecules"). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf

**Definitions**
- *Efficiency (OS College):* useful energy or work output divided by total energy input. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
- *Non-conservative force:* one whose work depends on the path; friction is the standard example. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/
- *IGCSE 0625:* efficiency = "useful energy output / total energy input" (×100% for a percentage); the words "lost", "wasted" and "dissipate" appear 0 times in the subject content. Bridge: "If a teacher says energy is 'lost', they mean it spread out into the surroundings." source/research/school-wording.md §1, §6

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- Eff = W_out / E_in (OS College). Representative values (Table 7.2): cycling and climbing 20%, swimming at the surface 2%, steam engine 17%, petrol engine 30%, diesel engine 35%, nuclear plant 35%, coal plant 42%, electric motor 98%, compact fluorescent lamp 20%, residential gas heater 90%, solar cell 10%. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
- A 60 W incandescent bulb gives only 5 W as light (OS College 7.7): `python3 -I -c 'print(round(5/60*100,1))'` → 8.3% efficient. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-7-power

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- *Bouncing ball with phyphox's acoustic stopwatch (BOOKPLAN):* the time between two clicks is the flight time t of one bounce, up and down, so the bounce height is h = gt²/8 (from free fall, OS College 2.7). ## 7.5 Power and the bill

**Concept sequence**
1. Power is how fast work is done, or how fast energy is transferred: P = W/t. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-7-power ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/20-6-power/
2. Same work, different time, different power: hauling a TV up four floors with a pulley in 2 minutes vs carrying it up the stairs in 5 minutes. https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/9-1-work-power-and-the-work-energy-theorem
3. The watt: 1 W = 1 J/s. A 60 W bulb transfers 60 J every second. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-7-power ; https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/week-7-kinetic-energy-and-work/20-6-power/
4. Energy used = power × time, E = Pt. Electricity bills count energy in kilowatt-hours because household use is typically kilowatts for hours. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-7-power
5. ## Case: The perpetual-motion machine (grounding notes)

- The bookkeeping rule (7.1) is the whole argument: a machine that delivers more energy than goes in would need the changes to sum to something other than zero, which has never been observed. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter13.pdf
- Every real device has efficiency below 1 (Table 7.2), so a machine left to run on its own output slows and stops. https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/7-6-conservation-of-energy
