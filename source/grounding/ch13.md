## 13.1 Which metals stick?

**Concept sequence**
1. "Only certain materials, such as iron, cobalt, nickel, and gadolinium, exhibit strong magnetic effects. Such materials are called ferromagnetic." Other materials show only weak effects, "detectable only with sensitive instruments" ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force); [CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
2. A magnet near unmagnetised iron magnetises it locally, with unlike poles closest. That is why the iron is pulled ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
3. Inside, small regions called domains act like small bar magnets. In each domain the atoms' poles line up. An outside magnet makes the domains line up more, and some grow ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
4. Heat or hard blows scramble the domains. Above the Curie temperature a ferromagnet cannot be magnetised. For iron this is 1043 K (770 °C) ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
5. Steel is not one thing. Ferritic stainless steels are ferromagnetic. Austenitic stainless steels are not, though cold work can make some of them weakly magnetic ([Carpenter Technology](https://www.carpentertechnology.com/blog/magnetic-properties-of-stainless-steels)).
6. Indian coins: the ₹1 and ₹2 are ferritic stainless steel. Whether a ₹5 coin is depends on its series (RBI, below).

**Definitions**
- Ferromagnetic: strongly magnetic, "after the Latin word ferrum for iron" ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
- Curie temperature: the temperature "above which they cannot be magnetized" ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
- Austenitic vs ferritic (metallurgy). "All austenitic stainless steels are paramagnetic (nonmagnetic)" when fully annealed, and cold work can make some, such as Types 302 and 304, weakly ferromagnetic. Ferritic grades are ferromagnetic ([Carpenter Technology](https://www.carpentertechnology.com/blog/magnetic-properties-of-stainless-steels)). Carpenter is a steelmaker, so this is an industry source, not OpenStax or OCW.
- **IGCSE 0625** topic 4.1 is "Simple phenomena of magnetism" ([school-wording.md](../research/school-wording.md)). Our file does not quote its wording on magnetic materials.

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- Coin compositions, quoted from RBI press releases:
  - **22 July 2011, "Issue of new series of Coins".** 50 paise, ₹1 and ₹2 are "Ferritic Stainless Steel… Iron-83% Chromium-17%". **₹5 is "Nickel Brass", Copper 75%, Zinc 20%, Nickel 5%**, 23 mm ([RBI PR 2011-2012/120](https://www.rbi.org.in/Commonman/English/scripts/PressReleases.aspx?Id=1124)).
  - **27 September 2007.** "New Ferritic Stainless Steel Coins of Rs. 5 denomination" with the theme "Connectivity and Information Technology": 23 mm, mono-metallic, Iron 83%, Chromium 17% ([RBI 2007](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=17274)).
  - **1 April 2010 (RBI Platinum Jubilee coin set).** "Re.1 and Rs.2 will be of FSS (Ferritic Stainless Steel), the Rs.5 coin will be Nickel Brass" ([RBI currency PR](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewCurrencyPressRelease.aspx?Id=22284)).
  - **2019 series** (G.S.R. 184(E), 6 March 2019): the composition table was **not readable** in this pass. The secondary copy I found omits it ([taxheal copy](https://www.taxheal.com/new-coinage-of-1-rupee-2-rupees-5-rupees-10-rupees-and-20-rupees-rules-2019.html)). See Gaps.
- Iron's Curie temperature is 1043 K, or 770 °C ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)). `python3 -I -c 'print(1043-273)'` → 770.

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- Composition check for the 2011 ₹5 coin: `python3 -I -c 'print(75+20+5)'` → 100. Iron 0%: it is a copper alloy. Whether 5% nickel in brass makes the coin noticeably magnetic is **not stated by any source fetched**, so test it and don't assert it.

**Warnings the sources give**
- Weakly magnetic materials exist, but only sensitive instruments detect them ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
- Austenitic grades can become weakly magnetic after cold work, such as bending or deep drawing ([Carpenter](https://www.carpentertechnology.com/blog/magnetic-properties-of-stainless-steels)). So a spoon's bowl may stick faintly where its handle doesn't. That conclusion is inferred from Carpenter, so test it.

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **"Magnets attract all metals"** (misconceptions.md §11). False: only a few, iron, nickel, cobalt (and gadolinium), plus alloys with the right structure ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
- **"Steel is magnetic."** It depends on the steel's crystal type, not just whether there is iron in it ([Carpenter](https://www.carpentertechnology.com/blog/magnetic-properties-of-stainless-steels)). The minimal true version: "some stainless steels are arranged so that their tiny magnets can line up, and some aren't."
- **"Every ₹5 coin is magnetic."** False per the RBI 2010–2011 releases.
- **"Nickel is in the coin, so it's magnetic."** Nickel is ferromagnetic as a metal (PH 20.1). A coin with 5% nickel in brass is a different material. Don't infer.
- **"A bigger magnet is always stronger"** (misconceptions.md §11). Our sources don't address it. Avoid claiming either way.

## 13.2 The Earth is a magnet

**Concept sequence**
1. A freely hanging magnet turns so that one end points north, and that end is called its north pole. That is a compass ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
2. Unlike poles attract. So the magnetic pole near Earth's geographic North Pole is actually a *south* magnetic pole ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force); [CP 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-1-magnets)).
3. Poles always come in pairs. Cut a magnet and each piece has a north and a south pole ([CP 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-1-magnets)). The 8.02 notes add "Unlike electric charges which can be isolated, the two magnetic poles always come in a pair" and note that a compass needle "will line up along the direction of the magnetic field" ([8.02 notes §8.1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/ch8magneti_field/)).
4. Field lines show the direction a compass's north end points, and they are denser where the field is stronger ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)). They form closed loops through the magnet ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
5. Earth's field at Mumbai has a size and a direction. It points roughly north, and it also dips about 28° into the ground (WMM, below). That dip is what Gilbert's terrella showed (history.md §6).

**Definitions**
- Magnetic field direction: "the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle points" ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
- Tesla (T) is the SI unit of magnetic field ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- A typical figure for Earth's field: "The Earth's field is about 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ T" ([CP 22.9 Example 22.6](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law)).
- **Mumbai's field, computed** from NOAA/NCEI's World Magnetic Model 2025. Coefficients are `WMM_2025.COF` (epoch 2025.0, dated 11/13/2024), taken from the `pygeomag` 1.1.0 package.
  - I implemented the synthesis myself and checked it against NOAA's official test values ([WMM2025_TEST_VALUES.txt](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/WMM2025_TEST_VALUES.txt)). Every printed figure matched, for example F = 55178.5 nT at (80°N, 0°E, 2025.0) and F = 41064.3 nT at (0°, 120°E).
  - At **Mumbai (19.08°N, 72.88°E), sea level, 28 Oct 2026 (decimal year 2026.82)**:
    - total F ≈ **43,600 nT (43.6 μT)**;
    - horizontal H ≈ **38,400 nT (38.4 μT)**;
    - vertical Z ≈ 20,700 nT, pointing down;
    - inclination ≈ **28.3°** down;
    - declination ≈ **0.0°** (a compass points almost exactly at true north in Mumbai).
  - Alibag observatory (18.64°N, 72.87°E): F ≈ 43,400 nT, inclination ≈ 27.4°.
  - NOAA's online calculator needs an API key, so I could not query it directly. Model page: [NOAA WMM](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model).
  - Check: `python3 -I -c 'import math;H,Z=38365,20688;print(round(math.hypot(H,Z)),round(math.degrees(math.atan2(Z,H)),2))'` → 43587 28.34.
- Mumbai vs the textbook value: `python3 -I -c 'print(round(43.587/50,2))'` → 0.87. So Mumbai's field is about 87% of OpenStax's round "5 × 10⁻⁵ T".

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- See 13.3 for the current that makes a wire's field equal to Mumbai's H.

**Demonstrations**
- Floating magnetised needle on a leaf in a bowl (misconceptions.md §11). Stroke the needle one way with a magnet about 20 times. Mumbai flat: safe; handle the needle with care.
- phyphox magnetometer shows Earth's field (misconceptions.md §11; BOOKPLAN sensor lab). Expect about 40–45 μT total in Mumbai (WMM above). Phone sensors are uncalibrated and affected by nearby steel, so treat the reading as rough.

**Concept questions**
- "Toward which magnetic pole of Earth is the north pole of a compass needle attracted?" The south magnetic pole, which sits near the geographic North Pole ([PH 20.1 Grasp Check](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)). Strong `:::predict`.

**Warnings the sources give**
- "Confusion arises because the geographic term 'North Pole' has come to be used (incorrectly) for the magnetic pole that is near the North Pole." ([CP 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-1-magnets))

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **"Earth's North Pole is a magnet's north pole"** (misconceptions.md §11). As a magnet it is a south pole ([CP 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-1-magnets)).
- **"Earth contains a giant bar magnet."** PH 20.1 says Earth "can be thought of as containing a giant magnet". Keep "acts like" (CP 22.1's wording) and don't say "contains". Our sources don't explain what really makes Earth's field.
- **"North pole = positive charge"** (misconceptions.md §11, Maloney). Magnetic poles are not charges ([CP 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-1-magnets) contrasts them).
- **"The compass points straight along the ground."** In Mumbai the field dips about 28° into the ground. A compass shows only the horizontal part.

**Gaps**
- The cause of Earth's field (the geodynamo) is not covered in the pages fetched.
- Gilbert, Colaba and Alibag: history.md §6 only. No OCW or OpenStax source.

## 13.3 Electricity makes magnetism

**Concept sequence**
1. Ørsted (1820) found that a current-carrying wire deflects a compass needle. This was "the first significant evidence that the movement of charges had any connection with magnets" ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)). His currents were not large ([CP 22.9](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law)).
2. Around a long straight wire the field lines are circles. Right-hand rule: thumb along the current, and the fingers curl the way the field goes ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
3. The strength is B = μ₀I/(2πr), so it is weaker farther from the wire ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force); [CP 22.9](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law)).
4. A coil of current makes a field like a bar magnet's. Iron filings show the same pattern for both ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
5. "Current: The Source of All Magnetism": even a permanent magnet's field comes from its electrons' orbital and spin currents (a crude picture) ([CP 22.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets)).
6. Ampère generalised this into Ampère's law, which became part of Maxwell's equations ([CP 22.9](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law); [8.02 Class 15–16](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/pages/class-activities/)).

**Definitions**
- μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A, "the permeability of free space" ([PH 20.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-1-magnetic-fields-field-lines-and-force)).
- **IGCSE 0625 4.5** includes the "magnetic effect of a current" ([school-wording.md](../research/school-wording.md)). Lower Secondary 8Pe covers "magnetic fields; electromagnets".

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- B = μ₀I/(2πr) ([CP 22.9 eq 22.24](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-9-magnetic-fields-produced-by-currents-amperes-law)).

**Worked numbers we can reuse**

- B at 1 cm from 1 A: `python3 -I -c 'import math;print(round(4*math.pi*1e-7*1/(2*math.pi*0.01)*1e6,1))'` → 20.0 μT. A compass under a wire carrying about 1 A then turns by about atan(20/38.4): `python3 -I -c 'import math;print(round(math.degrees(math.atan(20/38.4)),1))'` → 27.5°. This ASSUMES the wire runs north–south over the compass, so its field is perpendicular to H.

## 13.4 The first motor

**Concept sequence**
1. A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force, F = IℓB sin θ ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
2. Motors "convert electrical energy into mechanical energy". They are "the most common application of magnetic force on current-carrying wires" ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
3. In a loop, the forces on opposite sides make a torque, τ = NIAB sin θ ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
4. To keep turning the same way, brushes reverse the current every half turn ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).

**Definitions**
- Electric motor: a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy using the force on a current in a magnetic field ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
- **IGCSE 0625 4.5** covers "force on a conductor, d.c. motor" ([school-wording.md](../research/school-wording.md)).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- F = IℓB sin θ; τ = NIAB sin θ ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- Order of magnitude (ASSUMED: 1 A, 2 cm of wire, B ≈ 0.1 T near a small magnet): `python3 -I -c 'print(1*0.02*0.1)'` → 0.002 N. That is a small push, about the weight of 0.2 g (0.002/9.8 kg). This explains why home motors use light wire. The B ≈ 0.1 T value is unsourced.

**Demonstrations**
- 8.02 Class 15 PRS "Experiment 6: Predictions 1–5" ask for the force on a wire above, in front of and behind a magnet ([8.02 PRS15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/prs_w06d2_qonly/)). Use the same predict-then-look structure.
- **Homopolar motor (BOOKPLAN 13.4)**: an AA cell, a neodymium disc magnet and a bent copper wire. **Not in any source fetched.**
  - Safety for the adult-led version: brief runs only, because the wire is a near short and gets hot. Neodymium magnets pinch fingers and must never be swallowed.
  - Faraday's 1821 version used mercury (history.md §6). **Never use mercury.**
  - The safety gate must review this demo.

**Concept questions**
- PH 20.2: "What happens to the torque provided by an electric motor if you double the number of coils?" It doubles, since τ ∝ N ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
- 8.02 PRS15 Predictions 1–3: which way is the force on a wire above, in front of or behind a magnet? ([8.02 PRS15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/prs_w06d2_qonly/))

**Warnings the sources give**
- Torque is zero at θ = 0, which is why the brushes are needed ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **"The magnet pushes the electricity round."** The force acts on the moving charges in the wire, so on the wire. The magnet is pushed back equally (Ch 3). There is no "electricity substance" being shoved.
- **"A motor uses up current."** No. Same current in and out (Ch 12). It converts energy.

**Gaps**
- No source covers the homopolar motor or Faraday's 1821 mercury apparatus (history.md has the scene).

## 13.5 Magnetism makes electricity

**Concept sequence**
1. In 1831 Faraday and Joseph Henry independently showed "that magnetic fields can produce currents" ([PH 20.3](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-3-electromagnetic-induction)).
2. Faraday's ring: switching on a current in one coil gives a pulse in the other coil. Switching it off gives a pulse the opposite way, and a steady current gives nothing ([CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux)).
3. Moving a magnet in or out of a coil induces current "only when the magnet moves with respect to the coil". Opposite motion gives opposite current, faster motion gives more, and none flows when there is no motion ([PH 20.3](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-3-electromagnetic-induction); [CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux)).
4. The general rule: *any change in magnetic flux* Φ = BA cos θ induces an emf ([CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux); [8.02 Class 20 slides](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/presentati_w09d1/), "Ways to Induce EMF": change B, A or θ).
5. Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change ([8.02 Experiment 5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/experiment5/): "the system always responds so as to try to keep things the same").
6. A generator is a coil rotated in a magnetic field. It is "very similar in construction to a motor" ([CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux); [PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)). Its emf alternates as ε₀ sin ωt ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
7. Power stations turn the coil with falling water, steam or wind ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)). Transformers step the voltage up for transmission (120–700 kV) and down for homes ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).

**Definitions**
- Electromagnetic induction: "Any change in magnetic flux Φ induces an emf" ([CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux)).
- Faraday's law: ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt ([8.02 Class 20 slides](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/presentati_w09d1/)).
- **IGCSE 0625 4.5** covers "induction, a.c. generator… transformer" ([school-wording.md](../research/school-wording.md)).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- Φ = BA cos θ, in T·m² ([CP 23.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux)).
- Transformer: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)).
- Transmission at 120–700 kV. Substations step down to 5–30 kV, then homes get "120, 240, or 480 V" ([PH 20.2](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/20-2-motors-generators-and-transformers)). India's figure is 230/240 V (see ch12 §12.6, MERC).

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- Why transmit at high voltage: wire loss is I²R. For fixed power, I = P/V, so raising V by 10× cuts the loss by 100×. `python3 -I -c 'P=1e6;R=1;print([round((P/V)**2*R) for V in (10e3,100e3)])'` → [10000, 100] W (ASSUMED 1 MW and 1 Ω). This is the PH 20.2 argument with our numbers.

**Demonstrations**

  - **Falling magnet**: it "slows as it approaches a copper ring which has been immersed in liquid nitrogen".
  - **Jumping ring**: "An aluminum ring jumps into the air when the solenoid beneath it is energized".
  - **Electric guitar** pickup.
  - Mumbai flat: the jumping ring needs mains, so **no**. A home "magnet dropped down a copper or aluminium pipe" variant is our adaptation, not from a source. It is safe (no electricity), but needs a strong magnet, so give the swallowing and pinch warnings.

It needs a galvanometer or a sensitive multimeter (feasible). An LED wired to a coil will not light from a hand-moved fridge magnet; don't promise that (ASSUMED, not sourced).

## 13.6 Light is electromagnetic

**Concept sequence**
1. Maxwell brought together the work of Ørsted, Coulomb, Ampère, Gauss and Faraday ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)). In words: field lines start on charges; magnetic field lines have no ends; a changing magnetic field induces an electric field; and magnetic fields come from moving charges *or changing electric fields* ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)).
2. A changing E makes B and a changing B makes E. Together they travel as a wave at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ([8.02 notes §13.3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/chapte13em_waves/)).
3. The wave speed matched the measured speed of light. Maxwell "correctly predicted that visible light is caused by electromagnetic waves" ([CP 24.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-1-maxwells-equations-electromagnetic-waves-predicted-and-observed)).
4. Hertz (starting in 1887) made and detected the waves with tuned circuits. He showed they travel at the speed of light and reflect, refract and interfere ([CP 24.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-1-maxwells-equations-electromagnetic-waves-predicted-and-observed); [8.02 notes §13.3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/chapte13em_waves/)).
5. "Electromagnetic waves are produced when electric charges are accelerated… Radiation cannot be produced by stationary charges or steady currents." ([8.02 notes §13.8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/chapte13em_waves/))
6. Radio, microwaves, IR, visible, UV, X-rays and γ-rays are the same kind of wave with different frequencies ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)).

**Definitions**
- EM wave: oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)).
- **IGCSE 0625 3.3** is "Electromagnetic spectrum" ([school-wording.md](../research/school-wording.md)).

**Laws, equations, standard values**
- c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀), with ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²) and μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A ([CP 24.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-1-maxwells-equations-electromagnetic-waves-predicted-and-observed)). `python3 -I -c 'import math;print("%.4e"%(1/math.sqrt(4*math.pi*1e-7*8.85e-12)))'` → 2.9986e+08 m/s.
- Visible light: "between 400 nm and 750 nm". Ultraviolet runs "from 400 nm down to about 10 nm" ([CP 24.3](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-3-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)).
- Production by type ([CP 24.3 Table 24.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-3-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)):

  | Type | Produced by |
  |---|---|
  | Radio and TV | "Accelerating charges" |
  | Microwaves | "Accelerating charges & thermal agitation" |
  | IR, visible, UV | "Thermal agitations & electronic transitions" |

**Worked numbers we can reuse**
- J. C. Bose's 5 mm waves (history.md §6): `python3 -I -c 'print(round(2.998e8/5e-3/1e9,1))'` → 60.0 GHz.
- Visible light in frequency: `python3 -I -c 'c=2.998e8;print(round(c/750e-9/1e12),round(c/400e-9/1e12))'` → 400 to 750 THz.
- A 1 m radio wave (GMRT is a "metrewave" telescope, history.md §9): `python3 -I -c 'print(2.998e8/1/1e6)'` → 299.8 MHz. GMRT's actual frequency bands are not sourced.
- Indian mains as a "wave" (50 Hz): `python3 -I -c 'print(round(2.998e8/50/1e3))'` → 5996 km wavelength.

**Demonstrations**

**Fidelity traps for a 12-year-old version**
- **OpenStax imprecision.** PH 15.1 says "Electromagnetic radiation is generated by a moving electric charge, that is, by an electric current" ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)). 8.02 says steady currents do **not** radiate; only *accelerating* charges do ([8.02 notes §13.8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/chapte13em_waves/)). The minimal true version: "shake a charge back and forth and it sends out a wave."
- **OpenStax error on remotes.** PH 15.1 and CP 24.3 group TV remote controls with radio waves ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum); [CP 24.3 Table 24.1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/24-3-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)). A *typical* TV remote uses about 940 nm infrared ([NASA Infrared Waves](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/07_infraredwaves/)). Some remotes and car keys are radio, so say "a typical TV remote".
- **"Light needs a medium."** Maxwell's own wording speaks of "the same medium" (history.md §6 quote), but EM waves "can all travel across empty space" ([PH 15.1](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/15-1-the-electromagnetic-spectrum)). If the Maxwell quote is used, flag that the "medium" idea was later dropped. Our sources don't tell that story, so keep it short.
- **The Hertz "no use" quote**: HEDGE (history.md §6; BOOKPLAN §9).

**Gaps**
- J. C. Bose and GMRT: history.md only. GMRT's operating frequencies are unsourced.

### Independent reader follow-up, 30 October 2026: receiver energy

The battery powers the bell in the Bose story; that is not universal to receivers. A crystal radio can produce sound from received-wave energy alone, without local battery power. Source checked: [Open University, Building a crystal radio set](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/science-maths-technology/science/physics-and-astronomy/physics/building-crystal-radio-set). This is a source-supported counterexample, not a new household antenna-building activity.
