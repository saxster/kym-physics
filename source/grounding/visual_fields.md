## Model contracts and traps

### Charge and circuits

- `charge`: fixed positive centres; transferred electrons are counted. In polarisation modes the removed electrons remain represented separately so the neutral sample is not falsely given the source's opposite charge. Bound charges have smaller displacements than mobile conductor charge; displacements are schematic.
- `cage`: analytic uniform external field plus induced conducting-sphere dipole. Outside, the induced field is R³[3(E·r)r/r⁵−E/r³]; in the empty cavity it is −E. Their sum vanishes inside. Transparency is an inspection view, not an opening. This electrostatic model does not claim to compute RF shielding.
- `circuit-signal`: one metre takes 10 ns at a chosen 10⁸ m/s signal speed, while a chosen 0.1 mm/s electron drift moves only 10⁻¹² m in that interval. Distinct clock scales are labelled.
- `circuit-diagnose`: nonlinear illustrative exponential LED I=I_s[exp(V_LED/0.08 V)−1], with I_s selected for 20 mA at 2.0 V red / 3.3 V white. Bisection solves emf=V_LED+I(330 Ω+r); terminal voltage=emf−Ir. Open/reverse states give zero current. The plotted load curve and source line share their selected operating-point marker. These are declared teaching parameters, not a fitted LED specification. MIT 6.012 Lecture 16 supports the exponential junction model; its actual thermal voltage is not asserted to equal our chosen effective 0.08 V.
- `circuit-probes`: parallel branch currents share terminal voltage and add; series current is common. Unequal branch resistances are explicit. The original circuit widgets remain available.
- `protection`: a prospective fault with protective-earth, isolated-case and virtual alternative-return cases. A latched switch opens above the chosen ideal 30 mA imbalance; a separate balanced 20 A overload opens a 16 A comparison breaker despite zero imbalance. Post-trip currents are zero. No human current/dose prediction, real trip-time certification or installation procedure is inferred from the display.

### Magnetic fields and machines

- `domains`: schematic domain orientation and two complete cut pieces. The linked region-width diagram shows schematic domain-wall movement as the favourable region grows. The model does not quantitatively solve exchange interactions or hysteresis. Never label permanent-magnet moments as moving classical electron orbits.
- `earth-field`: centred dipole moment points toward geographic south, giving inward dip in northern latitudes. tan(dip)=2tan(latitude). This deliberately differs from the book's measured Mumbai field; the display calls it an ideal dipole.
- `field-probe`: long straight wire B=μ₀I/(2πr); 1 A at 1 cm gives 20 μT. Vector addition with the chosen 38.4 μT northward background yields 27.50° deflection. Coil mode sums the on-axis Biot–Savart fields from selected circular turns; it fixes the centre probe and prints its distinct arrow scale. The tram example omits its return conductor and is labelled illustrative.
- `motor`: N=10, B=0.5 T, rectangular sides 0.2 m and area 0.04 m². θ is the angle between coil normal and B. Coil scene rotation is 90°−θ. Current reverses when sinθ changes sign in commutated mode. Opposite wire currents experience opposite I L×B forces; summing r×F gives N I A B sinθ with the appropriate commutator sign. Explicit tests check this vector/scalar agreement. Run uses chosen inertia 0.08 kg·m² and viscous damping 0.35 s⁻¹. Opening sets electrical torque to zero while an already moving rotor coasts. Holding is an imposed inspection clamp; back emf is omitted.
- `generator`: linked flux=NABcosθ, emf=NABωsinθ. Play advances θ at the selected ω; the apparent rotation is externally supplied, and the scene does not invent load current.
- `transformer`: ideal common changing flux, V₂/V₁=N₂/N₁ and V₁I₁=V₂I₂. Steady DC gives no sustained secondary output. Switching transients, losses and saturation are omitted. Drawn turns symbolise the counted turn ratios.
- `em-wave`: plane-wave E and B are mutually perpendicular, transverse to +x propagation, and in phase. Rotating polarisation rotates both. E and cB use independent diagram scales; curves describe values at points, not material tracks. f=c/λ.

### Earth, Moon and Sun

- `day-night`: equinox local solar-time geometry; neither civil clock correction nor actual star ephemeris is promised.
- `seasons3d`: the globe axis remains fixed in inertial space around a circular orbit. Declination is the exact geometric asin(sin(tilt)sin(orbital longitude)), not tilt times sine. Local noon altitude and daylight follow the same declination. Tilt zero leaves twelve-hour days at the non-polar latitudes provided.
- `moon-rotation`: a synchronous near-side marker rotates once per orbit. A non-rotating sphere keeps its marker fixed in space. Illumination is tied to the Sun direction, independently of the face marker.
- `eclipses`: lunar plane fixed at its selected inclination; nodes lie on the x axis. Lunar coordinates are D(cos u, sin u sin i, sin u cos i). The orbit line uses the same coordinates. Sun direction rotates with year position. The lit fraction is (1−m_hat·s_hat)/2. Solar/lunar intersection tests use finite Sun, Earth and Moon dimensions; mean radii and circular distance are model choices. The visible Earth-shadow frustum and Moon penumbra are calculated at the 3D common scale, not fitted by eye. The Sun is outside the displayed volume. The 2D height diagram uses compressed horizontal distance, named explicitly. These controls do not predict calendar dates, visibility or exact eclipse contacts.
- `tides3d`: compute the source acceleration at each surface point and subtract the source acceleration at Earth's centre. Points cover three latitude rings rather than only an equatorial cross-section. The Sun can be included separately. The linked cos(2θ) graph is an ideal equilibrium response, not a hydrodynamic forecast or Haji Ali tide table.
- `foucault`: local slow precession=−Ω sin(latitude); Ω uses a 23.9344696 h sidereal day. Northern rotation is clockwise in the floor plan, south reverses, equator vanishes. The lesson uses the local small-amplitude approximation and does not claim a globally fixed swing plane at every latitude.
- `halo`: Sun and Earth point-mass acceleration are compared with Ω²r needed for the Earth's orbital year. They do not cancel at L1. The three-dimensional 177.86-day halo is an explicitly schematic closed curve with different projections, not a solved restricted-three-body trajectory. ISRO's station-keeping account explains why a real mission must correct its path.

### Modern physics and stellar inference

- `photoelectric`: E=1239.841984/λ_nm eV, work function 2.71 eV. Maximum KE=E−work, stopping voltage is that positive difference in volts. Photon flux controls counts; no photon-energy pooling is used. A uniform electron-energy distribution is an illustrative collection model. Multiphoton/high-intensity phenomena lie outside the single-photon model.
- `atom-scale`: approximately 10⁵ diameter ratio; a one-centimetre nucleus corresponds to a one-kilometre atom reference width. The nucleus marker is separately enlarged except in common-scale mode. The surrounding cloud is symbolic, never a planetary orbit or hard boundary.
- `radiation-decay`: each simulated nucleus has an independently sampled exponential decay time, N_expected=N₀2^(−t/T_half). A fixed seed makes the example reproducible. Beam exposure, material contamination and residual emission are separate modes. No radiological safety thresholds are taught by the model.
- `solar-fusion`: atomic masses 1.00782503223 u and 4.00260325413 u give a released fraction about 0.7119%. Energy=m_released c². The net account includes electrons and neutrinos; atomic masses are bookkeeping, not atoms in a cool solar core. Radiative transport is shown as a seeded random walk, convection as outward/returning parcels, and surface emission as outward markers. They are schematic stage sequences, not a photon transit-time simulation.
- `stellar-spectrum`: ground-state Saha approximation at fixed electron density 10²⁰ m⁻³ followed by Boltzmann n=2 population. Both ionisation and excitation matter, so the line proxy peaks at intermediate temperature for fixed composition. Line depth is visibly a population proxy, not a fitted stellar-atmosphere spectrum.
- `distance-ladder`: parallax arcseconds=baseline_AU/d_pc with a selectable 0.1–2 AU baseline, with separately stated 0.001 arcsecond measurement uncertainty. The Cepheid M_V=−2.76log10(P)−1.4 relation is an illustrative calibration; extinction, metallicity and calibration error are omitted. Received flux falls as distance squared. The curve is explicitly normalised, with actual mean/maximum/minimum relative flux annotations and time ticks changing with the selected period. The apparent-size/brightness marker is symbolic and not used as the measurement.
- `white-dwarf`: ideal zero-temperature Nauenberg form R=7788 km sqrt[(1.44/M)^(2/3)−(M/1.44)^(2/3)]. At/above 1.44 no stable-model radius is displayed. The limiting mathematical zero is not a real zero-size white dwarf. Initial stellar and final core mass are independently selected, and no universal initial-to-final mapping is invented. Mean density uses selected core mass and radius.
- `cosmos`: selected observers all recover v=Hd, H=70 km/s/Mpc as a chosen illustrative value. Bound-galaxy markers keep fixed size while separations expand. Wavelength ratio=a and z=a−1 are shown; the wavelength comparison visibly reduces the crest count in a fixed viewing window as a grows. This is homogeneous Hubble flow, not a calculation of peculiar velocities or high-redshift recession observations.

### Distinctive story experiments

- `telescope`: equatorial axle elevation equals geographic latitude. Ooty's cylindrical trough is represented by parabolic cross-section wires, not a circular dish. The 200 cm comparison window displays a 100 cm wavelength while the selected spacing changes the grid count and spacing. Mesh response compared with wavelength is qualitative; no precise RF reflection coefficient is claimed.
- `tracks`: β=p/sqrt(p²+m²); multiple-scattering proxy uses 13.6 MeV/(βp)sqrt(x/X₀)[1+0.038ln(x/X₀)] at chosen x/X₀=0.01. The seeded track is spatially magnified; grain-density proxy is 1/β². The lesson does not reconstruct historical plates or infer a historical mass from invented measurements.
- `parity`: illustrative angular density W(μ)=(1−aμ)/2 gives hemisphere fractions 1/2±a/4. The chosen a=0.8 is not historical measured data. Under x reflection, y-directed axial spin reverses while y-directed polar electron velocity does not; the correlation is different. Spin arrows do not depict a classical spinning nucleus.
- `bose-counting`: two labelled particles in two states have four labelled arrangements; indistinguishable bosons have three occupation states; two identical same-spin fermions have one allowed occupation. General probabilities are not assigned merely from the count.
- `eclipse-evidence`: Einstein solar-limb prediction 1.75 arcseconds scales as inverse impact parameter. The simulated point is prediction+0.12 arcseconds, with an independently controlled uncertainty. Zero, half and full predictions can be included/excluded by that interval; agreement does not prove a theory. The 3D ray bend is enlarged and the arcsecond plot is the measurement representation.
