# Motion renderer corrections and their physical contracts

29 October 2026. Supplement to `visual_mechanics.md`, following the independent mechanics gate. These are original calculated illustrations, not historical measurements or a biomechanical prediction.

The supporting primary pages were inspected during this work: [Newton’s second law and system boundaries](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-3-newtons-second-law-of-motion-concept-of-a-system), [projectile motion](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/3-4-projectile-motion), [impulse](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/8-2-impulse), and [Eratosthenes’ geometry](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/2-2-ancient-astronomy). The derivations below extend these principles; they do not claim that a source provides our particular cartoon model.

`mechanics_motion_models.mjs` supplies geometry that the actual renderer consumes. Tests in `test_mechanics_motion_geometry.mjs` check physical constraints on that geometry independently.

- **Local shadows.** For sphere radius R, radial unit normal n and gnomon height h, the base is Rn and top is (R+h)n. A vertical downward solar ray reaches the tangent ground after h/cos(angle), giving tangent shadow length h tan(angle). Tests require the endpoint to lie in the actual tangent plane and the incoming ray to remain parallel at both cities. The globe and local inset use the same selected angle.
- **Released arrow.** The model uses acceleration (−b vx, −g−b vy). The b=0 limit is x=v₀t, y=6−gt²/2. For b>0, k=(1−exp(−bt))/b, x=v₀k, y=6−g(t−k)/b. Drag always has nonpositive dot product with velocity. Body size is enlarged and lift/wind omitted; this is a force-source lesson, not archery prediction. No forward bow force survives release.
- **Braking passenger and bag.** Feet follow a prescribed train deceleration. The upper body is a 1.4 m inverted rigid rod, initially vertical and moving with the train. About its accelerating pivot, θ̈=(g sinθ+a cosθ)/L. The short 0.6 s sequence integrates this equation with 1 ms steps; the loose bag retains its ground velocity on an ideal smooth floor. Both views subtract the same observer coordinate. Tests require fixed torso length, a stationary zero-speed limit and the bag’s common ground motion. Muscle response, stepping and collisions are omitted.
- **Supporting materials.** Normal and tangent bases follow the selected support tilt. The normal force is mg cosθ and uphill friction is mg sinθ. The load’s bottom and support’s top use exactly one contact position. Displacement is visually enlarged, bounded and explicitly separated from the printed physical compression N/k. Spring, sponge, ruler and transparent table views keep that contact. Lattice sites remain ordered under the largest displayed compression.
- **Contact and impulse.** Contact junctions bridge the two actual surfaces and shear in sticking mode. Sliding animates a labelled schematic stretch, break and reformation cycle. The tablecloth graph has a physical force/time scale; its shaded area is FΔt=mΔv. Mass scales both friction and impulse while leaving the acceleration unchanged under the stated Coulomb model.
- **Rolling ramp.** Both the slider and marked solid sphere use the same distance/velocity chart scales. The rolling marker angle is distance/radius; the ideal uniform sphere has acceleration 5g sinθ/7. Longitudinal motion is shown on a common physical scale while ramp height is framed separately for legibility.

All sources are credited by the generated book’s experiment sources. The independent gate’s original findings remain intact; subsequent closure evidence must identify the rebuilt code and screenshots.
