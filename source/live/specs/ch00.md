# Live specs: Chapter 0, The physicist's way of seeing

BOOKPLAN §5 marks no live figures in Chapter 0; its live piece is the **Powers of Ten explorer** (`m_scale`, front
matter `#explore-scale`), which Section 0.3 links to and warms up for. The notes below are for whoever builds that
explorer, plus one optional widget that would earn its place in the case file. Do not add `:::live` lines to
`src/ch00.md` until the widgets exist.

## Notes for the Powers of Ten explorer (`#explore-scale`)

- Section 0.3 tells the reader to "start at yourself and zoom out until the Earth fills the view, counting the ×10
  steps; then zoom in to a hydrogen atom and count again". The explorer's readout should make those counts easy:
  show the current power of ten and the number of ×10 steps from the previous rung ("Earth: 10⁷ m, 7 steps up from you").
- Rungs that Figure 0.3 (`c0_ladder`) already uses, from OpenStax Physics Table 1.3 and College Physics 2e 1.2, so the
  two must agree: proton 10⁻¹⁵ m · hydrogen atom 10⁻¹⁰ m · a wave of visible light 10⁻⁶ m · grain of sand 10⁻³ m ·
  a person 10⁰ m · Earth's diameter 10⁷ m · the Sun ~10⁹ m · Earth–Sun 10¹¹ m · one light year 10¹⁶ m · Milky Way 10²¹ m.
- Gaps the grounding note flags: **Mumbai's size and the Earth–Moon distance are in no fetched source.** If the explorer
  adds those rungs (BOOKPLAN wants "Kym → Mumbai → Earth → Moon's orbit"), the builder must source them and tell the
  fact gate; the chapter text deliberately does not state either number.
- The scale is logarithmic. Never show a "0" on it (OpenStax's own log-scale figure makes that mistake); the axis runs
  …, 10⁻¹, 10⁰, 10¹, … in both directions.
- Honesty: real ratios only. If the zoom animates, say in the readout that the zoom is slowed, not the sizes.

## Optional: `c0_eratosthenes` → `w_shadow` (not in BOOKPLAN; proposed)

- **Still figure:** `c0_eratosthenes` (Earth as a circle, two sticks, parallel rays, the shadow angle equal to the
  central angle).
- **What the reader controls (≤ 3):**
  1. a slider for the *distance between the two sticks*, 1,000 to 10,000 stadia (default 5,000);
  2. a slider for the *measured shadow angle* at the northern stick, 1° to 30° (default 7.2°); the drawing's central
     angle follows it exactly;
  3. a menu for *which stadion*: Olympic (192 m) · "about 1/6 km" (166.7 m) · 160 m · "unknown".
- **Readout:** "1/50 of a circle → circumference = 50 × 5,000 = 250,000 stadia". With a stadion chosen, also "= 48,000 km,
  20% above today's 40,000 km" (Olympic) or "= 41,700 km, 4% above" (1/6 km) or "= 40,000 km exactly" (160 m). With
  "unknown" chosen: "somewhere between a few percent and about 20% too large; the unit was never written down".
- **Physics it must honour:** circumference = 360° ÷ angle × distance; angles and arcs drawn consistently (the central
  angle equals the shadow angle because the rays are parallel); the Sun's rays stay parallel at every setting.
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** nothing moves; the angle is drawn true to the slider, so at 7.2° it is small and the
  readout should say "drawn to scale: this is why Eratosthenes needed a tall column".
- **Sensors/audio:** none.
