## 1.2 `c1_graph_walk` → `w_graphwalk` (BOOKPLAN: Live)

- **Still figure:** `c1_graph_walk`, a position–time graph of a corridor walk (forward 2 m/s for 3 s, still for 3 s,
  back 1 m/s for 3 s, still at 3 m), with a friend's dashed line (from 8 m, back at 0.5 m/s) crossing it at t = 4 s
  and t = 8 s.
- **What the reader controls (≤ 3):**
  1. **tabs**: "Replay Figure 1.2" (the preset walk and the friend, drawn in real time over 12 s) · "Walk it yourself";
  2. in "Walk it yourself", a **position slider** (0–8 m along a drawn corridor above the graph): while the clock runs,
     wherever the thumb is, that is where the walker is, and the graph draws the line live;
  3. a **Start / Stop** button (12 s runs; Stop freezes the graph; a second press clears it). Reduced motion: the
     preset draws at once, fully; the walk-it-yourself mode still works because the reader drives it.
- **Readout (one or two sentences, live):** "t = 4.0 s, position 6.0 m, velocity 0 m/s: the line is flat, you are
  standing still." / "velocity −1.0 m/s: the line slopes down, you are walking back." In the replay, at the two
  crossings: "4 s: same place as your friend, different speeds (0 and 0.5 m/s)" and "8 s: same place again, 1.0 and
  0.5 m/s". Velocity shown is the slope over
  the last 0.5 s (Δposition ÷ Δtime), rounded to 0.1 m/s.
- **Physics it must honour:** the horizontal axis is *time* (label it "time (s)", never leave it bare); the line can
  never go backwards in time; velocity = slope; a flat line is zero velocity; a downhill line is negative velocity.
  The corridor drawing above the graph shows the walker's real one-dimensional path, so the reader sees that a sloping
  line is not a sloping path.
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** nothing; the clock runs in real time (say so in the readout: "real time").
- **Sensors/audio:** none.

## 1.5 `c1_toss_strobe` → `w_videotrack` (BOOKPLAN: Live; uses `api.video()`)

- **Still figure:** `c1_toss_strobe`, the one-metre toss drawn every 0.1 s against 10 cm tiles, with the gaps labelled.
- **What the reader controls (≤ 3):**
  1. **"Choose a video"** (`api.video({label, fps})`): a slow-motion clip from the phone; the fps menu the API provides
     (30 / 60 / 120 / 240, default 240). The clip never leaves the device, and the readout says so.
  2. **"Mark every …"**: 0.1 s (default) · 0.05 s · every frame. The frame-step buttons jump by that interval, so each
     tap marks the ball at the next sample; at 240 fps "0.1 s" means every 24th frame, at 120 fps every 12th, at
     30 fps every 3rd.
  3. **Scale**: tap the top and bottom of one tile (or two tape marks) and type the real distance in cm (default 10 cm).
     Until the scale is set, heights show in "tiles".
  Before any clip is chosen, a **Demo** tab plays the computed one-metre toss (the still's own numbers, V₀ = 4.43 m/s,
  heights [0, 39.4, 68.9, 88.7, 98.7, 98.9, 89.2, 69.8, 40.6, 1.5] cm at 0.1 s steps) so the widget teaches with no
  camera at all.
- **Readout:** a height–time graph of the marks, drawn as the reader taps, plus a table of gaps. The sentence under
  it updates: "Gaps so far: 39, 30, 20 cm. Each gap is about 9.8 cm shorter than the one before: that shrinking is the
  acceleration." At the top: "between 0.4 s and 0.5 s the ball moved 0.2 cm, yet the gaps kept shrinking at the same
  rate: zero velocity, full acceleration." When six or more marks exist, show an estimate of g from the change of
  gap per sample² (g ≈ Δgap ÷ Δt², in m/s²), with its spread, and "the textbook value is 9.8 m/s²; yours will be a
  little lower if the ball is light and the air slows it".
- **Physics it must honour:** y in video pixels converts to cm only through the reader's own scale; time comes from
  frame numbers ÷ fps (use `requestVideoFrameCallback` times where available). **Never compare neighbouring frames by
  default**: at 240 fps the gap changes by g/f² = 0.17 mm per frame, below what a phone camera resolves against a tile,
  and the "every frame" option must say so in the readout. The fit for g must use the sampled interval, not the frame
  interval.
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** nothing; the readout states the real sample interval and frame rate.
- **Sensors/audio:** `api.video()` only (file picker, no camera stream). No upload, no storage beyond the page's memory.

## Optional: `c1_four_cars` → `w_accelcars` (proposed, not in BOOKPLAN)

- Tabs for the car's direction (right / left), a slider for acceleration from −2 to +2 m/s², Play. The v arrow grows or
  shrinks each frame by a·dt, the a arrow stays fixed; the readout says "speeding up" or "slowing down" from the
  relative directions, and "a is negative" / "a is positive" from the sign, so the reader sees the two statements come
  apart. The car stops and reverses when v passes zero (and the readout says "v = 0 for an instant; a unchanged", the
  seed of 1.5). Honest ratios: arrow lengths proportional to v and a; real time.
