## c2_two_ramps → `w_ramp` (Section 2.3, "The ball that never stops")

**What it teaches.** Galileo's thought experiment: with less friction the ball climbs back nearer its starting height,
and with a flatter second ramp it travels further to get there. With no friction and a flat ramp it never stops.

**Controls (3).**
1. *Second ramp angle* slider: 0° (flat) to 40°, step 5°, default 30°.
2. *Friction* slider: μ = 0 to 0.30, step 0.02, default 0.10, shown as "friction 0.10" with "none" at 0.
3. *Release* button (also serves as reset). No autoplay.

**Model.** Treat the ball as a sliding puck (a point mass), not a rolling ball: say so in the readout ("drawn as a
sliding puck; a real ball also spins, which Chapter 7 can afford"). First ramp fixed at 30°, start height 1.0 m.
Along a slope of angle θ the acceleration along the surface is g(sin θ − μ cos θ) on the way down and −g(sin θ + μ cos θ)
on the way up (friction always against the sliding); on the flat it is −μg. g = 9.8 m/s². Check case from the
grounding note: θ = 25°, 60 kg, 45 N friction (μ = 45/(60 g cos 25°)) gives 3.39 m/s² down the slope. Integrate with a
fixed small step (e.g. 2 ms) and clamp the puck to the track; stop when speed falls below 1 mm/s and the net
along-track force would not restart it.

**Readout.** One or two sentences, updated live. Worked examples (computed 2026-10-28 with python3 -I; first ramp 30°,
start height 1.00 m): at μ = 0.10 and a 30° second ramp, "Highest point reached: 0.70 m of the 1.00 m it started with.
Friction spread the rest out as warming." At μ = 0.10 and 15°: 0.60 m, after 2.33 m along the ramp. With μ = 0, any
angle: "Back to exactly 1.00 m: with no friction, nothing takes the motion away." With the flat ramp and μ = 0: "Still
going at 4.43 m/s. Nothing is slowing it, so nothing stops it (the track repeats on screen; the real puck would carry
straight on)." With the flat ramp and μ = 0.10: "Stopped after 8.3 m. Friction did that." (μ = 0.20: 3.3 m.) Also
name what is exaggerated: "Motion is shown at half speed."

**Physics it must honour.** Energy bookkeeping every frame: height regained ≤ start height, equality only at μ = 0;
with μ = 0 the puck reaches exactly the start height on any up-slope (floating-point tolerance 1 mm); speed at the
bottom is √(2gh) when μ = 0 (4.43 m/s for 1.0 m). The dashed starting-height line from the still figure stays on
screen. The flat-track case must loop visibly rather than fake a stop.

**Visual language.** Same as the still: book colours, dashed start-height line, the puck in ink, a ghost puck at the
highest point reached. A small friction arrow on the puck, labelled, while it slides (hidden when μ = 0). No sound,
no sensors. Pause when off screen; reduced motion opens paused on the still frame.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D only, well under 150 KB.
