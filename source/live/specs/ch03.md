## c3_tug_cart → `w_forces` (Section 3.1, "Adding forces")

**What it teaches.** Forces on one object add with direction; the net force, and only the net force, changes the
motion. Balanced forces mean constant velocity, which may be non-zero.

**Controls (3).**
1. *Pull to the left* slider: 0 to 500 N, step 10, default 390.
2. *Pull to the right* slider: 0 to 500 N, step 10, default 390.
3. *Floor* tabs: "smooth" (no friction) / "rough" (kinetic friction 100 N against the motion, static friction up to
   100 N while at rest). Default smooth.

**Model.** Cart mass 50 kg (named in the readout). Net force = right − left (− friction when rough and moving, or
static friction cancelling the net pull up to 100 N while at rest). a = F_net / m; v and x integrate with a fixed
small step; the cart moves along a track that scrolls so it never leaves the stage. Worked check: 390 vs 390 → a = 0,
cart stays at whatever velocity it has; 390 vs 490 on the smooth floor → a = 100/50 = 2.0 m/s². Reset button inside
the stage (not counted as a control) returns the cart to rest.

**Readout.** "Net force 100 N to the right. The cart speeds up at 2.0 m/s²." / "Net force 0. The cart keeps its
velocity: 3.2 m/s to the right." (The second sentence is the point of the widget: once the cart is moving, equalising
the pulls must *not* stop it.) With the rough floor at rest and a small net pull: "Net pull 60 N, but static friction
cancels it. Nothing moves until the pull beats 100 N." Say "1 second on screen = 1 second" (real time; no exaggeration).

**Physics it must honour.** F = ma every frame; the velocity readout must match the integrated motion; static friction
never exceeds the applied net pull; kinetic friction opposes the velocity and cannot reverse it in one step (clamp to
rest). Arrow lengths for the two pulls, friction and the net force share one scale (the still uses 1 px per 3.9 N).

**Visual language.** Same cart, same force arrows (bold, labelled with newtons) as the still; the net-force arrow drawn
below the cart, labelled "net". A small velocity arrow (blue, labelled m/s) above the cart. No sound, no sensors.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D, well under 150 KB. Link out beside the figure to PhET "Forces and Motion: Basics" (CC BY 4.0
if released before 29 March 2026; verify in its About dialog, attribution next to the link).

## c3_momentum_carts → `w_momentum_carts` (Section 3.5, "Momentum and rockets")

**What it teaches.** In a collision the total momentum of the two carts is the same before and after, whatever the
masses and however bouncy the collision.

**Controls (3).**
1. *Mass of cart 1* slider: 0.10 to 1.00 kg, step 0.05, default 0.350.
2. *Mass of cart 2* slider: 0.10 to 1.00 kg, step 0.05, default 0.500.
3. *Collision* tabs: "bounce" (elastic) / "stick" (perfectly inelastic). Default bounce.
Initial velocities are fixed at +2.00 m/s (cart 1) and −0.50 m/s (cart 2), matching the OpenStax Physics 8.3 worked
example, so the default is a verified test case. A "Collide" button starts the run; "Again" resets.

**Model.** 1D, frictionless track. Elastic: v1' = ((m1 − m2)v1 + 2m2v2)/(m1 + m2), v2' = ((m2 − m1)v2 + 2m1v1)/(m1 + m2).
Inelastic: v' = (m1v1 + m2v2)/(m1 + m2). Test case (computed 2026-10-28): defaults, bounce → total momentum 0.45
kg·m/s before and after; v1' = −0.94 m/s, v2' = 1.56 m/s (matches the textbook's −0.94 and our 1.56). Defaults, stick →
v' = 0.45/0.85 = 0.53 m/s.

**Readout.** Before: "Total momentum 0.45 kg·m/s to the right (0.70 from cart 1, −0.25 from cart 2)." After: "Still
0.45 kg·m/s: cart 1 now −0.33, cart 2 now 0.78." Also, for a stick collision: "They move off together at 0.53 m/s."
Say "shown at half speed".

**Physics it must honour.** Momentum conserved to floating-point precision every frame, including during the contact
(treat the collision as instantaneous at touch); masses drawn with area proportional to mass; velocity arrows to one
scale; momentum arrows (a second, thinner pair) to another scale, both labelled. Energy is not conserved in the
stick case and the readout must not claim it is.

**Visual language.** The two carts of the still, in book colours; blue velocity arrows with m/s labels; a running
"total momentum" line under the track that does not change. No sound, no sensors.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D, well under 150 KB. Optional link-out to PhET "Collision Lab" with the same licence check.
