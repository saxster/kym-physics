# Live figure specs, Chapter 4 (Falling)

Written by the chapter writer for the widget agents. No `:::live` lines are in `src/ch04.md`; add them under the
figures named below when the widgets exist. Every widget: book colours, labelled forces and vectors, no autoplay,
pause off screen, reduced motion opens paused, chunk under 150 KB. Numbers come from the chapter's ledger
(`provenance/ch04.yml`, `computed:` lists).

## w_airfall — 4.2 Air gets in the way

- **Figure:** `c4_drag_balance` (still: raindrop at three moments plus the speed–time curve).
- **Teaches:** drag grows with speed until it equals weight; then the speed stops changing (terminal speed). Mass and
  area decide the terminal speed, not "lightness".
- **Controls (3):**
  1. *Air* slider, 0 to 1, shown as "no air" … "Mumbai air" (fraction of ρ = 1.225 kg/m³; the chapter's figures use
     this density).
  2. *Pair* tabs: "hammer and feather" (1.32 kg hammer; feather modelled as 1 g with 30 cm² facing area, C = 1.0,
     stated as a model value in the readout), "flat sheet and crumpled sheet" (both 5 g A4: flat 0.0624 m² with
     C = 1.1; crumpled as a 4 cm ball with C = 0.47), "2 mm and 4 mm raindrop" (4 mg/3.0 mm² and 33 mg/12.6 mm²,
     spheres, C = 0.5).
  3. *Drop* button (both objects released together from 1.6 m, the Apollo 15 height; the raindrop pair from 20 m
     so they reach terminal speed; the height is written on the stage).
- **Readout:** per object, speed now in m/s and "drag = N% of weight". When an object stops accelerating: "steady at
  6.5 m/s: drag now equals weight". With air at 0: "no air: they fall together, 0.57 s". Landing order and the gap
  in seconds between landings.
- **Physics to honour:** integrate a = g − ½CρAv²/m each frame (RK2 or better, dt ≤ 5 ms); g = 9.8 m/s²; the 2 mm
  raindrop must settle at 6.5 ± 0.1 m/s at air = 1 and the 4 mm drop at about 9.2 m/s (the model's number; the
  readout says "real drops this big flatten and break up, so about 10 m/s is the real ceiling"). Weight arrow fixed
  length; drag arrow length proportional to drag, in the force colour, labelled. Check every frame that drag never
  exceeds weight for a body released from rest, and that with air = 0 both land within 1 frame of each other.
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** time slowed 4× (readout: "slowed 4 times"); object sizes not to scale (said on the stage).
- **Sensors/audio:** none.

## w_projectile — 4.4 Thrown sideways

- **Figure:** `c4_two_coins` (still: dropped and flicked coins flashed every 0.1 s off a 75 cm table).
- **Teaches:** horizontal and vertical motions are independent; the flicked coin falls exactly like the dropped one;
  flight time depends only on height.
- **Controls (3):**
  1. *Sideways speed* slider, 0 to 4 m/s (0.5 m/s steps), applied to the second coin.
  2. *Launch angle* slider, −20° to +30° (default 0°, horizontal); the first coin is always simply dropped.
  3. *Launch* button: both coins leave the table edge at the same instant; a second press resets.
- **Readout:** "both in the air for 0.39 s" (with angle 0), landing distance of the flicked coin ("lands 0.78 m out at
  2 m/s"), and at each strobe flash a line "same height: yes" while the angle is 0. With an upward angle, the
  readout says the thrown coin lands later because it started upward, and reports both times. Dashed level lines
  join the two coins at each flash, as in the still.
- **Physics to honour:** x = v cos θ · t, y = v sin θ · t − ½ g t² with g = 9.8 m/s² and the table height 0.75 m;
  equal sideways steps every 0.1 s (strobe markers); landing time for θ = 0 is 0.391 s at every speed. Scale 240
  px/m matching the still so the strobe positions coincide with the drawing at 2 m/s. No air (the readout says so
  once: "no air in this model; a real long throw falls short").
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** time slowed 3× ("slowed 3 times"); coin size not to scale.
- **Sensors/audio:** a single soft click on each landing (api.audio tone, 40 ms), so a simultaneous landing sounds
  like one click; off by default, toggled with the launch button's long-press or a small "sound" checkbox if the
  control budget allows (otherwise omit).

## w_whirl — 4.5 Going round

- **Figure:** `c4_whirl` (still: marble in a paper plate with a gap).
- **Teaches:** the inward force does the turning; released, the object goes straight along the tangent at constant
  speed; the "outward force" exists only in the turning view.
- **Controls (3):**
  1. *Speed* slider, 1 to 4 m/s (ball on a 1 m string).
  2. *Release* button: lets go at the ball's current position; a second press resets to whirling.
  3. *View* tabs: "from the floor" (inertial) and "from the ball's seat" (frame rotating with the ball).
- **Readout:** while whirling: "the string pulls inward with F = m v²/r = … N; the velocity points along the circle".
  On release: "no inward pull now: straight line at the same speed, … m/s". In the turning view: "in this spinning
  view a pretend outward force of … N appears so that the rules still work; from the floor there is none", and the
  outward arrow is drawn dashed and labelled "pretend (only in this view)".
- **Physics to honour:** uniform circular motion with r = 1 m and the chosen v; the inward force arrow has length
  proportional to m v²/r (m = 0.2 kg shown); after release the path is the exact tangent at the release point and
  the speed is unchanged (check each frame: |v| constant to 0.1%). In the rotating view the released ball must
  trace the correct curved path (the inertial straight line transformed), not fly radially outward.
- **Slowed or exaggerated:** real time; the ball's size exaggerated.
- **Sensors/audio:** none.
