Three figures in this chapter are marked **Live** in BOOKPLAN §5. The chapter text has no `:::live` lines; the widget

## 10.3 `c10_mirror` → `w_mirror` (Canvas 2D)

**What she controls (3):** drag the object point anywhere in front of the mirror; drag the eye up or down along its
vertical line; a toggle "show the traced-back rays".

**What it shows:** the flat mirror as a vertical line; two or three rays from the object point that reflect into the
eye, each obeying the law of reflection at its own point on the mirror, with the normal drawn at one of them and the
two angles marked; the reflected rays traced back (dashed) to the image point behind the mirror. A faint "ghost" of the
object at the image. Scale bar: 1 px = 1 cm.

**Readout:** "Object 80 cm in front of the mirror. Image 80 cm behind it, 160 cm from the object. At the marked point
the ray arrives at 23° to the normal and leaves at 23°." When the eye is dragged: "Same image. Only the rays that reach
your eye have changed."

**Physics it must honour:** angle of reflection = angle of incidence, exactly, at every reflection point; image distance
equals object distance to the pixel; the traced-back rays meet at one point for every eye position; moving the object
sideways moves the image the same way, moving it towards the mirror moves the image towards the mirror by the same
amount (left–right reversal falls out of this). Nothing is exaggerated; nothing animates except the drag.

**Optional second tab, "whole body":** a 150 cm figure, a mirror whose top and bottom can be dragged, and a slider for
distance from the mirror. The readout says which part of the body is visible and that the required mirror height
(75 cm) does not change with distance (CP 25 Conceptual Q28). Static: no loop needed.

## 10.4 `c10_refraction` → `w_refraction` (Canvas 2D)

**What she controls (3):** angle slider (0°–89°) for the arriving ray; tabs for the second material (water 1.333 ·
glass 1.52 · diamond 2.419; air is 1.00); a direction toggle "air → material" / "material → air".

**What it shows:** the boundary with the normal drawn; the arriving ray, the refracted ray (Snell's law) and the partly
reflected ray; the angles labelled in degrees. While playing, a row of wavefronts marches along the ray: inside the
material their spacing shrinks by the factor *n* and they advance at *c*/*n*, so the swing of the beam is visible as the
line of crests turning. In the "material → air" direction, past the critical angle the refracted ray disappears and the
reflected ray becomes bright and labelled "total internal reflection".

**Readout:** "Air to water at 45°: the light slows to three-quarters of its speed and continues at 32.0° to the normal.
Some light reflects too." Past the critical angle: "Water to air at 50°: beyond 48.6° no light gets out. The surface
is a mirror from below."

**Physics it must honour:** n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ with the Table 25.1 indices; speed in the material *c*/*n*;
wavefront spacing λ/*n*; critical angle sin θ = n₂/n₁ (48.6° water, 41.1° glass, 24.4° diamond); the reflected ray at
the angle of incidence; path reversible (flipping the direction toggle at the same geometry gives the same line). The
time scale is slowed by about 10¹⁰ and the readout says "slowed enormously so you can see the crests".

**Optional second tab, "the coin in the cup":** the 8 cm cup of the Case, a fill-level slider and the eye fixed on the
45° sightline over the rim; the visible strip of the bottom grows from 0 to 3.0 cm as water is poured, computed by
tracing the ray (not the d/n shortcut). Readout: "Filled to the brim: the sightline bends to 32° and reaches 5.0 cm
from the near wall instead of 8.0 cm. The coin has not moved."

## 10.5 `c10_eye` → `w_eye3d` (three.js)

**What she controls (3):** a slider for the distance of the object (25 cm to "far away", logarithmic); tabs "ideal eye ·
short-sighted · long-sighted", each with a "glasses on/off" button that places the correcting lens in front; a toggle
"cover half the pupil".

**What it shows:** a cut-away eyeball she can turn by dragging (cornea, iris and pupil, lens, vitreous, retina), with a
small arrow object in front and rays from its top and bottom bending at the cornea and at the lens, meeting on the
retina (sharp) or in front of or behind it (blurred). The image on the retina is drawn upside down. As the object comes
nearer the lens visibly fattens (accommodation). With half the pupil covered, the image stays whole and dims.

**Readout:** "Object far away: cornea and lens together 50 dioptres, image sharp on the retina, upside down." At 25 cm:
"Lens fattened: 54 dioptres, 8% more. Still sharp." Short-sighted, far object: "Light meets 2 mm in front of the retina:
blurred. A diverging lens puts it back." Half pupil: "Same image, half the light."

**Physics it must honour:** total power 50 D far, 54 D at 25 cm (CP 26.1 Ex 26.2), with the cornea providing about
two-thirds of the bending and the lens the rest and all of the adjustment; lens-to-retina distance 2.00 cm; thin-lens
arithmetic 1/d_o + 1/d_i = P for where the rays meet; indices from Table 26.1 (cornea 1.38, aqueous 1.34, lens 1.41,
vitreous 1.34) if the ray bends are drawn per surface; the image inverted; glasses change the light and never the eye
(the eye's own power is unchanged when glasses go on). Exaggerated and named in the readout: the eye is drawn about
10 cm across, the bends are drawn at single surfaces, and the object arrow is scaled to fit the view.

**Fallback:** without WebGL the still SVG stays and a 2D cross-section with the same slider can be offered.
