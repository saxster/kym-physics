## 11.2 `c11_banana` → `w_colourmix` (Canvas 2D)

**What she controls (3):** three sliders for the light (red, green, blue, 0–100%); tabs "light on a white wall ·
paints on paper · an object under this light"; in the object tab, a chooser (banana · leaf · blue cloth · white kurta).

**What it shows:** *Light tab:* three overlapping discs of light on a wall whose overlaps show the additive mixes, with
every region labelled in words (red + green = yellow, all three = white). *Paints tab:* the sliders become cyan, magenta
and yellow paint; overlapping patches darken, labelled (cyan + yellow = green, all three = near black). *Object tab:* the
chosen object lit by the mixed light; its displayed colour is the product of the light and the object's reflectance.

**Readout:** "Red 100% + green 100% on a white wall: yellow. Both lights reach your eye, and your red and green cones
fire together, which is what yellow does." Object tab: "Banana under red light: it sends back the red (about 90%) and
there is no green or blue to send back. It looks red." White kurta under blue light: "It sends back nearly all of the
blue. It looks blue, although in daylight you call it white."

**Physics it must honour:** additive mixing by adding the three channels; subtractive mixing by multiplying
reflectances (cyan passes blue and green, absorbs red; yellow passes red and green, absorbs blue; magenta passes red
and blue); object colour = light × reflectance per channel, with reflectances declared in the readout as a three-channel
model (banana R .9 G .8 B .1; leaf R .2 G .6 B .1; blue cloth R .1 G .2 B .8; kurta .95 each). It must never show a
"pure" red object under pure blue light as anything but black, and must say that a real phone screen's red is not pure.
Nothing animates; `static: true`.

## 11.3 `c11_sky` → `w_skycolour` (WebGL fragment shader via `api.gl`)

**What she controls (3):** Sun height slider (−2° to 90°); tabs "Earth · thin air · the Moon (no air)"; drag to look
around the sky dome (optional; otherwise a fixed wide view with the horizon).

**What it shows:** a sky dome from a Mumbai sea wall (dark sea and a thin wall silhouette at the bottom), rendered by
single-scattering: for each pixel's view direction, integrate the scattered sunlight along the ray through an
atmosphere with real-ratio wavelength dependence. The Sun's disc itself takes the colour of the direct beam after its
path through the air. On the Moon tab the sky is black and the Sun white. A small inset shows the geometry from the
side: the Sun's ray, the air layer, and the path length, with "air layer drawn far thicker than it is".

**Readout:** "Sun 10° above the horizon: its light crosses about 6 times as much air as at noon. Blue is scattered
about 6 times more than red, so most of it has left the beam: the Sun looks orange and the sky near it yellow; overhead
is still blue." Moon tab: "No air, so nothing scatters the sunlight towards you. The sky is black and the Sun white."

**Physics it must honour:** scattering coefficient ∝ 1/λ⁴ evaluated at three wavelengths (red 700, green 530, blue
450 nm), so blue scatters 5.86× red and green about 3× red; the optical path computed with a curved Earth (not the flat
1/sin(elevation), which overshoots near the horizon); direct-beam colour = exp(−τ) per wavelength; sky colour = the
single-scattering integral along the view ray. Exposure and tone-mapping are display choices and the readout names them.
Partial polarisation of skylight (CP 27.8) is optional: a "polariser" angle control may dim the sky about 90° from the
Sun, but that would be a fourth control, so leave it out unless a control is dropped.

**Fallback:** `api.gl`'s 2D fallback draws a vertical gradient computed on the CPU at 64 samples with the same model.

## 11.5 `c11_young` → `w_interference` (Canvas 2D, or `api.gl` for the ripple view)

**What she controls (3):** source spacing slider (0.1–1.0 mm); wavelength slider (400–700 nm, with the screen's
approximate colour); tabs "ripple tank · stripes on a screen".

**What it shows:** *Ripple tank:* two sources in step sending out circular waves, crests light and troughs dark, the
pattern of doubled and cancelled places visible as lines fanning out; slowed enormously (the readout says so). *Screen
tab:* the intensity pattern on a screen 1.00 m from the sources, bright and dark stripes with their spacing marked, and
the two path lengths to the pointer position drawn, with their difference in wavelengths.

**Readout:** "Sources 0.5 mm apart, light 550 nm, screen 1 m away: stripes 1.1 mm apart. At the pointer the two paths
differ by 1.0 wavelengths: crest meets crest, bright." Or "…by 1.5 wavelengths: crest meets trough, dark."

**Physics it must honour:** superposition of two coherent point sources; bright where the path difference is a whole
number of wavelengths (d sin θ = mλ), dark at half-integers; stripe spacing λL/d in the small-angle limit, with the exact
angles used for drawing; the ripple-tank wavelength and spacing in the same units so the fan of bright lines matches the
screen tab (spacing/wavelength ratio real: at d = 0.5 mm and λ = 550 nm the ratio is about 900, so the tank view must
zoom to a region a few wavelengths across near the sources or display a reduced ratio and *say so* in the readout).
Time slowed by about 10¹⁴ in the ripple view, named in the readout. Shorter wavelength → narrower stripes; wider spacing
→ narrower stripes.
