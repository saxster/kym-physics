## c12_potential_hill → `w_circuit` (Section 12.3, "What gets used up?")

**What it teaches.** The current is the same at every point of a loop, inside the battery included; what is divided up
round the loop is the energy per coulomb (the volts); energy is delivered where the loop is hard to push through, and
the whole loop starts moving at once while each charge only creeps.

**Scene.** Canvas 2D, 640 × 360, two views stacked or side by side depending on width. *Top:* the circuit as the still
draws it, unrolled: battery block at the left, a lead, the 330 Ω resistor, a lead, one to three red LEDs, a lead back.
Beads (the charge) move along the loop, all at the same speed, and three counters at marked points ("leaving +",
"after the resistor", "returning to −") count beads passing per second: the three counts are always equal. *Bottom:*
the rope-loop model from the text: a ring of hands, one pair labelled "battery" hauling the rope, one pair per LED
labelled "grips", the rope moving at a speed proportional to the current; the gripping hands glow warm in proportion to
the power delivered there. The height profile from the still (volts as height) sits above the top view and updates with
the number of LEDs: battery up 9 V, resistor down (9 − 2n) V, each LED down 2 V.

**Controls (3).**
1. *LEDs in the loop* tabs: 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 (default 1). Four LEDs gives 3 mA and a dim glow; the readout says so.
2. *Battery* slider: 1.5 V to 9 V in 1.5 V steps, default 9 V (a 2 × AA setting at 3 V shows one red LED lit
   dimly through the 330 Ω and two red LEDs dark, which is the series-LED fact behind the chapter's case).
3. *Run / Pause* button (also reset). No autoplay.

**Model.** I = max(0, (V − 2.0·n)) / 330 (red LED forward voltage 2.0 V typical, treated as fixed with current; say so
in the readout: "a real LED's 2 V shifts a little with current"). Bead speed on screen ∝ I, with 21 mA mapped to about
60 px/s; counters at the three points sample the same bead stream, so equality is built in, and the counters must show
identical integers every second. Power: P_battery = V·I; P_LED = 2.0·I each; P_resistor = I²·330; assert
P_battery = ΣP_LED + P_resistor every frame (floating tolerance 1e-9 W) and show the three numbers. Worked values:
9 V, 1 LED: 21.2 mA, 0.191 W = 0.042 + 0.148. 9 V, 2 LEDs: 15.2 mA, 0.136 W = 0.030 + 0.030 + 0.076. 9 V, 3 LEDs:
9.1 mA. 9 V, 4 LEDs: 3.0 mA. 3 V, 1 LED: 3.0 mA. 3 V, 2 LEDs: 0 mA (dark; "two red LEDs need about 4 V").
LED brightness ∝ P_LED (not I²), clamped to a visible minimum glow above 1 mA and off below.

**Readout.** Two sentences, updated live, for example: "Same current everywhere: 21 mA leaving the battery, 21 mA after
the resistor, 21 mA coming back. The battery delivers 0.19 W; the LED hands over 0.04 W as light and warmth, the
resistor 0.15 W as warmth." Then the exaggeration line: "Beads are drawn moving about ten million times faster than
real electrons creep (about 8 µm/s at 21 mA in half-millimetre wire); the push that starts them all moving is not
drawn, because at 10⁸ m/s it would be instant." With 0 mA: "No push to spare: 3 V is less than two red LEDs' 4 V, so
nothing flows and nothing lights."

**Physics it must honour.** Equal counts at all three points, always. Energy conservation checked every frame, as
above. Beads are never created or destroyed: a fixed population circulates. Brightness ∝ power delivered at that LED;
all LEDs in a given loop equally bright. Swapping LED and resistor order (not a control; fixed layout) would change
nothing, and the readout may say so once. No bucket model: beads do not carry "energy packets" in the drawing; the
warmth appears at the gripping hands and the LEDs only.

**Visual language.** Book colours; the force-red arrow colour for the current labels as in the still; the volts profile
in the vector blue; beads in ink. Rope hands as simple outlines. No sound, no sensors. Pause when off screen; reduced
motion opens paused on the still frame with the counters showing equal totals.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D only, well under 150 KB.

## c12_series_parallel → `w_seriesparallel` (Section 12.5, "Series and parallel")

**What it teaches.** Series: one path, same current, the push divided; parallel: each branch has the full push and its
own current, the currents add at the battery, and a broken branch leaves the others alone. The MIT PRS10 brightness
results: a second bulb in parallel leaves each as bright and doubles the battery current; a second bulb in series halves
the current and gives each a quarter of the power (for ideal resistors; "much dimmer" for real bulbs).

**Scene.** Canvas 2D, 640 × 360. The two circuits from the still, drawn with the same symbols, one at a time (tab).
Each LED has its own 330 Ω in the parallel view; one shared 330 Ω in the series view, exactly as the home box builds
them. Current arrows at each marked point carry live labels; a small meter beside the battery shows its current and
"battery runs down ×1 / ×2 / ×3 as fast".

**Controls (3).**
1. *Series / Parallel* tabs.
2. *LEDs* slider: 1 to 3 (default 2).
3. *Unclip LED 1* toggle button (restores on second press). No autoplay; current arrows animate only while the
   figure is open and visible.

**Model.** Ideal battery V = 9 V (state it: "a real battery sags a little when it supplies more current, Section
12.4"). Series with n LEDs and one 330 Ω: I = max(0, 9 − 2n)/330, the same at every point; volts: 2 V per LED, the
rest across the resistor. Parallel with n branches each 330 Ω + LED: I_branch = (9 − 2)/330 = 21.2 mA each, I_battery
= n × 21.2 mA; 9 V across each branch. Unclip LED 1: series → every current 0, all dark; parallel → that branch 0, the
others unchanged, battery current drops by 21.2 mA. Power per LED = 2.0 × I_LED; show it to make the "¼ as bright"
comparison honest: series 1 LED 0.042 W, 2 LEDs 0.030 W each (so each gets about 72% of the power, because the shared
resistor takes a smaller share with LEDs than an ideal-bulb pair would; the readout must say the exact numbers and
*not* claim ¼ for this circuit). Ideal-bulb mode is not needed; keep the real kit.

**Readout.** For example, parallel, 2 LEDs: "Each branch has the full 9 V and carries 21 mA, so the battery supplies
42 mA and runs down twice as fast. Unclip one LED and the other does not change." Series, 2 LEDs: "One path: 15 mA at
every point. Each LED has 2 V; the resistor takes the other 5 V. Unclip either LED and the only path is broken." After
an unclip in series: "0 mA everywhere: a gap anywhere stops the whole loop."

**Physics it must honour.** Kirchhoff bookkeeping every frame: in series the current labels are identical; in parallel
the branch currents sum to the battery current to the milliamp shown; the volts round any closed path sum to zero (2n +
V_resistor = 9 in series; 2 + 7 = 9 in each parallel branch). Charge conservation at the fork: arrows into a junction
equal arrows out. No current flows in a broken branch, and nothing else changes when a parallel branch is broken.

**Visual language.** Same symbols and colours as the still (c12_series_parallel), current labels in the force red,
"9 V across each branch" in ink. No sound, no sensors.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D only, well under 150 KB.

## Not made live (deliberately)

- c12_rubbing: the counting argument is static by design. PH 18.1 links the PhET "Balloons and Static Electricity"
  sim for anyone who wants to drag charges about; link out with the PhET attribution rather than build our own.
- c12_loop: a one-wire vs loop toggle would duplicate the first tab of `w_circuit` at 0 mA; the still carries it.
- c12_iv_graph: the LED curve is schematic (one datasheet point), so animating it would overstate what we know. The
  PhET "Ohm's Law" sim covers the resistor line; link out.
- c12_earth_wire: a safety diagram; animating a fault current through a person is not something this book wants to
  make playful.
