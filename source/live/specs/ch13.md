## c13_wire_field → `w_field3d` (Section 13.3, "Electricity makes magnetism") — three.js

**What it teaches.** The field of a straight current is circles round the wire, weaker farther out, with a direction
that follows the current (right-hand rule); a coil's field is a bar magnet's field; a compass near the wire swings by an
amount set by the ratio of the wire's field to the Earth's horizontal field.

**Scene.** three.js via `three_kit.js` (`makeStage`, drag to turn, Turn buttons, gentle spin while playing). Two tabs:
*Wire* shows a vertical wire with 5–6 field-line circles at radii 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 cm (scene units), each with small
arrowheads showing the direction, plus a flat compass disc lying 1 cm below the wire with a needle that points along the
vector sum of Mumbai's horizontal field (38.4 μT north) and the wire's field; *Coil and magnet* shows a 10-turn coil and,
beside it, a bar magnet, both with their field lines drawn from the same dipole-like family so the reader sees the same
shape. Field lines are tubes in the vector-blue colour; current arrows in ink; the compass needle red-north as in the
still.

**Controls (3).**
1. *Current* slider: 0 to 3 A, step 0.1 A, default 1 A (Wire tab); on the Coil tab it scales the coil's field and the
   magnet is fixed.
2. *Reverse current* button (flips the sign; arrows and needle respond).
3. *Wire · Coil and magnet* tabs. No autoplay; the stage opens on the still frame and spins only when playing.

**Physics it must honour.** B = μ₀I/(2πr) exactly for the wire: at 1 A, 20.0 μT at 1 cm, 10.0 at 2 cm, 6.7 at 3 cm,
5.0 at 4 cm, 3.3 at 6 cm. Field-line spacing or tube brightness must fall as 1/r (say in the readout which encoding is
used). Direction: anticlockwise seen from above for current upward (right-hand rule), reversed with the current. The
compass needle angle from north is atan(B_wire/H) with H = 38.4 μT and the wire running north–south 1 cm above the
needle: 1 A → 27.5°, 1.92 A → 45°, 2 A → 46°, 3 A → 57.4°; 0 A → 0°. Field lines are closed curves; nothing moves
along them (no "flow" particles). The coil's field must have the same topology as the magnet's (closed loops through
the core), and the readout must state that the coil's and the magnet's lines are drawn with the same rule.

**Readout.** One or two sentences, for example: "1.0 A: the wire's field is 20 μT at 1 cm, 10 μT at 2 cm, half as much
for twice the distance. Under a north–south wire the compass swings 27° east of north." After *Reverse*: "Same size,
opposite way round: the needle swings 27° west." Exaggeration line: "Field lines are a drawing of the direction a compass
would point; the real field fills all the space between them." Coil tab: "A coil of current has a bar magnet's field:
one end north, one end south, closed loops through the middle."

**Visual language.** Book colours and themes; labelled arrows; the compass disc as in the still. No sound, no sensors.
Pause when off screen; reduced motion opens paused, no spin.

**Budget.** three.js is the shared chunk; this widget's own chunk well under 150 KB. `webglOK` fallback: the still.

## c13_induction → `w_induction` (Section 13.5, "Magnetism makes electricity") — Canvas 2D

**What it teaches.** A current appears only while the field through the coil is changing; opposite motion gives the
opposite current; faster motion gives more; more turns give proportionally more; the induced current opposes the change
(Lenz). The decisive frame is the magnet held still inside the coil: zero.

**Scene.** The still's layout: a horizontal coil, a bar magnet the reader drags left and right along the axis with the
pointer (or arrow keys), and a centre-zero meter above. The meter needle shows the induced voltage, proportional to the
rate of change of flux through the coil, which the widget computes from the magnet's position and velocity using a
dipole model: flux Φ(x) through the coil as a smooth bell-shaped function of the magnet's centre position x (dipole on
axis; use Φ(x) ∝ 1/(1 + (x/a)²)^{3/2} with a = coil radius as the shape), ε = −N dΦ/dt by finite difference each
frame. A second trace under the meter draws ε against time for the last 4 s so the reader sees the two opposite pulses
of an in-and-out stroke and the flat zero while the magnet rests.

**Controls (3).**
1. Drag the magnet (pointer or arrow keys); a *Nudge in / Nudge out* pair of buttons for keyboard users moves it at a
   fixed speed for 0.5 s.
2. *Turns* slider: 1, 10, 50, 200 (default 50); the meter scale rescales so that the trace stays readable, and the
   readout gives the ratio ("200 turns: 4 times the 50-turn kick").
3. *Flip magnet* button (swap N and S): the same motion now gives the opposite kick.

**Physics it must honour.** ε = 0 whenever the magnet is at rest, wherever it is, including fully inside the coil (the
widget must not fake a residual reading). ε ∝ speed for the same position (a drag at double speed gives double the peak;
check numerically). ε ∝ N exactly. Reversing the motion or flipping the magnet reverses the sign. The time integral of
ε over a complete in-and-out stroke is zero (the two pulses cancel): assert this on the trace. Lenz: show a small arrow
on the coil marking the induced current's direction, and the readout says "the induced current pushes back on the
approaching magnet / pulls back on the leaving magnet". Nothing lights: the readout should say that a hand-moved fridge
magnet will not light an LED, so the picture uses a sensitive meter.

**Readout.** For example: "Moving in at 0.2 m/s: the needle kicks right. Hold still: zero, however strong the magnet.
Moving out: the needle kicks left." And the exaggeration line: "The meter responds instantly here; a real needle lags a
little. Faraday's needle in 1831 did the same two kicks, one at switch-on and one at switch-off."

**Visual language.** Same symbols as the still (coil of arcs, N/S magnet, meter). No sound, no sensors.

**Budget.** Canvas 2D only, well under 150 KB.

## c13_earth_field → `w_magnetometer` (Section 13.2, optional sensor lab)

**What it teaches.** The Earth's field at Mumbai is about 44 μT total, dipping about 28°; a compass shows only the
horizontal part; a fridge magnet a hand's breadth away swamps it.

**Sensor.** `api.motion` does not expose the magnetometer. This widget needs the Generic Sensor API `Magnetometer`
(Chrome on Android, behind the "Generic Sensor Extra Classes" flag on some versions; permission-gated), read at about
10 Hz, giving x, y, z in μT in the device frame. Where the API is missing (iOS Safari, desktop), the button says
"Magnetometer not available here" and points to the free phyphox app's "Magnetometer" experiment, as `api.motion` does
for motion. Nothing leaves the device; readings are not stored.

**Controls (≤ 3).**
1. *Use the magnetometer* button (permission prompt on press; stops when the figure closes or scrolls off screen).
2. *Hold* button: freezes the current reading so the reader can write it down.
3. (none else) A text line reminds the reader to lay the phone flat on a wooden or plastic surface away from steel.

**Readout and physics it must honour.** Show the total |B| = √(x² + y² + z²) in μT, the horizontal part √(x² + y²) and
the vertical part z, and the dip angle atan2(|z|, horizontal), each rounded to the nearest μT or degree. Beside them,
show the WMM2025 expectation for Mumbai as fixed reference text: total 43.6 μT, horizontal 38.4, down 20.7, dip 28°.
State plainly that phone magnetometers are uncalibrated and that steel nearby (table legs, the phone's own parts) shifts
the reading, so agreement to within about 10 μT is good. Do not compute declination (the phone's heading is not reliable
enough) and do not call the reading a measurement of the Earth alone. If |B| exceeds about 150 μT, say "a magnet or
steel is nearby" rather than reporting it as the Earth's field. Device-frame to ground-frame conversion is not needed
because the three quantities shown are rotation-invariant or depend only on the phone lying flat (say so).

**Visual language.** A simple three-bar display (total, horizontal, down) in book colours with the WMM values as faint
reference marks, plus the dip angle drawn as in the still's right panel.

**Budget.** Tiny; no three.js. The still figure stays as the first view and the no-JavaScript view.
