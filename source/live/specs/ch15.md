# Live figure specs, Chapter 15 (Atoms, stars and the universe)

No `:::live` lines are in `src/ch15.md`; these specs are for the widget builders. Still SVGs stay as the first view.
Book colours and theme classes only; nothing moves until the reader taps; pause off screen; reduced motion opens
paused; at most three controls each. Both may later live as tabs of The Sky explorer (`#explore-sky`), in which case the
figure buttons open the explorer at the matching tab.

## Figure c15_parallax → `w_parallax`

- **Idea taught (15.5):** parallax is the half-shift of a nearby star against far stars as the Earth moves round its
  orbit; the farther the star, the smaller the angle; distance in parsecs = 1 / parallax in arcseconds.
- **Controls (3):** a *distance* slider for the nearby star (1 to 100 parsecs, log scale, default 1.3 pc, Proxima); a
  *month* slider or play button that moves the Earth round its orbit (January to December); a *view* toggle between
  "from above the orbit" (the geometry of the still figure) and "through the telescope" (the near star drifting against
  the fixed far stars as the months pass, a small ellipse whose width is twice the parallax).
- **Picture:** the still figure's geometry, with the sight line redrawn every month; in telescope view, a star field with
  the near star tracing its yearly loop, the far stars fixed, and a scale bar in arcseconds.
- **Readout:** "Distance 1.3 pc (4.25 light-years). Parallax 0.77 arcseconds: a ₹1 coin seen from about 6 km. Over six
  months the star shifts by twice that." At 100 pc: "Parallax 0.01 arcseconds. With errors of 0.001″ this is where the
  method stops being good to 10%."
- **Physics it must honour:** p = 1/d exactly (arcseconds and parsecs); the baseline is 1 AU for p, 2 AU for the full
  shift; the loop in telescope view is an ellipse whose shape depends on the star's direction (keep it a circle at the
  pole of the orbit and say so, or draw the general ellipse correctly); the ₹1-coin comparison uses 22 mm and
  206,265 arcseconds per radian (0.022 × 206,265 / p metres).
- **Exaggerated or slowed:** in the "from above" view the angle is drawn thousands of times too large to be visible, and
  the readout says so and gives the true angle. In telescope view the shift is to scale against the printed scale bar.
- **No sensors or audio.** Optional stretch (from MIT 8.282J PS10 Problem 5): a second tab "be Leavitt" plotting Cepheid
  period against apparent brightness for her 1912 Small Magellanic Cloud stars; the data must come from the 1912
  Harvard Circular (public domain), not from the OCW problem set's image.

## Figure c15_ruler → `w_expansion`

- **Idea taught (15.7):** space stretching between galaxies gives every observer the same law, speed proportional to
  distance, with no centre; galaxies themselves do not grow; light waves crossing stretching space are stretched.
- **Controls (3):** a *stretch* slider (scale factor 1.0 to 3.0, default 1.0, with a play button that doubles the ruler
  in one minute of readout time); an *observer* menu choosing which ant (galaxy) is "me" (the 2 cm ant by default; the
  7 cm and 12 cm ants as alternatives); a *mode* toggle between "ruler" (one dimension, the still figure) and "balloon"
  (a circle of dots, the raisin-bread picture in two dimensions, with one short wave drawn between two dots).
- **Picture:** ruler mode: the ruler lengthening at the same pixels-per-centimetre, ants riding with it, arrows from
  "me" to each other ant labelled with the measured recession speed. Balloon mode: dots on a growing circle, each dot
  the same size throughout, the drawn wave lengthening with the circle.
- **Readout:** "Seen from the ant at 2 cm: the others recede at 2, 5 and 10 cm per minute, in proportion to their
  distance. None of the ants moved; the ruler stretched. Choose another ant: the same law." In balloon mode: "The dots do
  not grow (gravity holds a galaxy together). The wave has stretched by the same factor as the balloon: that is the
  redshift." With a stretch factor s: "wavelength × s; redshift z = s − 1."
- **Physics it must honour:** positions scale exactly with the factor (x → s·x), so speed = (s−1)/t × original distance
  from the observer, identical in form for every observer; dots and ants keep constant size; the wave's wavelength scales
  by s; z = Δλ/λ₀ = s − 1 is shown numerically. A line "H ≈ 22 km/s per million light-years" connects the toy to the
  real value without claiming the toy's time scale is real.
- **Exaggerated or slowed:** the stretch happens in a minute, which is a cartoon of billions of years; say so. State
  AS's warning in the readout: the ruler and balloon have edges and a middle, the universe has neither.
- **No sensors or audio.**
