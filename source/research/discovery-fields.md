## Grounding and source record

- Circuit current conservation, series/parallel paths, resistor voltage and power use [grounding/ch12.md](../grounding/ch12.md), especially 12.3–12.4, and MIT OCW [8.02 circuit notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/chap7dc_circuits/). The code reuses `circuit` and `diodeOperatingPoint`. The LED's exponential curve is the existing model; the design challenge explicitly switches to a stated 2.0 V fixed-drop approximation. A separate 10 Ω series resistance is declared. For a 6 V source, the 20 mA design limit requires `(6−2)/0.02−10 = 190 Ω` in addition to those 10 Ω. This is virtual work, not instructions to connect a real meter or battery.
- The useful-device activity applies the same divider law. The LDR law is an invented, declared teaching example: `R = 10000(100/lux)^0.7 Ω`. The NTC example uses `R = 10000 exp[3950(1/T−1/298.15)] Ω`, with absolute temperature in kelvin. [Vishay's thermistor datasheet](https://www.vishay.com/docs/29049/ntcle100.pdf) grounds the NTC/B-parameter component convention; the chosen 10 kΩ/3950 K combination is a teaching assumption, not a claim about a particular listed part. At 25 °C it gives 10000 Ω, at 50 °C 3588.182582 Ω. At 20 lux the LDR gives 30851.693136 Ω. An unloaded divider reaches half its 5 V supply when both resistances are equal. Swapping the sensor reverses the response. An ideal comparator without hysteresis is intentional and disclosed.
- Motor and generator mechanisms use [grounding/ch13.md](../grounding/ch13.md), 13.4–13.5, and existing `motor`/`generator` primitives. The relevant [MIT magnetic-force notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/mit8_02s07_chap08/) and [generator account](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-5-electric-generators) are linked. The paused motor has no commutator: torque can reverse. For N=10, B=0.5 T, I=2 A, A=0.04 m² and angle=90°, wire force is 2 N and torque is 0.4 N m. Turning at 2 rad/s produces 0.4 V. An open generator supplies no current and hence no magnetic output force/power in this case. The speaker has the same `NBLI` force link and a declared static spring constant of 20 N/m; it is not a dynamic acoustic model. Current reversal reverses displacement. No audio, clock or animation is started here.
- The unknown-star case extends [grounding/ch15.md](../grounding/ch15.md), especially its graphics-fidelity note. Wavelengths were checked against NIST primary tables for [hydrogen](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/hydrogentable2.htm), [helium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/heliumtable2.htm) and [sodium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/sodiumtable2.htm), accessed 15 November 2026. The selected air wavelengths are rounded to 0.001 nm. Hydrogen: 410.174, 434.046, 486.134, 656.281 nm; helium: 447.148, 501.568, 587.562, 667.815 nm; sodium: 588.995, 589.592 nm. These laboratory positions are used as synthetic absorption signatures; the simulation does not pretend to fit a stellar atmosphere. Inverse Doppler inference uses a common fractional line shift and the existing exact c constant (also documented by [BIPM](https://www.bipm.org/en/measurement-units/si-defining-constants)). The maximum 300 km/s is about 0.001c. At +299.792458 km/s, a 588.995 nm line appears at 589.583995 nm. The displayed normalised Planck continuum and Wien relation give a 289.7771955 nm peak at 10000 K. The existing Saha–Boltzmann proxy affects illustrative line depth, not an abundance inference. Air/vacuum conversion, reddening, blends and real atmosphere structure are omitted.
- The planet case adds geometry to the existing perturbation lesson. [NASA's methods account](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/how-we-find-and-characterize/) grounds the two distinct observations: blocked starlight and line shifts caused by stellar radial motion. The geometry and numbers here are independently calculated rather than copied from another application: a circular relative orbit at ten stellar radii, one solar-mass/radius star, and a dark planet. Projected separation is `10 sqrt(sin²φ + cos²φ cos²i)` in stellar radii. Only the foreground half of the orbit can block light. Exact circle-overlap area handles grazing transits; a central full transit of radius ratio 0.1 blocks 0.01 of a uniform star. Inclination 80° gives a closest separation of 1.73648 stellar radii and no transit for that planet, while its radial-velocity signal persists. Inclination 0° removes both signals. With G=6.67430e−11, Mstar=1.98847e30 kg, Rstar=6.957e8 m and Mplanet=1.89813e27 kg, the chosen orbit has period 3.6612515162 days and edge-on stellar velocity amplitude 131.7804872143 m/s. These constants are stated model inputs, not new precision measurements. The stellar-face inset is labelled ×5. No limb darkening, instrumental sensitivity or time-window selection is simulated; the prose names their relevance instead of claiming a null observation excludes a planet.
- The decay activity reuses `decay` and `decayEnsembles`, grounded in [grounding/ch15.md](../grounding/ch15.md) and the [half-life account](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/31-5-half-life-and-activity). It uses independent seeded inverse-exponential lifetime samples, with separate seeds for three ensembles. Expected fraction is `2^(−t/H)`; activity is `(ln2/H)Nexpected`, a rate rather than a count of remaining nuclei. At N0=1000, H=6 s and t=6 s, expectation is 500 nuclei and activity 57.76226505 decays/s. A separate deterministic detector sample accepts each decay with probability 0.4, then adds Poisson background. The displayed one-second count is distinct from instantaneous expected count rate. Model seconds do not imply an isotope or a dose. Comparison retention belongs to the notebook, not hidden module state.
- The common-distance trail uses [grounding/ch04.md](../grounding/ch04.md), the point-charge account in the [MIT electric-field notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/mit8_02s07_chap02/), and [NASA's electromagnetic account](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter6-4/). It normalises each different physical quantity to its own value at 1 m. At 2 m the point-source values are 0.25; at 3 m they are 1/9. The constant-area beam is an explicitly finite-range ideal counterexample. Spherical energy flow explains the light-intensity relation; it is not presented as energy flow explaining static gravity or electricity.
