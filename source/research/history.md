# Kym's Physics Book: Scenes, Cast and True Stories (research report)

I did this in web search mode: about 75 searches, plus a few result summaries that quoted page text. Every URL below appeared in my search results. I did not open each page in full, so primary-source spot-checks are still worth doing where I flag them.

**Labels:** TRUE = documented · HEDGE = plausible, tell it as "the story goes" · LEGEND = doubtful or false, with what really happened.

---

## 1. Motion and force

- **Alexandria, 6th c. CE: John Philoponus.** Aristotle said heavier things fall faster, in proportion to their weight. Philoponus wrote that if you drop two very different weights, the difference in fall time is tiny, and he argued that motion through a vacuum is possible. His own theory still had errors. He is the first "confused, then partly right" figure. TRUE. https://www.vaticanobservatory.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Impetus-Philoponus.pdf · https://www.encyclopedia.com/topic/John_Philoponus.aspx
- **Bukhara / Paris, 11th–14th c.: Ibn Sina (*mayl*) and Jean Buridan (*impetus*).** Why does an arrow keep flying after it leaves the bow? Ibn Sina discussed Philoponus's idea in *The Book of Healing* and proposed *mayl* (inclination). Buridan named the property "impetus" and said it grows with speed and quantity of matter. It is a forerunner of momentum, but not the same idea. Latin scholars probably did not read Philoponus directly. TRUE, though I could not reach the SEP entry; the details come from encyclopedia sources. https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/impetus · https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2004/entries/causation-medieval/
- **Delft, 1586: Simon Stevin and Jan Cornets de Groot.** They dropped two lead balls, one ten times heavier than the other, about 30 ft from the Nieuwe Kerk tower onto a wooden board. They heard a single thud. Stevin published it in 1586, three years before Galileo reached Pisa. The real version of the Pisa story. TRUE (some later embellishments are exaggerated). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_tower_experiment
- **Pisa, c. 1589–92: Galileo and the Leaning Tower.** The only source is his late-life secretary Viviani. Galileo never describes the tower himself. Most historians think it was a thought experiment or at most a private test. LEGEND/HEDGE. Use Stevin for the real drop. https://physicsworld.com/a/the-legend-of-the-leaning-tower/
- **Pisa Cathedral, c. 1583: Galileo and the swinging lamp.** This also comes only from Viviani, with no date. The lamp now hanging there was installed after Galileo left Pisa. LEGEND as usually told; the pendulum work itself is real. https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/itineraries/place/CathedralOfSantaMaria.html
- **Padua, c. 1604: Galileo's inclined planes.** Falling is too fast to time, so he slowed it down with ramps. Stillman Drake's reconstruction has Galileo singing a steady beat while the ball clicked over lute-style gut "frets" that he moved until the clicks were evenly spaced. HEDGE (Drake's interpretation). The ramps are TRUE. https://www.teacherplus.org/?p=16841
- **Venice and Padua, 1609–10: Galileo's telescope.** He heard of the Dutch spyglass in May 1609. On 25 August 1609 he showed an 8–9× telescope to the Doge from the San Marco campanile and got a pay rise. On 7 January 1610 he saw three "stars" beside Jupiter, and by 15 January he realised they were moons. *Sidereus Nuncius* came out on 13 March 1610. TRUE. https://early-astronomy.web.itd.umich.edu/western_telescope.php · https://www.astronomy.com/today-in-the-history-of-astronomy/jan-7-1610-galileo-sees-four-moons-of-jupiter
- **Woolsthorpe, 1666: Newton's apple.** Newton himself told William Stukeley in 1726, about 60 years later: "occasion'd by the fall of an apple, as he sat in contemplative mood." There really was an apple tree. It did not hit his head. The Royal Society librarian calls it "an 18th-century sound bite." HEDGE. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna34944738
- **Cambridge, August 1684 → London, 1687: Halley and the *Principia*.** Halley asked Newton what orbit an inverse-square force would produce. Newton said an ellipse; he had worked it out but could not find the paper. Halley pushed him to write it up. The Royal Society had spent its money on Willughby's *History of Fishes*, which did not sell and was partly used to pay Halley's salary, so Halley paid to print the *Principia* himself. TRUE. https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Halley.html · https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/courses/m309-01a/wang/history.htm
- **Leiden, 1722 → Cirey, 1740: 's Gravesande, Émilie du Châtelet and vis viva.** Is the "force" of motion mv or mv²? 's Gravesande dropped brass balls into soft clay. A ball moving twice as fast made a dent four times as deep. Du Châtelet built this into her *Institutions de physique* (1740) on Leibniz's side. Do not say she "proved" it. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_%27s_Gravesande · https://spark.iop.org/history-concept-energy-and-work
- **Descartes:** I found no scene worth using. He can appear in the vis viva argument as the "mv" side.

## 2. Gravity and orbits

- **Kusumapura (Patna), 499 CE: Aryabhata.** He said the stars seem to move westward because the Earth turns, like a person in a boat who sees the shore slide backwards. He explained eclipses as shadows, not demons. Some scholars date the text nearer 510. Avoid the "heliocentric" and "99.4% accurate" claims you will see online. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhatiya · https://www.schoolsobservatory.org/learn/history/biographies/aryabhata
- **Ujjain region, 628 CE: Brahmagupta's "attraction."** Al-Biruni (c. 1030) reports Brahmagupta saying that heavy things fall to the Earth by a law of nature, because it is the Earth's nature to attract them, as it is water's nature to flow. That is a qualitative idea of attraction. It has no inverse-square law, no link to orbits and no mathematics. HEDGE. Use it as a true idea that went nowhere for 1,000 years, not as "India discovered gravity first." I could not see Sachau's exact wording, so check his *Alberuni's India* vol. 1 before quoting. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17600 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_(Al-Biruni)
- **Ujjain, c. 1150: Bhaskara II.** He headed the Ujjain observatory and wrote the *Siddhanta Shiromani*, whose *Goladhyaya* covers the sphere, the Earth and instruments. The "Bhaskara's gravity" verse appears only on non-scholarly sites. TRUE for his life; HEDGE for the gravity claim. https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Bhaskara_II
- **Hven, 1576–97 → Prague, 1600–01: Tycho and Kepler.** Tycho built Uraniborg on an island and spent about 20 years measuring Mars without a telescope. Kepler arrived in Prague in February 1600. Tycho died in October 1601 and Kepler got the data. The "8 minutes of arc" that would not fit a circle eventually gave him ellipses (*Astronomia Nova*, 1609). TRUE. The poisoning theory is speculation; a 2010 exhumation was inconclusive. https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/thompson.1847/161/watershed.html
- **Christmas night 1758, near Dresden: Palitzsch sees Halley's comet.** Halley died in 1742 having predicted a return in 1758. Clairaut, Lalande and Nicole-Reine (Hortense) Lepaute spent months calculating the pulls of Jupiter and Saturn and predicted perihelion around mid-April 1759. A farmer and amateur astronomer saw it first, on 25 December 1758. TRUE. Lepaute is a real woman calculator. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_Palitzsch · https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19800003728/downloads/19800003728.pdf
- **Clapham, 1798: Cavendish weighs the Earth.** He used a torsion balance (Michell's design) with lead spheres, inside a shed, and watched it through telescopes set in holes in the wall so his body heat would not disturb it. Result: Earth's density is 5.48 times water (modern value 5.51). TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_experiment
- **Berlin, 23 September 1846: Neptune.** Uranus was drifting off course. Le Verrier calculated where an unseen planet should be and mailed it to Galle. Galle and d'Arrest found it the same night the letter arrived, within about 1° of the prediction. Adams's earlier calculation was about 12° off and played no part in the discovery. Challis had seen it twice without recognising it. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_Neptune
- **Hadley Rille, Moon, 2 August 1971: Dave Scott.** He dropped a hammer and a falcon feather together and they landed together. "How about that! Mr Galileo was correct in his findings." TRUE. The quote is from a transcription, so check the NASA audio. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo_15_feather_drop.html · https://physicsworld.com/a/the-legend-of-the-leaning-tower/
- **Jaipur, 1720s–30s: Sawai Jai Singh II's Jantar Mantar.** Giant masonry instruments, UNESCO-listed in 2010. The large Samrat Yantra's shadow is said to read time to about 2 seconds, but that figure comes from guides, not UNESCO. TRUE, with HEDGE on the 2-second figure. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_Jaipur · https://jantarmantar.org/learn/observatories/instruments/samrat/index.html

## 3. Pressure, floating, air

- **Syracuse, c. 250 BCE: Archimedes and the crown.** The "Eureka" bath story comes from Vitruvius, about two centuries later. In 1586 the 22-year-old Galileo wrote *La Bilancetta*, arguing that Vitruvius's overflow method would need impossible precision, and proposed a hydrostatic balance instead. The crown story is LEGEND. Archimedes' principle is TRUE. Galileo debunking it at 22 is a great scene. https://math.nyu.edu/Archimedes/Crown/bilancetta.html · https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/fact-or-fiction-archimede
- **Florence, 1643–44: Torricelli.** Pumps cannot lift water more than about 10 m, and nobody knew why. Torricelli filled a tube with mercury and tipped it into a dish. It fell to about 76 cm and left empty space above. In a letter to Ricci (11 June 1644) he wrote: "Noi viviamo sommersi nel fondo d'un pelago d'aria." We live submerged at the bottom of an ocean of air. TRUE. https://www.chemistryworld.com/opinion/torricellis-barometer/1017293.article · https://web.lemoyne.edu/Giunta/torr.html
- **Puy de Dôme, 19 September 1648: Florin Périer for Pascal.** If air has weight, there should be less of it above you on a mountain. Pascal was too ill to climb, so his brother-in-law carried a barometer up. The mercury fell by about 3 inches (≈8 cm). A control tube stayed in Clermont with a priest watching it, and it did not change all day. TRUE. https://en.scientificrussia.ru/articles/under-atmospheric-pressure · https://dbnl.org/tekst/_gid001198401_01/_gid001198401_01_0125.php
- **Regensburg, 1654 (or later): von Guericke's hemispheres.** Two copper half-spheres with the air pumped out, and horses could not pull them apart. The date and horse count vary: a 1657 letter says 12 horses, popular accounts say 16, and one says 30. Say "teams of horses." TRUE, details HEDGE. https://physicstoday.aip.org/backscatter/magdeburg-hemispheres
- **Boyle:** The chemistry book probably already has him. I did not research a separate scene.

## 4. Energy and heat

- **Glasgow, 1761: Joseph Black.** Heat ice that is already melting and the thermometer does not rise; you just get more water. The heat is "latent." He never properly published it; it lives in his lecture notes. TRUE. https://chem.libretexts.org/Ancillary_Materials/Exemplars_and_Case_Studies/Exemplars/Culture/Original_Writings_of_Joseph_Black_on_the_Melting_of_Ice
- **Munich arsenal, 1797–98: Count Rumford.** Cannon got hot while being bored, apparently without limit. He bored a blunt drill into a barrel sitting in water, with no fire. "At two hours thirty minutes it actually boiled!" That is bad news for caloric as a fluid. TRUE. The quote is from his 1798 paper; check the "astonishment of bystanders" line before using it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Inquiry_Concerning_the_Source_of_the_Heat_Which_Is_Excited_by_Friction · https://spark.iop.org/revolutionary-rumford
- **Paris, 1824: Sadi Carnot.** How good can a steam engine be? He printed 600 copies of a 118-page booklet at his own expense, and almost nobody read it. He reasoned from his father's work on water engines: heat "falls" from hot to cold the way water falls over a wheel. TRUE. The waterfall framing is a historians' summary, so do not quote it as Carnot's words. https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/papers/Analogy_Carnot_online.pdf
- **Java / Batavia, 1840: Julius Robert Mayer.** A ship's doctor bleeding sailors in the tropics found their venous blood surprisingly bright red. His idea: in the heat the body burns less food to stay warm. From there he reached conservation of energy (1842), was ignored, and was vindicated in the 1860s. TRUE in outline; his reasoning is partly reconstructed. https://galileo.phys.virginia.edu/classes/152.mf1i.spring02/MayerJoule.htm · https://press.princeton.edu/node/14062
- **Manchester, 1840s → Chamonix, 1847: Joule.** The paddle wheel is TRUE: falling weights churn water and warm it measurably. The honeymoon is TRUE: Thomson (Kelvin) met Joule and his new wife Amelia near Mont Blanc in 1847. The waterfall thermometer is HEDGE/LEGEND: the story surfaced about 35 years later and no result exists. https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2009/12/joule-abstract-converting-mechanical-heat · https://spark.iop.org/unsettling-james-joule
- **Pune / Thumba, 1948–76: Anna Mani.** She trained in meteorological instruments at Imperial College, joined the India Meteorological Department (IMD) in 1948, and standardised the drawings for about 100 weather instruments so India could make its own. She ran a solar-radiation network for the International Geophysical Year (1957–58), measured ozone from 1960, built the Thumba met tower for Sarabhai in 1963, and retired as Deputy Director General in 1976. Monsoon link: IMD's instruments are how the monsoon gets measured. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Mani · https://scroll.in/article/1004783/indias-met-department-beyond-the-life-and-times-of-anna-mani
- **Every Mumbai kitchen: the matka.** Water seeps through the clay, evaporates and takes heat with it. It cools less in humid air, because it cannot go below the wet-bulb temperature. That is why a matka works better in Pune in April than in Mumbai in July. Measured drops are 5–15 °C depending on air humidity. TRUE. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.00667 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43939-025-00516-z

## 5. Waves, sound and light

- **Smithy, c. 500 BCE: Pythagoras and the hammers.** First told by Nicomachus about 600 years later. The physics is wrong: hammer weights do not set pitch. LEGEND. **What really happened (Florence, c. 1589): Vincenzo Galilei**, Galileo's father, hung weights on strings and found that an octave needs four times the weight, not twice. TRUE, and a lovely father-and-son pairing. https://courses.lumenlearning.com/music-app-rford/chapter/pythagoras/ · https://aps.org/apsnews/2015/07/death-vincenzo-galilei
- **Cairo, c. 1011–21: Ibn al-Haytham.** Euclid and Ptolemy thought the eye sends out rays. He argued that light enters the eye: bright light hurts your eyes, and every point of an object sends out its own ray. He described dark-room (camera obscura) images in the *Book of Optics*. TRUE. The story that he faked madness to escape Caliph al-Hakim after promising to control the Nile is HEDGE; Wikipedia calls it legend. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Haytham · https://www.bibalex.org/SCIplanet/en/Article/Details.aspx?id=5200
- **Woolsthorpe/Cambridge 1666 → letter of 6 February 1672: Newton's prism.** Through a hole in a shutter in a darkened room, a prism spreads sunlight into colours. In the "experimentum crucis" he sent one colour through a second prism, and it did not change. So white light is a mixture. TRUE (the 1672 letter; the 1666 dating is Newton's own). https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/New_Theory_About_Light_and_Colour
- **Paris Observatory, 1676: Ole Rømer.** In September he predicted that the eclipse of Io on 9 November would come about 10 minutes late, and it did. Light takes time to cross Earth's orbit. Rømer gave a time, not a speed; Huygens did the arithmetic and got a value about 30% low. TRUE. https://www.amnh.org/learn-teach/curriculum-collections/cosmic-horizons-book/ole-roemer-speed-of-light
- **London, 24 November 1803: Thomas Young.** He held a thin card, about 1/30 inch wide, edge-on in a sunbeam and saw fringes inside its shadow, which is interference. The famous "double slit" is a later textbook tidy-up. TRUE, but tell it as the card experiment. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young%27s_interference_experiment
- **Paris, 1818–19: Fresnel, Poisson, Arago.** Poisson, a particle-theory man on the prize jury, worked through Fresnel's wave maths and found it predicts a bright spot in the centre of a round shadow, which he thought absurd. Arago tested it and found the spot. Fresnel won; Poisson was never convinced; Maraldi had actually seen the spot decades earlier. TRUE. https://physicsworld.com/a/the-spot-in-the-shadow/ · https://www.britannica.com/science/Poissons-spot
- **Prague, 25 May 1842 → Utrecht–Maarssen line, 1845: Doppler and Buys Ballot.** Doppler presented his idea to a handful of members of the Royal Bohemian Society. His main example, the colours of double stars, was wrong. Buys Ballot tested it with horn players on an open wagon pulled by a locomotive past musicians with perfect pitch. The note rose on approach and fell after. TRUE. https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Christian_Doppler · https://muurformules.sitestest.hum.uu.nl/mural-paintings/christophorus-buys-ballot/?lang=en
- **Suresnes → Montmartre, 1849: Fizeau's cogwheel.** He shone light through a 720-tooth wheel to a mirror 8.6 km away and spun the wheel until the returning light hit a tooth. Result about 315,000 km/s, roughly 5% high. TRUE. https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Fizeau/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fizeau%27s_measurement_of_the_speed_of_light_in_air (I did not research Foucault's rotating mirror.)
- **Mediterranean, September 1921: Raman on the SS *Narkunda*.** Lord Rayleigh had said the sea is blue because it reflects the sky. Raman used a Nicol prism to block reflected skylight, and the sea was still blue, so water itself scatters light. He wrote a letter to *Nature* on board and posted it when the ship reached Bombay. TRUE, with a Mumbai hook. https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2009/02/this-month-in-physics-history · https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/raman-sea/
- **Calcutta (IACS), February 1928: Raman and K. S. Krishnan.** Scattered light comes out with a slightly changed colour. Krishnan's diary for 5–28 February records it; on 9 February Raman said it should be called the "Raman-Krishnan effect." The diary is torn off mid-sentence after 28 February. 28 February is now National Science Day. The 1930 Nobel citation reads "for his work on the scattering of light and for the discovery of the effect named after him." TRUE. https://collections.archives.ncbs.res.in/handle/42412/4108 · https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1930/summary/ · https://ethw.org/Milestones:Raman_Effect
- **Calcutta, 1919–20: Raman and Sivakali Kumar on drums.** Most drums give out-of-tune (inharmonic) overtones. The tabla and mridangam give harmonic ones because the black loaded patch (*syahi*) tunes the drumhead. *Nature* 104, p. 500 (15 January 1920). TRUE. https://repository.ias.ac.in/52555 · https://www.livehistoryindia.com/story/cover-story/c-v-ramans-work-on-indian-music/ (I did not research his violin work.)

## 6. Electricity and magnetism

- **London, 1600: William Gilbert's *terrella*.** He made a small magnetic sphere, a "little Earth," and showed a dip needle tilts over it the way compasses do at sea. Conclusion: the Earth is a magnet. He also dismissed the sailors' belief that garlic ruins a compass as "fable and falsehood." It was della Porta who actually breathed garlic on a lodestone. TRUE. https://www.theiet.org/membership/library-archives/the-iet-archives/archives-highlights/earth-the-magnet-william-gilberts-terrella · https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/laws-of-attraction/
- **Philadelphia, June 1752: Franklin's kite.** No first-hand account in Franklin's handwriting; Priestley's account came 15 years later; the only witness was his son. Historians split: Tucker says it was invented, Isaacson accepts it. HEDGE. https://whyy.org/articles/ben-franklins-kite-story-charged-with-confusion/
- **Copenhagen, 21 April 1820: Ørsted.** During a lecture, a compass near a current-carrying wire twitched, so slightly the audience didn't notice. Reversing the current reversed the twitch. He published four pages in Latin in July. TRUE. https://aps.org/apsnews/2008/07/1820-oersted-electromagnetism
- **Royal Institution, 3–4 September 1821 → 29 August 1831: Faraday.** First a wire spinning around a magnet in a dish of mercury, the first motor. Ten years later, an iron ring with two coils: switching one on made a needle on the other jump, which is induction (and the first transformer). TRUE. https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2001/08/faraday-electromagnetism · https://www.theiet.org/resources/library/archives/biographies/faraday.cfm
- **Royal Institution, 1836: the Faraday cage.** He built a foil-covered 12 ft cube, went inside with an electroscope while the outside was charged, and could not find "the least influence." TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_cage
- **"One day, sir, you may tax it": LEGEND.** There is no contemporary record. Gladstone became PM in 1868, the year after Faraday died; a Chancellor version is merely possible. https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/long-ago-and-faraday/ · https://physicsworld.com/a/who-said-that/
- **London, 1861–62 → 1865: Maxwell.** His equations give a wave speed that matches Fizeau's measured speed of light. "We can scarcely avoid the inference that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena." TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Physical_Lines_of_Force
- **Karlsruhe, 1887–88: Hertz.** He made and detected Maxwell's waves. The "It's of no use whatsoever" reply to students has no primary source. The IOP traces it only to a 2013 secondary article (Ramsay, *Neurodiagnostic Journal*). HEDGE; tell it as "the story goes." https://spark.iop.org/hertzs-useless-discovery
- **Calcutta, 1895: Jagadish Chandra Bose.** In a public demonstration, reportedly chaired by Lt-Governor Sir Alexander Mackenzie, his invisible waves passed through walls about 75 ft away, rang a bell and set off gunpowder. Accounts differ on the number of walls and the date. He used millimetre waves (about 5 mm, roughly 60 GHz). In January 1897 he spoke at the Royal Institution at Rayleigh's invitation. The IEEE Milestone was dedicated in Kolkata in 2012. TRUE, details HEDGE. https://ethw.org/Milestones:First_Millimeter-wave_Communication_Experiments_by_J.C._Bose,_1894-96 · https://ieeemilestones.ethw.org/Milestone-Proposal:First_Millimeter-wave_Communication_Experiments_by_J.C._Bose,_1894-96
- **The "Bose refused to patent" story: HEDGE, it needs nuance.** US Patent 755,840 (a crystal detector, filed 1901, granted 1904) was obtained by his friends Sara Bull and Sister Nivedita. Bose did not pursue it commercially and it lapsed. https://www.computerhistory.org/siliconengine/semiconductor-rectifiers-patented-as-cats-whisker-detectors/ · https://www.qsl.net/v/vu2msy/JCBOSE1.htm
- **Signal Hill, 12 December 1901: Marconi.** He reported hearing "S" (three dots) from Cornwall across the Atlantic. Sceptics think he heard static or a harmonic. TRUE claim, contested reception. https://ethw.org/Milestones:Reception_of_Transatlantic_Radio_Signals,_1901
- **Edison, Tesla and Topsy: LEGEND as usually told.** The AC/DC fight was mainly Edison versus Westinghouse. Topsy the elephant was killed in 1903, a decade after it ended, and there is no evidence linking Edison to it. https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/essaying-edison/essay/myth-buster-topsy-the-elephant
- **Colaba, Bombay, 1841–1904 → Alibag: the magnetic observatory.** Hourly magnetic readings from 1846. Charles Chambers (superintendent) published Colaba results in *Phil. Trans.* (1859–65 data). Nanabhoy Moos, the first Indian director, compiled the 1846–1905 volumes and helped move the magnetometers to Alibag because electric trams would add electromagnetic noise. Recording overlapped from 1904 to 1906, then the trams started. TRUE (peer-reviewed). https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/6/107/2015/ · https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-108999/
- **Bombay, 7 May 1907: the first electric tram,** Municipal Office to Crawford Market, 5:30 pm. Horse trams had run since 1874. TRUE (the date varies slightly between sources). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihanmumbai_Electric_Supply_and_Transport · https://wiki.fibis.org/w/Bombay_Tramways

## 7. Atoms, light and the quantum (light touch)

- **Bern, 1905: Einstein,** a patent clerk, published four papers in *Annalen der Physik* (light quanta, Brownian motion, relativity, E=mc²). His Nobel was for the photoelectric effect, not relativity. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_mirabilis_papers
- **Calcutta → London, 1920: Meghnad Saha.** Why do stars of different temperatures show different spectral lines? His ionisation equation links a star's spectrum to its temperature and pressure. Alfred Fowler hosted him at Imperial College and helped him revise the papers. TRUE. https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/physics-biographies/meghnad-saha
- **Dacca, 4 June 1924: Satyendra Nath Bose.** The *Philosophical Magazine* had not answered him, so he wrote to Einstein ("Respected Sir…"), saying he did not know enough German and asking Einstein to arrange publication if he thought it worthy. Einstein translated it himself (postcard of 2 July) and it appeared in August with Einstein's note. Particles of this kind are now called bosons. TRUE. https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/the-man-behind-bose-statistics
- **Harvard, 1925: Cecilia Payne.** Using Saha's equation, she found the stars are mostly hydrogen. Russell called it "clearly impossible," so her thesis labelled the values "spurious." Russell reached the same result in 1929. Struve later called it "the most brilliant PhD thesis ever written in astronomy." TRUE. https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/01/cecilia-payne-gaposchkin-universe-changed
- **Kungälv, Sweden, Christmas 1938: Lise Meitner and Otto Frisch.** Hahn's letter said uranium had produced barium, which made no sense. On a walk in the snow they worked out that the nucleus splits, with about 200 MeV released (*Nature*, February 1939). TRUE. The skis, the tree trunk and the exact day are HEDGE (they come from Frisch's memoir). https://www.epsnews.eu/2016/12/eps-historic-site-the-home-of-lise-meitner-in-sweden/ · https://inference-review.com/article/the-discovery-of-nuclear-fission
- **Darjeeling and Sandakphu, 1939–42: Bibha Chowdhuri and D. M. Bose.** They left photographic plates on mountains for cosmic rays to streak across, and found curved tracks of a new particle (mesons). Wartime shortages meant only half-tone plates. Powell later won a Nobel with better plates and acknowledged their method as the first attempt. In 1949 Chowdhuri joined Bhabha's group at TIFR in Bombay. TRUE. https://www.nature.com/articles/147240a0 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibha_Chowdhuri
- **Cambridge, 8 August 1928 → Bombay, 1945: Homi Bhabha.** He wrote to his father: "…business or a job as an engineer is not the thing for me… I am burning with a desire to do physics." The bargain: get a first in engineering, then do physics. On 12 March 1944 he wrote to the Sir Dorab Tata Trust. TIFR started in Bangalore in June 1945 and was inaugurated at "Kenilworth," Peddar Road, Bombay, on 19 December 1945. TRUE (the letter's wording varies a little). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homi_J._Bhabha · https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3356
- **Washington and New York, Christmas 1956: Chien-Shiung Wu.** Does nature care about left and right? Cobalt-60 cooled near absolute zero emitted more electrons one way. Lee and Yang got the 1957 Nobel; Wu was left out despite 23 nominations. TRUE. https://lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/chien-shiung-wu · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment

## 8. Space

- **At sea, Bombay → England, 1930: Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar,** aged 19, aboard the SS *Pilsna*. He added relativity to white-dwarf theory and found a maximum mass (today about 1.4 Suns). Eddington mocked it in 1935. Nobel 1983. TRUE. https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Chandrasekhar_Eddington/
- **Harvard, 1912: Henrietta Leavitt,** one of the women "computers" studying photographic plates. In the Small Magellanic Cloud, where all the stars sit at about the same distance, brighter Cepheids blink more slowly. That gave astronomers a cosmic measuring tape. A 2025 reanalysis says her result holds up. TRUE. I could not verify the "$0.30 an hour" wage, so drop it. https://astrobites.org/2019/03/08/leavitt-variable-stars/ · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17438
- **Thumba, Kerala, 21 November 1963: Sarabhai's first rocket.** A US-made Nike-Apache launched beside St Mary Magdalene church, which became the control and assembly building, with rockets placed about where the altar had been. Parts were carried by bicycle and bullock cart. TRUE for the church and launch; HEDGE for the bicycle (feature articles only; a famous ISRO photo is often cited, but I did not see it in an ISRO source). https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/60th-anniversary-of-indias-first-rocket-launch-fishworkers-latin-catholic/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike-Apache
- **Kapustin Yar, USSR, 19 April 1975: the Aryabhata satellite,** 360 kg, built in India and launched by the Soviet Union. It re-entered the atmosphere in 1992. TRUE. https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/aryabhata_1.html
- **Salyut 7, 3 April 1984: Rakesh Sharma.** Indira Gandhi asked how India looked from space; he answered "Saare Jahan Se Achha." TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakesh_Sharma
- **Karnal → Columbia, 1997 and 2003: Kalpana Chawla.** Robotic-arm operator on STS-87. She died when Columbia broke up on 1 February 2003, 16 minutes before landing. TRUE. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/chawla_kalpana.pdf
- **Moon, 2008–09: Chandrayaan-1.** NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3) on board found signs of water and hydroxyl, announced 24 September 2009. ISRO says its impact probe's CHACE instrument saw water earlier; one review thinks the CHACE signal may be contaminated. TRUE for M3, HEDGE for the CHACE claim. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/chandrayaan-1/ · https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.5597
- **Mars, 24 September 2014: Mangalyaan,** first Indian Mars mission and in orbit at the first try. It cost about ₹450 crore (~$74M) against NASA's MAVEN at $671M. "Cheaper than the film *Gravity*" (~$100M) is TRUE in numbers but compares a whole mission with a film budget. Tell it that way. https://physicstoday.aip.org/opinion/new-york-times-highlights-stark-frugality-of-indias-mars-orbiter · https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/indias-mars-mission-was-cheaper-all-these-hollywood-movies-n214861
- **Lunar south polar region, 23 August 2023, 18:04 IST: Chandrayaan-3.** Vikram landed at about 69.3°S, named Shiv Shakti Point on 26 August. Pragyan's laser instrument (LIBS) confirmed sulphur, and the rover drove about 101 m. TRUE. https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2024/ListTechnicalPresentations/2024_STSC_technical_presentations_slides/9_26_--_4_INDIA_Chandrayaan3.pdf
- **L1, 6 January 2024: Aditya-L1** entered a halo orbit about 1.5 million km from Earth, with a 178-day loop. TRUE. https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/halo-orbit-insertion-adtya-l1.html
- **Gaganyaan, status at 28 October 2026:** not yet flown. On 24 October 2026 the ISRO chief said the uncrewed G1 flight, carrying the robot Vyommitra, is about two months away. Crewed flight is aimed at 2027. It has slipped repeatedly since a 2020 target, so write "planned" and re-check before printing. https://www.uniindia.com/south-india/space-gaganyaan-test-flight-isro-chief/224116 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaganyaan-1

## 9. Mumbai physics

- **Haji Ali causeway: tides.** The causeway is fully submerged at high tide. Mumbai's tide is mixed semi-diurnal (two highs a day of unequal height, larger at new and full moon). On one sample day the forecast was a 3.93 m high and a 1.43 m low. I could not verify the often-quoted "4.5–5 m" figure as a range rather than a high-water height. Use INCOIS or Survey of India tide tables for the book's numbers. HEDGE on numbers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji_Ali_Dargah · https://services.incois.gov.in/documents/ITCOocean/OITC018/L13-Srinivas K_TIDES.pdf · https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-on-alert-as-393-metre-high-tide-expected-today-imd-warns-of-heavy-rain-strong-winds
- **Bandra–Worli Sea Link:** a cable-stayed bridge 5.6 km long with a 250 m main span. Four lanes opened on 30 June 2009 and all eight on 24 March 2010. TRUE. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandra%E2%80%93Worli_Sea_Link
- **Monsoon:** IMD's normal onset for Mumbai is 11 June (it was 10 June before the 2020 revision; Kerala is 1 June). The first theory came from **Edmond Halley (1686)**: the monsoon is a giant sea breeze driven by land heating faster than sea. Halley again, which is great for continuity. Then a twist: Sulochana Gadgil and colleagues (2021) argue the giant-sea-breeze picture fails tests, and that rainfall bands linked to the ITCZ (the equatorial rain belt) matter more. That is "wrong, then less wrong" happening right now. TRUE. https://mausam.imd.gov.in/backend/assets/press_release_pdf/IMD_Press_release_new_onset_and_withdrawal_dates%28Final%29.pdf · https://eprints.iisc.ac.in/69617/ · https://courses.ems.psu.edu/meteo3/book/export/html/2271
- **Nehru Planetarium, Worli:** inaugurated on 3 March 1977. TRUE. https://www.nehrucentremumbai.in/planetarium-about-us/
- **GMRT, Khodad near Narayangaon (about 80 km from Pune):** 30 dishes, each 45 m, spread over about 25 km, designed and built 1984–96 under Govind Swarup, routine operation from 2000, run by TIFR's NCRA. TRUE. https://www.ncra.tifr.res.in/research/the-giant-metrewave-radio-telescope · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govind_Swarup
- **Local trains:** good everyday physics (inertia when the train brakes, the force of a crowd), but I found no historical scene or sourced crowding figure. Use it as Kym's lived example, not as a scene. Dabbawalas are not physics; skip them.

## True stories that sound made up (checked)

1. **Cavendish's shed:** he weighed the Earth by telescope, through holes in a shed wall, and reportedly talked to his housekeeper only by notes. TRUE; housekeeper detail HEDGE. https://claphamsociety.com/articles/31eccentric-scientist/
2. **Rømer predicted an eclipse would be 10 minutes late, and it was.** TRUE. (URL in §5)
3. **The Arago spot:** a sceptic's "absurd" prediction came true. TRUE. (§5)
4. **Buys Ballot's horn players on a train.** TRUE. (§5)
5. **Fizeau's cogwheel across Paris.** TRUE. (§5)
6. **Galileo's fingers and tooth**, taken from his body in 1737, lost after 1905, found at auction in 2009, now in the Museo Galileo. TRUE (which fingers varies between sources). https://astronomy2009.org/news/updates/622/index.html
7. **Faraday's cage:** he sat inside a charged 12 ft foil box with an electroscope and found nothing. TRUE. (§6)
8. **Foucault's pendulum, Panthéon, 1851:** 67 m wire, 28 kg bob. Newspapers asked "Avez-vous vu tourner la Terre?" ("Have you seen the Earth turn?"). TRUE. https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/10.1016/j.crhy.2017.11.003.pdf
9. **1919 eclipse:** Eddington was accused in 1980 of cherry-picking data. A 1978–79 Greenwich reanalysis of the Sobral plates largely supported the 1919 result, and Kennefick argues the data exclusions were sound. TRUE, with the controversy noted. https://www.nature.com/news/2007/070910/full/news070903-20.html · https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/did-researchers-cook-data-from-first-general-relativity-test
10. **Millennium Bridge, June 2000:** walkers fell into step with the sway and made it worse. It closed two days after opening; dampers cost about £5m; reopened 2002. TRUE (a 2021 paper offers a rival explanation). https://enr.com/articles/53233-new-explanation-given-for-londons-wobbling-millennium-bridge-after-two-decades
11. **Tacoma Narrows, 1940:** not simple resonance but wind-driven twisting flutter (Billah & Scanlan, 1991). The only death was Tubby, a dog, who bit the professor trying to rescue him. TRUE. https://ketchum.org/wind.html · https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2023/06/13/professor-farquharsons-dog
12. **Einstein's brain**, kept without permission in 1955 by pathologist Thomas Harvey, cut up and stored in jars, returned in 1998. TRUE. https://www.npr.org/2005/04/18/4602913/the-long-strange-journey-of-einsteins-brain
13. **The kilogram:** a metal cylinder that slowly changed mass was retired on 20 May 2019; the kilogram is now defined by the Planck constant. TRUE. https://physicsworld.com/a/new-definition-of-the-kilogram-comes-into-force/
14. **Mpemba:** a Tanzanian schoolboy's hot ice-cream mix froze first and his class laughed. He published with Osborne in 1969. TRUE story. Whether the effect is real is still DISPUTED: Burridge & Linden (2016) found no meaningful evidence; Bechhoefer's controlled experiments support it. https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/the-mpemba-effect-fact-or-fiction/3008257.article · https://www.quantamagazine.org/does-hot-water-freeze-faster-than-cold-physicists-keep-asking-20220629/
15. **The Royal Society couldn't afford the *Principia* because of a fish book.** TRUE. (§1)
16. **Bombay's magnetic observatory was driven out of the city by trams.** TRUE. (§6)

## The 10 strongest chapter-opening scenes

1. **`:::scene Delft, 1586`**: Stevin and de Groot hear a single thud. A real drop, before Galileo, and it sets up the Pisa myth.
2. **`:::scene Puy de Dôme, 1648`**: Périer climbs with the mercury tube while a priest guards the control tube below.
3. **`:::scene Clapham, 1798`**: Cavendish peers through a hole in his shed wall to weigh the world.
4. **`:::scene Paris, 1818`**: Poisson calls the bright spot absurd; Arago looks and finds it.
5. **`:::scene Copenhagen, 1820`**: the compass twitches in a lecture and nobody in the audience notices.
6. **`:::scene Berlin, 23 September 1846`**: Le Verrier's letter arrives and Neptune is there the same night.
7. **`:::scene Calcutta, 1895`**: Bose's invisible waves ring a bell through the walls.
8. **`:::scene The Mediterranean, 1921`**: Raman holds a prism over the rail of the *Narkunda* and later posts his letter to *Nature* from **Bombay**.
9. **`:::scene Dacca, 1924`**: "Respected Sir": an unknown teacher writes to Einstein.
10. **`:::scene Thumba, 1963`**: a rocket beside a fishing-village church.

**Mumbai bookends:** `:::scene Colaba, 1904` (trams chase the magnetometers to Alibag) and `:::scene Kenilworth, Peddar Road, 1945` (TIFR opens). **Reserves:** Kungälv 1938, Utrecht 1845, Hadley Rille 1971.
