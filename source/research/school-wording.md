## 1. Cambridge IGCSE Physics (0625)

**Programme page:** https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625/

**Syllabus PDFs listed on that page:**
- 2026–2028: https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697209-2026-2028-syllabus.pdf. The cover says: "Use this syllabus for exams in 2026, 2027 and 2028… Exams are also available in the March series in India." It is Version 2.
- An update note for 2026–2028 is linked at /Images/748914-2026-2028-syllabus-update.pdf. I saw it listed but did not open it.
- 2029: https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/764332-2029-syllabus.pdf. It says "version 1, published September 2026" and "There are no significant changes which affect teaching." This matters because Kym will probably sit IGCSE around 2029–2030, and the content below should still apply.

**Topic headings (confirmed) and sub-topics:**
1. **Motion, forces and energy**
   - 1.1 Physical quantities and measurement techniques
   - 1.2 Motion
   - 1.3 Mass and weight
   - 1.4 Density
   - 1.5 Forces: 1.5.1 Effects of forces, 1.5.2 Turning effect of forces, 1.5.3 Centre of gravity
   - 1.6 Momentum
   - 1.7 Energy, work and power: 1.7.1 Energy, 1.7.2 Work, 1.7.3 Energy resources, 1.7.4 Power
   - 1.8 Pressure
2. **Thermal physics**
   - 2.1 Kinetic particle model of matter: 2.1.1 States of matter, 2.1.2 Particle model, 2.1.3 Gases and the absolute scale of temperature
   - 2.2 Thermal properties and temperature: 2.2.1 Thermal expansion, 2.2.2 Specific heat capacity, 2.2.3 Melting, boiling and evaporation
   - 2.3 Transfer of thermal energy: 2.3.1 Conduction, 2.3.2 Convection, 2.3.3 Radiation, 2.3.4 Consequences of thermal energy transfer
3. **Waves**
   - 3.1 General properties of waves
   - 3.2 Light: 3.2.1 Reflection, 3.2.2 Refraction, 3.2.3 Thin lenses, 3.2.4 Dispersion
   - 3.3 Electromagnetic spectrum
   - 3.4 Sound
4. **Electricity and magnetism**
   - 4.1 Simple phenomena of magnetism
   - 4.2 Electrical quantities: charge, current, e.m.f. and p.d., resistance, electrical energy and power
   - 4.3 Electric circuits
   - 4.4 Electrical safety
   - 4.5 Electromagnetic effects: induction, a.c. generator, magnetic effect of a current, force on a conductor, d.c. motor, transformer
5. **Nuclear physics**
   - 5.1 The nuclear model of the atom: 5.1.1 The atom, 5.1.2 The nucleus
   - 5.2 Radioactivity: detection, three types of emission, decay, half-life, safety
6. **Space physics**
   - 6.1 The Earth and the Solar System: 6.1.1 The Earth, 6.1.2 The Solar System
   - 6.2 Stars and the Universe: 6.2.1 The Sun as a star, 6.2.2 Stars, 6.2.3 The Universe

Each topic is split into Core and Supplement. "Candidates who are only taught the Core subject content can achieve a maximum of grade C."

**Exact wording that matters for your bridges (2026–2028 PDF):**
- **Mass:** "mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer"
- **Weight:** "weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass". The Supplement adds "weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass".
- **g as field strength:** "Define gravitational field strength as force per unit mass… g = W/m… equivalent to the acceleration of free fall". Its unit is listed as **N / kg**.
- **g as acceleration:** "acceleration of free fall g… is approximately 9.8 m / s²"
- **Balances:** "Know that weights (and masses) may be compared using a balance"
- **Vectors and scalars:** weight is listed as a vector, mass as a scalar.
- **Resultant force:** "an object either remains at rest or continues in a straight line at constant speed unless acted on by a resultant force". Also: "a resultant force may change the velocity of an object by changing its direction of motion or its speed".
- **Circular motion:** "motion in a circular path due to a force perpendicular to the motion". The word "centrifugal" appears 0 times.
- **Friction:** "solid friction as the force between two surfaces that may impede motion and produce heating"
- **Moments:** "moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot". Equilibrium is "no resultant force and no resultant moment".
- **Energy stores:** "energy may be stored as kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic (strain), nuclear, electrostatic and internal (thermal)"
- **Energy transfers:** "transferred between stores… by forces (mechanical work done), electrical currents (electrical work done), heating, and by electromagnetic, sound and other waves"
- **Conservation:** taught through flow diagrams in Core and Sankey diagrams in Supplement.
- **Efficiency:** "useful energy output / total energy input". The words "lost", "wasted" and "dissipate" appear **0 times** in the subject content.
- **Pressure:** "pressure as force per unit area… p = F/A" and "∆p = ρg∆h"
- **Gas pressure (Supplement):** "forces exerted by particles colliding with surfaces, creating a force per unit area"
- **Absolute zero:** "−273 °C… where the particles have least kinetic energy". It says least, not zero.
- **Temperature and internal energy:** "a rise in the temperature of an object increases its internal energy". Temperature rise is described as "an increase in the average kinetic energies of all of the particles".
- **Specific heat capacity:** "the energy required per unit mass per unit temperature increase; c = ∆E/(m∆θ)"
- **Heat words:** the topic is titled "Transfer of **thermal energy**". "Heating" is used as a process. The noun "heat" survives only in "specific heat capacity" and in examples such as "heat water".
- **Current:** "electric current is related to the flow of charge"
- **Conventional current:** "conventional current is from positive to negative and that the flow of free electrons is from negative to positive"
- **Light:** "Draw and use ray diagrams"; "Define and use the terms normal, angle of incidence…"
- **Day and night:** "the Earth… rotates on its axis, which is tilted, once in approximately 24 hours"
- **Seasons:** "orbits the Sun once in approximately 365 days and use this to explain the periodic nature of the seasons". Tilt is not explicitly linked to seasons.
- **Moon:** "approximately one month for the Moon to orbit the Earth… cycle of phases". Eclipses: 0 mentions.
- **Orbits:** "the force that keeps an object in orbit around the Sun is the gravitational attraction of the Sun"; "elliptical orbits"; orbital speed "v = 2πr/T"
- **Redshift:** "an increase in the observed wavelength of electromagnetic radiation emitted from receding stars and galaxies"; it is "evidence that the Universe is expanding and supports the Big Bang Theory". Also covered: CMBR, H₀ = v/d, and "all the matter in the Universe was present at a single point".
- **Not in the syllabus:** "upthrust" and "suction" (0 mentions each).

**Combined and Co-ordinated Sciences:** Cambridge also offers IGCSE Combined Science (0653) and IGCSE Sciences – Co-ordinated (Double) (0654). The 0654 page lists 2025–2027 and 2028–2029 syllabuses: https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-sciences-co-ordinated-double-0654/. Whether their physics wording matches 0625 is **UNVERIFIED** because I did not open those syllabuses.

## 2. Cambridge Lower Secondary Science (0893), Stages 7–9

**Official curriculum page:** https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-lower-secondary/curriculum/science. It lists six strands: Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Earth and Space, Thinking and Working Scientifically, Science in Context.

**Framework:** "Published in September 2020 for first teaching in September 2021". I could not reach an official PDF. My copy came from a third-party mirror, https://pdfcoffee.com/science-curriculum-framework-0893-tcm143-595685-pdf-free.html, and the fetch tool partly paraphrased longer objectives. Treat the codes and wording below as **check against the official PDF**. The list may also be incomplete.

**Physics substrands:** Forces and energy (Pf), Light and sound (Ps), Electricity and magnetism (Pe). Earth and Space has a separate substrand, Earth in space (ESs).

| Stage | Objectives (brief) |
|---|---|
| **7** | **7Pf:** energy changes in events; "energy tends to dissipate and in doing so it becomes less useful"; gravity as "a force of attraction between any two objects"; no air resistance in a vacuum.<br>**7Ps:** particle vibration in sound; no sound in a vacuum; echoes.<br>**7Pe:** "Use a simple model to describe electricity as a flow of electrons around a circuit"; conductors and insulators; measuring current in series; circuit symbols.<br>**7ESs:** planet formation by gravity; gravity holds the Solar System in orbit; tides; eclipses. |
| **8** | **8Pf:** speed = distance/time; distance–time graphs; "balanced and unbalanced forces"; "turning forces (moment = force x distance)"; "pressure is caused by the action of a force, exerted by a substance, on an area"; particle explanation of pressure in gases and liquids; diffusion.<br>**8Ps:** law of reflection; refraction "in terms of change of speed"; dispersion; adding and subtracting colours.<br>**8Pe:** magnetic fields; electromagnets.<br>**8ESs:** galaxies; asteroids. |
| **9** | **9Pf:** density and floating; "Describe the difference between heat and temperature"; "energy is conserved"; "thermal energy will always transfer from hotter… to colder", which it calls "heat dissipation"; conduction, convection and radiation; evaporative cooling.<br>**9Ps:** waveforms (loudness ↔ amplitude, pitch ↔ frequency); interference.<br>**9Pe:** parallel circuits; resistance = voltage/current.<br>**9ESs:** asteroid impacts; collision theory for the Moon's formation; nebulae. |

**Where 0893 differs from IGCSE:**
- 0893 says "turning force", "heat" and "electricity flows". IGCSE says "moment", "thermal energy" and "current/charge".
- 0893's moment formula leaves out "perpendicular".

## 3. IB MYP Sciences

**IBO public subject brief (URL from search results; the fetch was blocked with a 403):** https://www.ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/brochures-and-infographics/pdfs/myp-brief-sciences-en.pdf

**Copy I actually read:** the 2015 brief ("From 2014", © IBO 2015), mirrored by a school at https://www.hwa.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/myp-brief_sciences_-2015.pdf. The ibo.org programme page also returned a 403. I found no official notice of a newer MYP Sciences guide. A newer guide is **UNVERIFIED** either way.

**Structure:** "schools… generally develop discrete, modular or integrated science courses." Every subject needs at least 50 hours a year. The MYP covers ages 11–16, Years 1–5.

**Concepts:**
- Key concepts: "change, relationships and systems broadly frame the MYP curriculum."
- Related concepts: the brief says "Examples… include energy, movement, transformation and models."
- The full **Physics** related-concept list is from "MYP: From principles into practice, 2014". I read it in a school PDF (https://www.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/siteassets/schools/high-schools/r-w/watkinsmillhs/uploadedfiles/ib/key20and20related20concepts1.pdf): Consequences, Development, Energy, Environment, Evidence, Form, Function, Interaction, Models, Movement, Patterns, Transformation.

**Criteria (equally weighted, eight achievement levels each):**
- **A Knowing and understanding:** "scientific knowledge (facts, ideas, concepts, processes, laws, principles, models and theories)"
- **B Inquiring and designing**
- **C Processing and evaluating**
- **D Reflecting on the impacts of science**

**Optional Year 5 eAssessment:** about 30 marks for A, 60 for B+C and 30 for D. Its listed topics include "forces", "electromagnetism", "waves" and "states and properties of matter".

**How MYP Years 1–3 handle physics:** schools write their own units. I could not get school unit lists, so this is **UNVERIFIED**. Given the brief's statement above, expect integrated or modular science in Years 1–3, with each unit framed as "Key concept + related concept + global context".

## 4. IB DP Physics (first assessment 2025): where this goes later

The official IB "Physics guide, First assessment 2025" is hosted by a school: https://www.choithraminternational.com/IB%20Subject%20Guides/Physics.pdf. The ibo.org page returned a 403.

The five themes and their topics:
- **A. Space, time and motion:** A.1 Kinematics, A.2 Forces and momentum, A.3 Work, energy and power, A.4 Rigid body mechanics, A.5 Galilean and special relativity
- **B. The particulate nature of matter:** B.1 Thermal energy transfers, B.2 Greenhouse effect, B.3 Gas laws, B.4 Thermodynamics, B.5 Current and circuits
- **C. Wave behaviour:** C.1 Simple harmonic motion, C.2 Wave model, C.3 Wave phenomena, C.4 Standing waves and resonance, C.5 Doppler effect
- **D. Fields:** D.1 Gravitational fields, D.2 Electric and magnetic fields, D.3 Motion in electromagnetic fields, D.4 Induction
- **E. Nuclear and quantum physics:** E.1 Structure of the atom, E.2 Quantum physics, E.3 Radioactive decay, E.4 Fission, E.5 Fusion and stars

DP brings back "heat" as a process word: "energy as heat and as work", "heat engine".

## 6. Terminology clashes and suggested bridge sentences

| Term/idea | School wording (source) | Careful wording | Suggested bridge sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | "weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass" (IGCSE 1.3) | Weight is the pull of gravity on you, measured in newtons. | "Your school book says weight is a gravitational force. That's exactly our 'Earth's pull', written in newtons." |
| g | Field strength "force per unit mass" in N/kg; also "acceleration of free fall… approximately 9.8 m/s²" (IGCSE 1.2, 1.3) | The same 9.8 can be read two ways. | "You'll see g = 9.8 N/kg and g = 9.8 m/s². They're the same number seen from two sides: the pull on each kilogram, and how fast falling things speed up." |
| "Weight in kg" | "weights (and masses) may be compared using a balance" (IGCSE 1.3); everyday speech says "I weigh 40 kg". | Kilograms measure mass. A scale measures force and is labelled in kg. | "When someone says they weigh 40 kg, they mean their mass. In a physics answer, write mass in kg and weight in N." |
| Resultant force | "unless acted on by a resultant force" (IGCSE 1.5.1); "balanced and unbalanced forces" (0893 8Pf.03) | Net force | "Resultant force, net force and unbalanced force all mean the same thing: what's left over after the pushes cancel out." |
| Centrifugal force | Not used. IGCSE: "a force perpendicular to the motion" (1.5.1.12). | There is only an inward pull. The 'outward' feeling is your body trying to go straight. | "People say 'centrifugal force' throws you outward, but your syllabus never uses it. In exams, name the inward force: tension, friction or gravity." |
| Moment | "turning forces (moment = force x distance)" (0893 8Pf.04); "force × perpendicular distance from the pivot" (IGCSE 1.5.2) | Perpendicular distance | "Your Stage 8 sheet may say force × distance. In IGCSE you must add 'perpendicular', which is the distance measured at right angles to the force." |
| Energy stores and transfers | Stores listed, ending "internal (thermal)"; transfers "by forces… electrical currents… heating… waves" (IGCSE 1.7.1) | The same idea | "School calls the place where energy sits a store and how it moves a transfer. Our 'energy bank accounts' map straight onto that." |
| "Energy is lost" | "energy tends to dissipate… becomes less useful" (0893 7Pf.02); IGCSE uses "useful energy output" and never "lost". | It spreads out as thermal energy and becomes less useful. None is destroyed. | "If a teacher says energy is 'lost', they mean it spread out into the surroundings. It still exists, it's just not useful any more." |
| "Heat" as a noun | "difference between heat and temperature"; "heat dissipation" (0893 9Pf.02, 9Pf.04); IGCSE says "thermal energy", "heating", "internal energy" but keeps "specific heat capacity". | Heating is a process. What an object holds is internal (thermal) energy. | "Your Stage 9 book uses 'heat'. In IGCSE you'll write 'thermal energy'. The name 'specific heat capacity' is old and stayed put." |
| Particle model and absolute zero | "particles have least kinetic energy" at −273 °C (IGCSE 2.1.2) | Least, not zero | "Note the word 'least': even at absolute zero, particles aren't perfectly still." |
| Pressure | p = F/A; ∆p = ρg∆h (IGCSE 1.8); "force, exerted by a substance, on an area" (0893 8Pf.05) | The same idea | "The Δ in ∆p = ρg∆h means 'change in'. Go deeper and the pressure goes up." |
| Suction | Not used. Gas pressure is "particles colliding with surfaces" (IGCSE 2.1.2). | The outside air pushes in. | "Nothing 'sucks'. In an exam, explain that the air pressure outside is bigger and pushes." |
| "Electricity flows" | "describe electricity as a flow of electrons" (0893 7Pe.01); "current is related to the flow of charge" (IGCSE 4.2.2) | Charge flows. The energy is carried by the circuit's fields. | "Saying 'electricity flows' is fine in Stage 7. In IGCSE, say current, which is charge flowing per second." |
| Conventional current | "conventional current is from positive to negative… free electrons… from negative to positive" (IGCSE 4.2.2) | Both arrows are correct conventions. | "Circuit arrows go + to −, because that was decided before anyone knew about electrons. Electrons actually go the other way. Both are 'right'." |
| Light rays | "Draw and use ray diagrams"; "normal" (IGCSE 3.2) | A ray is a drawn line showing which way light travels. | "A ray isn't a thing that exists. It's a pencil line showing the direction light goes, and the normal is a line we draw at right angles to the surface." |
| Seasons | Orbit "approximately 365 days… explain the periodic nature of the seasons"; the axis "is tilted" (IGCSE 6.1.1) | The tilt causes seasons, not the distance from the Sun. | "The syllabus says the yearly orbit explains why seasons repeat. The reason seasons happen at all is the tilt." |
| Moon phases and "dark side" | "approximately one month… cycle of phases" (IGCSE 6.1.1.3); "dark side" is not used. | Far side. Half the Moon is always lit. | "'Dark side of the Moon' really means the far side, and it gets sunlight too." |
| Orbits | "the force that keeps an object in orbit… is the gravitational attraction of the Sun" (IGCSE 6.1.2); 0893 7ESs.02 says the same. | Falling around | "When school says gravity 'keeps' planets in orbit, think of a planet that keeps falling towards the Sun and keeps missing it." |
| Redshift and the Big Bang | "receding stars and galaxies"; "supports the Big Bang Theory"; "present at a single point" (IGCSE 6.2.3) | Space itself stretches. There was no explosion into empty space. | "Your syllabus says galaxies are 'receding'. A careful way to picture it is space stretching between them. 'Theory' here means a well-tested explanation, not a guess." |
