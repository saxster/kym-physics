## Further adventures

When you have finished this book, the adventure does not stop. Here are some places to go next, all free.

**MIT OpenCourseWare.** The Massachusetts Institute of Technology puts many of its courses online for anyone. Much of this book was built on them.

- [Physics Demonstration Videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-8-003-physics-demonstration-videos-spring-2012/): short films of the experiments MIT shows its students, from spinning wheels to glowing tubes (if an older video player does not load on the course page, search the demonstration title on the MIT Physics Technical Services / OpenCourseWare YouTube channels). For watching: the real versions need a lab.
- When you are older: [Classical Mechanics (8.01SC)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/), [Electricity and Magnetism (8.02)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/), [Vibrations and Waves (8.03SC)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/) and [Introduction to Astronomy (8.282J)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-282j-introduction-to-astronomy-spring-2006/).

**OpenStax.** Free textbooks written by teachers: [Physics](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/1-introduction) for school, and [Astronomy 2e](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/1-introduction) for the sky.

**PhET simulations.** The University of Colorado's free [physics simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/filter?subjects=physics): build circuits, launch projectiles, float blocks, send waves down a string.

**phyphox.** A free phone app from RWTH Aachen University that turns your phone's sensors into a laboratory: acceleration, sound, magnetic field, light. Several *Try it at home* boxes use it.

**The Feynman Lectures on Physics.** The lectures Richard Feynman gave to first-year students at Caltech in the 1960s are [free to read online](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/). They are hard, and wonderful. Try Chapter 1 of Volume I when you are fourteen or fifteen.

**In Mumbai.** The [Nehru Planetarium](https://www.nehrucentremumbai.in/planetarium-about-us/) in Worli has been showing people the sky since 1977. On National Science Day, 28 February, the anniversary of the Raman effect, many labs and universities in India open their doors.

**In the kitchen.** Every *Try it at home* box is a real experiment. Keep a notebook: what you predicted, what happened, what surprised you. That notebook is exactly what Galileo, Faraday and Raman kept.
