## Five ideas that run through everything

Physics can look like a long list of laws and formulas. It is not. Nearly everything in this book comes from four big ideas, used again and again in different places, and one habit that sits underneath them. When something puzzles you, ask which of them is at work.

:::raw
<ol class="ideas">
<li><b>Forces change motion; they don't keep it going.</b>A moving thing keeps moving, at the same speed in the same direction, unless something pushes or pulls on it. Things slow down because of friction and air, not because they run out of anything. And every force is one half of a pair: when you push the wall, the wall pushes you back exactly as hard.</li>
<li><b>Energy is never used up.</b>Energy moves from one store to another, a stretched spring, a moving ball, a warm cup, and the total never changes. What does change is how spread out it is. Once it has spread into slightly warmer air and floor, you cannot easily gather it back to do anything useful. That is what people really mean when they say energy is "used".</li>
<li><b>Waves and fields carry energy without carrying stuff.</b>A sound reaches your ear, but the air near the tabla stays near the tabla. Light crosses empty space from the Sun. A magnet pulls a pin across a gap with nothing in between. In each case energy travels, and the stuff it passes through does not.</li>
<li><b>One set of rules for Earth and sky.</b>The pull that drops a mango from a tree is the same pull that holds the Moon in its path, raises the tides at Haji Ali and steered Chandrayaan-3 to the Moon. The light from a torch obeys the same rules as the light from a star. Nature does not keep one rulebook for the ground and another for the heavens.</li>
</ol>
:::

And underneath all four is a habit: **estimate before you measure, and keep what you observe separate from the model you use to explain it.** The observation is what any careful person would see or measure. The model is our best explanation so far, and a better experiment can always improve it.
