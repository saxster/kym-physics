:::scene London, 1862

In a paper published in parts across 1861 and 1862, James Clerk Maxwell did a piece of arithmetic that still startles people. He had been building a theory of how electric and magnetic fields behave, borrowing Faraday's lines of force and giving them equations. The theory predicted that a disturbance in the fields should travel as a wave, and it gave the wave's speed in terms of two numbers from the electrical laboratory, measured by Weber and Kohlrausch with coils and charged jars. Maxwell put the numbers in. Out came 310,740 kilometres per second.

Thirteen years earlier Hippolyte Fizeau had measured the speed of light across Paris with a spinning cogwheel and got 314,858 kilometres per second. The two figures differed by about one per cent. Nothing about light had gone into Maxwell's equations; only electricity and magnetism. He wrote: "we can scarcely avoid the inference that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena." In 1865 he set the whole theory out again, cleaned up, in *A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field*. Light, the thing people had been splitting with prisms since Newton, was an electromagnetic wave.

One word in his sentence did not survive. Maxwell pictured the waves rippling through a "medium" filling space. Later physics dropped the medium: the waves need nothing to travel through, and cross empty space between the Sun and us at the same speed. Everything else in the inference stood.

#### Step 1: four rules in words

Maxwell's equations gather up what Ørsted, Ampère, Coulomb, Gauss and Faraday had found. In words, without the mathematics, they say four things. Electric field lines start and end on charges. Magnetic field lines have no ends; they always close into loops. A changing magnetic field makes an electric field, which is Faraday's induction. And a magnetic field is made by moving charges, or, the piece Maxwell added himself, by a *changing electric field*.

That last clause is the hinge. A changing electric field makes a magnetic field; a changing magnetic field makes an electric field. Each change creates the other, and the pair can run away together across space with nothing left behind to hold them. That is an electromagnetic wave: electric and magnetic fields swinging at right angles to each other and to the direction they travel, each one regenerating the other as they go.

#### Step 2: how to make one

Charges that sit still make no waves, and neither does a steady current in a wire. To launch a wave you must *shake* a charge: accelerate it, back and forth. Every jerk sends a ripple of changing field outwards at the speed

:::eq
**c = 1 ⁄ √(μ₀ ε₀) ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s**
:::

where μ₀ and ε₀ are the two laboratory constants Maxwell used. Put in the modern values and you get 299,860 kilometres per second. The speed of light is not a fact about light. It is a fact about electricity and magnetism.

#### Step 3: Hertz makes them

:::scene Karlsruhe, 1887

Maxwell died in 1879 without seeing his waves. In Karlsruhe, from 1887, Heinrich Hertz made them. He let a spark jump across a gap in a tuned circuit, so that charge surged back and forth very fast, and across the room a second loop of wire with its own tiny gap began to spark in sympathy: the wave had crossed the room and shaken the charges in the receiver. He went on to show that the waves travel at the speed of light, and that they reflect, refract and interfere exactly as light does, only with wavelengths enormously longer than light's.

The story goes that a student asked Hertz what use his discovery was, and that he replied it was of no use whatsoever. It is a good story, and it may even be true, but no record of the exchange exists from Hertz's lifetime; the earliest source anyone has traced is an article from 2013. Within fifteen years of his experiments, as the story *Invisible waves in Calcutta* tells, people were ringing bells through walls and sending signals across the Atlantic with Hertz's waves. We call them radio waves.

#### Step 4: the ladder of light

Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are all the same thing: electromagnetic waves, differing only in how fast the fields swing, and so in how long each wave is. Laid out in order they make the electromagnetic spectrum, a ladder whose rungs run from waves thousands of kilometres long to waves smaller than an atom's nucleus.

:::fig s_em_ladder() | The ladder of light, with wavelengths in powers of ten. Marked on it: the 6,000 km "wave" of Mumbai's 50 Hz mains, the metre-long waves the GMRT dishes near Pune collect, J. C. Bose's 5 mm waves, a TV remote's 940 nm infrared, and the thin band of visible light from 400 to 750 nm. Every rung travels at the same speed.

Visible light runs from about 400 nanometres (violet) to 750 nanometres (red); as a frequency, that is 400 to 750 million million swings per second. A typical TV remote talks to the television with infrared at about 940 nanometres, just too long for your eye, though not for a phone camera. J. C. Bose's waves in Calcutta were 5 millimetres long, 60 thousand million swings per second. The radio telescope at Khodad, near Pune, collects waves about a metre long. And the alternating current in your flat's wiring, swinging 50 times a second, corresponds to a wavelength of about 6,000 kilometres. Same wave, same speed, one ladder.

:::careful
> "Radio waves are made by an electric current."

Not a steady one. A steady current makes a steady magnetic field and no wave at all. You have to shake the charge: accelerate it back and forth, as the spark did in Hertz's gap and as the electrons do in a transmitter's antenna. Waves come from charges that are *accelerating*.
:::

:::careful
> "Light needs something to travel through, like sound needs air."

Maxwell thought so too, which is why his sentence says "medium". It turned out that the fields carry themselves: a changing electric field makes the magnetic one and vice versa, and empty space is enough. Sunlight crosses 150 million kilometres of nothing on the way to Marine Drive.
:::
