:::scene Bombay, 19 December 1945

In the drawing rooms of *Kenilworth*, a rented bungalow on Peddar Road in Bombay where **Homi Jehangir Bhabha** had been born thirty-six years earlier, the **Tata Institute of Fundamental Research** (**TIFR**) opened its doors. Glassblowers, cosmic-ray experimentalists, and young mathematicians shared the veranda and the converted garages. Within twenty years that household laboratory would grow into a seaside campus at Colaba and launch India's programmes in particle physics, radio astronomy, and computing.

#### A Letter to His Father

Born in Bombay on 30 October 1909, Bhabha grew up painting in oils, listening to classical music, and reading physics books in his grandfather's library. In 1927 he sailed to Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, because his father and his uncle **Dorabji Tata** wanted him to take an engineering degree and return to manage the Tata steel mills at Jamshedpur.

After a year in Cambridge, Bhabha wrote home to his father on 8 August 1928: *"I seriously say to you that business or a job as an engineer is not the thing for me. It is totally foreign to my nature and radically opposed to my temperament and opinions. Physics is my line. I know I shall do great things here... I am burning with a desire to do physics."* His father made a counter-offer: if Homi earned a First Class in the Mechanical Sciences Tripos, the family would support two further years at Cambridge for theoretical physics. Bhabha took his First in June 1930 and joined the Cavendish Laboratory.

#### Cosmic Showers and Bhabha Scattering

Working in Cambridge, Zurich, Rome, and Copenhagen during the 1930s, Bhabha calculated in 1935 the quantum cross-section for an electron scattering off its antimatter twin, the positron (e⁻ + e⁺ → e⁻ + e⁺). Particle collider teams still use **Bhabha scattering** as the standard ruler to calibrate their beam luminosity.

With **Walter Heitler** in 1937, Bhabha explained how a single primary cosmic ray striking the upper atmosphere cascades into a shower of electrons, positrons, and gamma rays. In 1938 he showed in *Nature* that unstable cosmic-ray muons survive the trip to sea level only because **Albert Einstein**'s relativistic time dilation slows their internal clocks near the speed of light.

#### Building the School Before the Machines

Home on holiday in 1939 when the Second World War broke out, Bhabha stayed in India to work alongside **C. V. Raman** at the **Indian Institute of Science** (**IISc**) in Bangalore, flying high-altitude cosmic-ray balloon payloads near the geomagnetic equator.

On 12 March 1944 Bhabha wrote to the **Sir Dorabji Tata Trust** proposing an institute that would train India's own physicists and mathematicians before big machines were built. Backed by **J. R. D. Tata**, TIFR started at Bangalore in June 1945, was inaugurated at *Kenilworth* in Bombay on 19 December 1945, and later moved to Colaba. Bhabha died on 24 January 1966 when Air India Flight 101 crashed on Mont Blanc.
