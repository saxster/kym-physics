:::scene Pune, 1948

When thirty-year-old **Anna Modayil Mani** walked into the Instruments Division of the **India Meteorological Department** (**IMD**) in Pune in 1948, a year after Independence, the country's weather stations still ordered their barometers, anemometers, rain gauges, and sunshine recorders from Britain. Even simple thermometers were imported. Working with a workshop of Indian machinists and glassblowers, Mani set out to design, calibrate, and standardise the drawings for nearly a hundred meteorological instruments so that India could measure its own sky.

#### Britannica Instead of Diamonds

Born on 23 August 1918 in Peermade in the Western Ghats of Travancore (now Kerala), the seventh of eight children of a civil engineer, Anna famously asked her parents for the *Encyclopædia Britannica* instead of the customary pair of diamond earrings on her eighth birthday.

After graduating with an honours degree in physics and chemistry from Presidency College in Madras in 1939, she won a scholarship in 1940 to work in **C. V. Raman**'s laboratory at the **Indian Institute of Science** (**IISc**) in Bangalore. For five years she recorded the fluorescence, absorption, and Raman spectra of hundreds of diamonds and rubies over sixteen-hour photographic exposures, publishing five single-authored papers. Although she submitted a complete doctoral dissertation, Madras University denied her a PhD on the technicality that she held an honours BSc rather than an MSc.

#### Precision by Design

In 1945 Mani sailed to London on a government scholarship, spending three years at Imperial College and the British Meteorological Office learning how weather sensors are built and calibrated in wind tunnels.

Joining the IMD at Pune in 1948 under **S. P. Venkiteshwaran**, Mani knew that atmospheric physics is only as honest as its instruments: if two anemometers on two hills have different bearing friction, the wind map they produce is fiction. Rising to Deputy Director General, she oversaw Indian Standards specifications for nearly a hundred weather instruments, built a national calibration wind tunnel, and established networks mapping solar radiation and wind energy across India long before renewable power became an industry.

#### Rockets and Ozone

In 1962 **Vikram Sarabhai** called on Mani to build the wind towers and weather instrumentation at **Thumba** for India's first rocket launch in November 1963. Encouraged by **K. R. Ramanathan**, she also developed the balloon-borne **Indian ozonesonde**, giving India a twenty-year vertical record of tropical atmospheric ozone before the Antarctic ozone hole made world headlines.

Standardising straight vertical walls lets a rain gauge in Kerala be compared directly with one in Assam.
