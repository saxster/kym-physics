:::scene Thumba, Kerala, 21 November 1963

At dusk on 21 November 1963, in a coconut grove beside the Arabian Sea at **Thumba** near Trivandrum, a two-stage Nike-Apache sounding rocket roared off its pad and climbed 200 kilometres into the twilight sky, releasing a glowing orange trail of sodium vapour that drifted with the upper-atmosphere winds. The launch control room was a local cattle shed, the bishop's house served as the drawing office, and the village church of **St Mary Magdalene** was the rocket assembly workshop.

#### Cosmic Rays in Ahmedabad

Born in Ahmedabad on 12 August 1919 into a family of textile philanthropists who supported **Mahatma Gandhi** and ran a Montessori school in their garden, **Vikram Ambalal Sarabhai** studied natural sciences at St John's College, Cambridge. Returning to India when the Second World War broke out in 1939, he researched cosmic-ray time variations under **C. V. Raman** at the **Indian Institute of Science** in Bangalore alongside **Homi Bhabha**, setting up Geiger counters in Bangalore and at 4,000 m in Kashmir. After completing his Cambridge doctorate, the twenty-eight-year-old Sarabhai founded the **Physical Research Laboratory** (**PRL**) in Ahmedabad in November 1947.

#### Why Thumba?

About 100 kilometres above the ground flows a narrow ionospheric ribbon of electric current called the **equatorial electrojet**, concentrated right along the Earth's **geomagnetic equator**. That magnetic line clips the southern tip of India directly through the fishing village of **Thumba**.

In 1962 Sarabhai and his young engineers, including **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam**, met the Right Reverend **Peter Bernard Pereira**, Bishop of Trivandrum. Invited to Sunday Mass, Sarabhai explained the scientific station to the fishing congregation, and Bishop Pereira told the villagers that science and faith both sought truth to uplift human life. Within a hundred days the community relocated to a new village and handed their church over to become the **Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station** (**TERLS**).

:::careful
**Bicycles and bullock carts.** Famous photographs show a rocket nose cone wheeled past coconut palms on a bicycle and a payload carried on a wooden bullock cart. Both really happened at Thumba: nose cones were pedalled from the church to the pad, and wooden carts were used so that iron chassis parts would not disturb sensitive magnetometers inside the payload. As **ISRO**'s own photo archive notes, however, the surviving photographs were re-staged for a press photographer a few months after the first launch.
:::

#### Satellites for the Villages

Leading India's space programme (which became **ISRO** in 1969) and chairing the Atomic Energy Commission after Bhabha's death in 1966, Sarabhai explained in Vienna in 1968 why a developing nation belonged in space: not for prestige or Moon races, but to forecast the monsoon, survey crops and groundwater, and broadcast classroom lessons to half a million villages by satellite. He died in his sleep near Trivandrum on 30 December 1971, aged fifty-two, just after reviewing the **SLV-3** satellite launcher design at Thumba.
