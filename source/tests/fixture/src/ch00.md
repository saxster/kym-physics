## 0.1 Blocks that hold text

:::scene Delft, 1586

A ==force== is a push or a pull. Forces are drawn as arrows: the arrow's length shows how big the force is.

:::note Force
A push or a pull on an object, measured in newtons (N).
:::

:::aside
An aside sits in the margin beside the paragraph above it.
:::

:::careful
> "A moving ball needs a force to keep it moving."

Nothing pushes a hockey puck across smooth ice, yet it keeps going. A force changes motion; it is not needed to keep it.
:::

:::evidence Two balls, one thud | 1586
Simon Stevin and Jan Cornets de Groot dropped two lead balls, one ten times heavier than the other, about 30 feet onto a board. They heard a single thud.
:::

:::predict
You drop a cricket ball and a ₹1 coin from the same height at the same moment. Which lands first?
- The cricket ball
- The coin
- They land together, or very nearly
???
They land together, or very nearly. Air slows the coin a little more than the ball, but from a table's height you cannot hear the difference.
:::

:::eq
weight = mass × 9.8 N/kg
:::

:::work
A 2 kg bag of rice:
weight = 2 kg × 9.8 N/kg
weight = 19.6 N, about 20 N
:::

:::check
What is the weight of a 0.5 kg mango on Earth?
???
0.5 kg × 9.8 N/kg = 4.9 N, about 5 N.
:::

Why does a stiffer spring bounce faster?

## 0.3 Coming back to an idea

:::recall 0.1
You dropped a cricket ball and a coin together. What decides which lands first?
???
Only air resistance. Without air, everything falls with the same acceleration, about 9.8 m/s².
